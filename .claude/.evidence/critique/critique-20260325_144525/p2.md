Phase 1 found multiple HIGH items where the "evidence" is the pre-mortem's own self-assertion (e.g., "Implementation completed and verified" in header vs "NOT TESTED" in Step 3.8). But no specialist identified this as a meta-level problem: the document violates its own methodology requirements. None of the 4 specialists applied the pre-mortem's own evidence standards to the pre-mortem. (adversarial-*:all — none flagged methodology self-violation)

2. **Cascade annotation syntax inconsistency** — The pre-mortem references "causes:" and "caused-by:" annotations in risk descriptions but these are not consistently applied across the document. adversarial-compliance flagged cascade incompleteness (COMP-001) but did not note that the cascade annotation syntax itself is used inconsistently — some risks show dependencies, others don't, with no explanation for why certain dependencies are annotated and others aren't. (p1_findings.md:1.6 — missed the syntax vs semantics distinction)

3. **Kill criteria as both safeguards and theater** — The KC's are defined to trigger abandonment, but Step 5 (Prevention Actions) proposes adding monitoring for them. If KC's require monitoring to be actionable, and monitoring isn't implemented, then the KC's are theater — they look like safeguards but are not operational. (p1_findings.md combined 3.2 + 3.5 — specialists saw both but didn't connect them)

This is a direct factual contradiction, not a severity disagreement. adversarial-critic and adversarial-quality both flagged aspects of this (META-002, G1) but treated it as a reasoning quality issue rather than a factual accuracy problem. The document claims something that is demonstrably false per its own evidence. (adversarial-critic:META-002, adversarial-quality:G1)

## Precision Failures

1. **"98 orphans as vanity metric" is imprecise** — adversarial-logic L4 says the document "simultaneously treats 98 orphans as vanity metric and as partial confirmation." But the actual problem is more specific: the SUCCESS THEATER section identifies it as vanity, while the VERIFICATION section uses it as partial confirmation. This isn't one document taking two positions simultaneously — it's two different sections at different stages of analysis reaching incompatible conclusions. The precision failure is that the consolidated finding misidentifies WHERE the contradiction occurs. (adversarial-logic:L4 vs actual document structure)

2. **L1 cascade flaw stated too strongly** — adversarial-logic claims T1→T2 is "two distinct failure modes, not a cascade." But the cascade analysis in Step 2.5 shows T1 can ENABLE T2 (if timeout fires mid-write, you get corruption). The finding frames them as "independent" but the cascade trace is about sequential enablement, not independent parallel paths. The cascade IS flawed (portalocker behavior mischaracterized) but the finding overcorrects by claiming complete independence. (adversarial-logic:L1 — precision: the cascade logic is wrong but the reasoning for why is imprecise)

3. **"Same-session samples" critique is partially wrong** — adversarial-critic META-003 says "same-session, same-developer samples inflate confidence." This is true for cross-session base rate estimation. But adversarial-logic's L3 critique is specifically about "1-2% failure rate lacks citation" — which is a different (and worse) problem: there's no citation at all, not just that the citation is from contaminated samples. The samples being same-session is a contamination issue; the 1-2% number having no source is a citation issue. Phase 1 conflates these two distinct problems. (adversarial-critic:META-003 vs adversarial-logic:L3)

4. **adversarial-compliance COMP-003 "scoring matrix inconsistent with cascade prioritization"** — This finding is vague. T2 is Risk 3 (LOW) in the scoring matrix but appears in cascade analysis. The finding doesn't explain whether: (a) T2 shouldn't be in the cascade at all, (b) the risk score is wrong, or (c) the cascade prioritization is wrong. "Inconsistent" could mean any of these. No specificity. (adversarial-compliance:COMP-003)

## Improvements to the Phase 1 Findings

1. **Add severity reconciliation step** — Phase 1 should explicitly resolve severity disagreements between specialists before consolidating. When one specialist says HIGH and another says MEDIUM on the same issue, document why one was accepted over the other or whether they describe different aspects of the same problem.

2. **Distinguish "not flagged" from "flagged at different severity"** — The success theater finding (adversarial-quality:6) appears to have been dropped entirely from consolidated findings. Document explicitly why each specialist's unique findings were included or excluded.

3. **Add a "conflicting findings" resolution section** — For issues like T2 (scored LOW but cascade-critical), Phase 1 should explicitly resolve rather than present both as true without reconciliation. "T2 is Risk 3 BUT appears on critical path — resolution: risk score revised to reflect cascade role" or similar.

4. **Calibrate against pre-mortem skill's own severity rubric** — The pre-mortem skill defines CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW. Phase 1 should map its severity assignments back to the skill's rubric and verify alignment. Currently there's no cross-check between "what Phase 1 called HIGH" and "what the pre-mortem skill would call HIGH for this type of finding."

5. **Flag items that appear in P1 blocking items but not as HIGH in body** — Items 4 (T6 test corpus) and 5 (KC criteria) appear in P1 blocking items but are rated MEDIUM in the body. This suggests the blocking items list was curated separately from severity ratings. Reconcile explicitly.

6. **Add "competing interpretations" for ambiguous findings** — Where specialists disagree (e.g., L1: is T1→T2 cascade "completely independent" vs "sequentially enabled"), document both interpretations and which one the consolidated finding accepts, with rationale.
