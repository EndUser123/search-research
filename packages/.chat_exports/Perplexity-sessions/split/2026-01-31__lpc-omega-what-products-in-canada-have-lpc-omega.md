## Assistant

No—phosphatidylserine (PS) is **not** the same thing as LPC‑DHA / lyso‑DHA, and it does not use the same MFSD2A “express courier” mechanism.

### How they differ chemically

- **LPC‑DHA / lyso‑DHA**: DHA is attached to a **lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC)** backbone, which is a *lyso*‑phospholipid with a choline head group and only one fatty acid chain. This specific LPC‑DHA form is what MFSD2A transports across the blood–brain barrier, making it highly efficient for brain DHA enrichment.[1][2][3]
- **Phosphatidylserine (PS)**: A different phospholipid with a **serine** head group and *two* fatty acid chains; it’s a structural component of neuronal membranes and myelin, and can be enriched with DHA (DHA‑PS), but its uptake route and kinetics are different and not MFSD2A‑dependent in the same way.[4][5]

So PS is a distinct molecule; you can have **DHA‑enriched PS**, but that is not LPC‑DHA.

### Functional implications

- LPC‑DHA studies show that giving DHA as **LPC** raises brain DHA more efficiently than giving it as triglyceride or even standard phospholipids, and shifts DHA away from fat tissue toward the brain.[2][3][6][1]
- PS supplements (usually soy‑derived PS) have their **own** evidence base: several trials show improved memory and cognitive function in aging and mild cognitive impairment at 300–800 mg/day.[7][8][9][4]
- Some animal work suggests **DHA‑PS or DHA‑PC** can target brain DHA better than triglyceride‑DHA, but still not as efficiently as LPC‑DHA in head‑to‑head comparisons.[10][5][1]

In practice: PS is a separate neuroactive phospholipid that may help cognition, but if you’re specifically trying to replicate the **MFSD2A / lyso‑DHA** mechanism Norwitz focuses on, generic phosphatidylserine supplements are not interchangeable with LPC‑DHA/Lysoveta‑type products.
