## Human

there's a new type of window ac unit called a u-shape.  are they are good?  are they better than normal window ac units?  quieter?

## Assistant

Yes, the new U‑shaped window AC units (like Midea U, GE ClearView, Hisense, MRCOOL, etc.) are generally **better than traditional window units for noise, efficiency, and comfort**, with some trade‑offs in price and installation complexity.[1][2][3]
## What “U‑shaped” window ACs are
U‑shaped units hang over the window sill so the compressor and condenser sit outside, while the indoor section sits inside, with the window glass closing down into the “U” between them. This design means the solid window glass (plus foam) is between you and most of the noisy, hot hardware instead of thin plastic side panels.[1][4]
Most popular models use variable‑speed inverter compressors and smart controls, which further improves efficiency and reduces the “on/off” cycling noise you get with older window units.[2][3][5]
## Are they good at cooling?
Independent tests (Wirecutter, TechGearLab, Consumer Reports, etc.) plus user reviews show U‑shaped units cool rooms as well as or better than conventional window ACs of the same BTU rating.[6][1][3]

- Wirecutter calls the Midea U “one of the quietest ACs we’ve ever tested” and still up to the job of cooling typical living spaces.[6]
- TechGearLab notes the Midea U‑shaped 12,000 BTU unit “excels at cooling larger spaces while doing it quietly and efficiently,” scoring better than most comparable units they tested.[3]
- Consumer Reports says U‑shaped models “generally perform as well as or better than conventional units at the basic job of cooling a room quickly and quietly.”[1]

Consensus: cooling performance is solid; you’re not trading cooling for quiet. I’d treat cooling capability as roughly equivalent to any good modern inverter window unit of similar BTU, with a slight edge in real‑world comfort because of better sealing and less heat leakage through flimsy plastic panels.[4][1]
## Are they better than normal window units?
### Key advantages
- **Much quieter:**  
  - Midea’s U units are rated as low as ~31–32 dBA at quiet settings, and marketing claims up to “12× quieter than traditional units,” which independent reviewers broadly support (though “12×” is marketing, not a strict acoustic ratio).[7][2][3]
  - TechGearLab found the U‑shaped Midea outscored most other window units in noise testing, highlighting the inverter plus U‑shape design as the reason.[3]

- **More efficient (lower power usage):**  
  - MRCOOL and Midea both claim ~35–45% better energy efficiency versus standard non‑inverter window units; MRCOOL’s U‑shaped model even received “Energy Star Most Efficient” certification in 2020.[4][2]
  - Inverter compressors ramp up and down instead of harsh on/off cycles, which reduces power spikes and total energy consumption.[2][5]

- **Better sealing and insulation:**  
  - The window glass + thicker foam are between the room and outside, so cooled air leaks less and heat ingress is lower than with typical thin plastic side panels.[1][4]
  - This also helps with outside noise, particularly from the compressor and street sounds.[1][4]

- **You can still open the window:**  
  - With most U‑shaped models you can slide the window up a bit to get some fresh air without removing the unit, which is impossible with a normal box unit that occupies the whole opening.[2][1]
  - Consumer Reports highlights this as a major lifestyle advantage if you occasionally want breeze plus AC.[1]
### Main downsides
- **Higher purchase price:**  
  - U‑shaped inverter units are typically noticeably more expensive than basic non‑inverter window ACs of similar BTU; Home Depot and other retailers frame them as a “step up” in price and performance.[2]
  - Some users on Reddit and reviews emphasize that they’re “on the pricier side” but worth it for quiet and comfort.[8][5]

- **Installation is different and a bit fussier:**  
  - They come with a bracket that mounts into the window frame/sill; setup is still DIY‑friendly, but more steps than dropping a standard box unit into a window and closing it over foam strips.[4][2]
  - Certain window types (very narrow sills, casement/crank windows) might be incompatible or require adaptation; Consumer Reports notes they’re not for every window type.[1]

- **Heavier and more specialized form factor:**  
  - The U‑shape and bracket make them quite secure (less chance of falling out), but weight distribution can be awkward during install compared to a single rectangular box.[4]
  - If the unit fails years later, you’re somewhat locked into that U‑shaped ecosystem rather than swapping to any generic rectangle without rethinking mounting.

Consensus: if you value quiet, efficiency, and comfort and you’re okay spending more and doing a slightly more involved install, U‑shaped units are objectively better than traditional non‑inverter window units. Against modern premium inverter box units, they’re mainly a win on noise and “keep the window usable.”[3][1][2]
## How much quieter are they in practice?
Noise is their biggest selling point, and independent measurements back it up.[6][4][2][3]

- Midea U and similar models advertise indoor noise levels down around the low‑30s dBA at minimal fan settings and mid‑40s dBA at typical use, versus many conventional window ACs running in the 50–60+ dBA range indoors.[9][7][2][3]
- A popular HVAC YouTube reviewer measured the MRCOOL U‑shape at ~47 dB from a typical listening distance and calls it “10 times quieter” than standard units, aligning with the marketing positioning.[9]
- TechGearLab’s testing found the U‑shaped Midea among the quietest units they’ve evaluated, outperforming most other window ACs in their noise metric.[3]
- Reddit and user reviews repeatedly describe the U‑shaped units as “super quiet,” “basically inaudible over background noise at lower levels,” making them especially favored for bedrooms.[8][5][10]

Why they’re quieter:

- The compressor and condenser are outside the window, with glass and thicker foam between them and the room, which blocks a lot of mechanical noise.[1][4]
- Inverter compressors ramp smoothly instead of loud start/stop cycles, which eliminates the big “clunk and buzz” moments that dominate perceived noise in older units.[2][5]
- The indoor section only has the fan and evaporator; with smart controls, fan speeds stay lower and more constant once the setpoint is reached.[2][3]

FACT: Independent lab tests and reviews consistently show U‑shaped inverter units quieter than typical window ACs of similar size.[6][1][3][2]
CONSENSUS: For bedroom or home office use where noise matters, they are currently among the best window AC choices.  
SPECULATION (high confidence): If you are sensitive to noise or work/sleep in the same room, you will likely perceive a significant improvement over a typical box window unit of similar BTU.
