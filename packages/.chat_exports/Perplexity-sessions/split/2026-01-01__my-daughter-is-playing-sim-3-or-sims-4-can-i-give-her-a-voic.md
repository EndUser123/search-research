## Assistant

Based on my research, here's what's available and what actually works for your daughter's situation:

## The Short Answer

**No commercial voice-activated AI game controller currently exists** that combines screen awareness with Sims game understanding out of the box. However, there are several practical alternatives that work today.

## The Challenge with The Sims

The Sims games (both 3 and 4) present a fundamental problem for voice control. Sims 4 on PC has **no native controller support**, and the gameplay is heavily menu-driven with complex UI interactions—clicking specific buttons in build mode, buy mode, inventory systems, and conversation wheels. This makes direct voice control extremely difficult compared to action games.[1]

## What Actually Works Today

### Option 1: **VoiceBot** (Recommended for Budget-Conscious Parents)
**Free forever (after 30-day trial)**[2]

This software lets your daughter speak voice commands that get translated into keyboard shortcuts, mouse clicks, and macros. You'd map common Sims actions to voice commands—for example, saying "build wall" executes a hotkey combination that activates the build menu. It's not AI-aware of what's on screen, but it's straightforward and has been used successfully for accessible gaming.

**Setup effort:** Moderate (requires custom mapping of Sims commands to voice phrases)

### Option 2: **Google Gemini 2.0 Screen Share** (Best Free Solution)
**Completely free**[3]

This recent capability lets your daughter play normally with mouse/keyboard while streaming her game screen to an AI assistant in the browser. She can pause and ask questions like "Why is my Sim unhappy?" or "What should I build next?" and the AI watches her gameplay in real-time to provide context-aware advice.

**Advantages:**
- Zero setup required
- AI actually understands what's happening in her game
- No latency issues (she's still controlling the game normally)
- Free through Google AI Studio
- Acts like having a knowledgeable gaming buddy

**Limitations:**
- The AI can't directly control the game; it's advisory only
- Useful for strategy/guidance, not hands-free control

### Option 3: **Windows 11 Native Voice Access** (Built Into Her PC)
**Free—already on Windows 11**[4]

Enable it at Settings > Accessibility > Speech. This lets her control the entire PC with voice, including mouse movement and clicks. It works with any game but has a learning curve—she'd need to learn to say "move mouse" to navigate and "click" to interact.

**Advantages:**
- No additional software needed
- Works with Sims 3 or 4
- Genuine voice control of the PC

**Limitations:**
- Not game-optimized; slower than mouse/keyboard
- Requires training and adjustment

### Option 4: **Custom AI Voice + Vision System** (Advanced/DIY)
Developers have recently built systems that combine voice input with screen capture and AI models (Claude, GPT-4o, or Gemini) to understand gameplay and execute commands. However, these require coding knowledge to set up and wouldn't work well for Sims' complex UI anyway.[5][6]

## My Recommendation for Your Situation

**Start with Gemini 2.0 Screen Share** (no setup) and **consider VoiceBot** if she wants more hands-free control. The combination gives her both AI guidance while playing plus voice command shortcuts for repetitive actions.

The honest truth: Sims games aren't designed for voice-only control due to their UI complexity. Even AI-powered controllers struggle with them. But with Gemini's screen sharing, she gets the next-best thing—an AI that watches and understands what she's doing, which is surprisingly helpful for gaming decisions.
