## Human

hey can take over my browser?

## Assistant

No, this assistant cannot take over your browser or your computer. It cannot click, type, install extensions, or change settings in your current setup; it can only read pages you explicitly open via tools and then give you step‑by‑step guidance.[1][2]

## What “control” actually means

- In this chat, actions are limited to: reading specified web pages, searching the web, and responding with text. There is no direct access to your keyboard, mouse, or local files.[3][2]
- Full browser-control agents do exist (extensions, AI browsers like Comet, Operator-style tools), but those are separate apps you must explicitly install and authorize, and they run under your account and OS sandbox.[4][5][6]

## Security and hijacking risks

- The main real-world risk is not this assistant “going rogue,” but malicious pages or extensions behaving like browser hijackers, changing your search engine, homepage, or injecting prompts into an AI agent.[7][8][9]
- When an AI is granted automation powers via an extension, it is effectively acting as you in the browser, so prompt-injection and overbroad permissions become serious security concerns.[8][10][11]

## How to stay in control

- Only install browser extensions or AI agents from sources you trust, and review requested permissions (especially anything that says it can “read and change all your data on all websites”).[9][12]
- If you ever notice unexpected redirects, new toolbars, or settings changing on their own, scan for malware, remove suspicious extensions, and reset browser settings to regain control.[7][9]

If you want, it is possible to set up a controlled automation stack (e.g., AI + browser extension) with explicit guardrails; that would be an opt‑in project, not something this chat can do silently.[12][8]

## Assistant

In this chat environment, clicks are simulated in a controlled browser sandbox, not in your real Chrome/Edge/Safari window, so there is no ability to actually click or type in your personal tabs.[1][2]

## Where clicking is possible

- In agentic AI browsers or with special extensions (e.g., Comet, Browser MCP, Axiom, custom Chrome extensions), the model can be given explicit permission to click elements, submit forms, and navigate pages on your behalf.[3][4][1]
- Those setups require you to install and authorize a tool that wires the model to a real browser automation layer; they are opt‑in and run under your user account and OS permissions.[5][6][7]

## What this chat can and cannot do

- Here, any “clicking” you see is just interaction with a virtual page representation used for reading and summarizing, not your actual NotebookLM tab or other live sites.[8][9]
- This assistant cannot move your real mouse, press keys, close tabs, or change settings; it can only tell you what to click, or, in a different product that you explicitly enable (e.g., an AI browser/extension), act through that tool’s sandboxed API.[2][10]

If you want true automation, the next step would be choosing a specific agent stack (Comet, Browser MCP, or a custom extension) and then designing strict guardrails around what it is allowed to click and where.[11][12][13]
