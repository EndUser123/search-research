## Assistant

For a three‑monitor setup, it is usually better to use a dock/expander that can drive all external monitors you routinely use, rather than splitting them between the dock and the laptop ports, unless you have a specific constraint like bandwidth, budget, or port availability that makes a 2‑output dock strictly necessary.[1]

## Key factors to compare

- Cable and workflow simplicity: If your dock supports two external displays and your laptop can natively drive a third via HDMI/USB‑C, using all three from the dock means you plug in one cable and all three come up, which is ideal for a fixed desk.[1]
- Port/driver constraints: Some laptops only support a limited number of independent displays over USB‑C/Thunderbolt, so even if the dock advertises three outputs, you may only get two distinct screens plus the laptop panel; in that case, offloading one to the laptop HDMI can be necessary.[1]
- Performance and stability: Running all externals through a single dock can simplify EDID/monitor detection and reduce weird behavior on sleep/wake, but it can also increase dependence on one device and one USB‑C link for bandwidth if you are pushing high resolutions and refresh rates.  

## When a dual‑output dock is “enough”

- Laptop has reliable native video: If your laptop’s built‑in HDMI or USB‑C alt‑mode port is rock‑solid at the resolution/refresh you use for one monitor, then a 2‑HDMI/DP dock is often sufficient and cheaper, because you can do: Dock → 2 externals, Laptop port → 1 external.  
- You move between locations: For travel or multiple desks, a simpler 2‑output dock per desk plus using the laptop port for the third screen can be a good balance between cost and plug‑in complexity.[1]

In your current cart, every dock you are considering is at least dual‑HDMI plus DP; several explicitly advertise triple‑display support, meaning they can handle 3 external monitors via the dock alone on compatible laptops.[1]

## When to prefer a dock with 3 outputs

- Single‑cable docking is a priority: If you want “sit down, plug one cable, all three screens and peripherals just work,” then a dock with three independent display outputs wired to the dock is the cleanest architecture.[1]
- You use multiple machines: For a desk shared by multiple laptops, having all monitors on the dock means each laptop only needs to connect to the dock, not to separate HDMI cables, which reduces re‑cabling and errors.  

Given your stack and workflow, I would treat 2‑output docks as acceptable only if you are comfortable permanently dedicating one monitor to the laptop’s own port and you do not mind plugging an extra cable when you dock. Otherwise, a dock that can drive all three externals is the more robust “architecturally clean” choice.
