## Human

I want to get a TV and internet here in Calgary, Alberta, but I don't want to pay Rogers and I don't want to pay Telus. Are there any other providers I can get in my apartment building?

## Assistant

You *do* have options beyond Rogers/Shaw and Telus in Calgary, but what’s available in your specific apartment depends on which networks your building is wired for (cable vs fibre vs any building‑specific fibre).[1][2][3][4]

## Reality of “non‑Rogers/Telus” in Calgary

Most smaller ISPs in Calgary actually resell service over Rogers/Shaw’s cable network or Telus’s DSL/fibre, even if you never deal with those companies directly.[5][6][4][1]

- Independent cable/DSL ISPs (use Rogers/Shaw or Telus infrastructure):
  - **Oxio** – highly rated, no contracts, simple pricing; uses cable (Rogers/Shaw) and sometimes Telus wholesale fibre.[2][6][3][4][1]
  - **Lightspeed** – budget cable internet, no contract; people in Calgary report good experiences and stable long‑term pricing.[7][6][4][5]
  - **TekSavvy** – long‑standing independent ISP; offers cable via Rogers/Shaw and some fibre via Telus wholesale.[6][3][4][1][5]
  - **CarryTel** – discount cable plans, usually no contract.[3][4][1]
  - **NetJOI / Netjoi** – Calgary‑focused independent, cable up to near‑gigabit, no contracts and “no price increase” positioning.[4][2][3]
  - Other independents mentioned in Calgary comparisons: **EBOX**, **VMedia**, **Wakey**, **Getus**, **Can‑Com**, **CIK Telecom**.[8][1][2][6][4]

- Truly separate last‑mile options (don’t rely on Rogers/Telus in the same way):
  - **Starlink** – satellite internet, available across Calgary; avoids local cable/fibre entirely but needs rooftop/sky view and is pricier.[9][5][3]
  - **Xplornet/Xplore** – satellite and fixed wireless; more often used in rural or fringe areas around Calgary than in dense apartments.[1][8][3]
  - **Moby** – local Calgary fibre provider with its *own* fibre into some buildings; great if your specific building is lit by them.[3]
  - **SkyChoice** – multi‑technology provider (TV, mobile, phone, internet) using whatever last‑mile tech is available; availability is address‑specific.[10]

The consensus in local discussions is that, inside Calgary city limits, your choices boil down to: Telus or Rogers/Shaw as physical networks; independents that ride on those; or satellite/rare building‑specific fibre.[9][7][5][6][4][1][3]

## TV vs internet: how to structure it

Given how expensive “TV + internet” bundles are from the big two, many Calgarians now get **internet only** (often from an independent ISP) and use streaming/IPTV for TV.[11][12][7][8][4][3]

- Internet:
  - If your building has cable (Shaw/Rogers): Oxio, Lightspeed, NetJOI, CarryTel, Wakey, TekSavvy are candidates.[7][5][2][6][4][3]
  - If your building has Telus fibre/DSL only: TekSavvy, Oxio, and some other independents offer wholesale fibre/DSL in select addresses.[5][4][1][3]
  - If neither is present or you want to avoid them at the infrastructure level: Starlink or possibly Moby/SkyChoice if your building is wired for them.[10][9][1][3]

- TV:
  - Most independents offer **IPTV** add‑ons (e.g., VMedia, VMedia‑style bundles, some TekSavvy/VMedia packages).[11][1]
  - Alternatively, you can:
    - Use mainstream streaming (Netflix, Prime, Crave, YouTube, sports apps).  
    - Use a reputable IPTV provider that runs over your internet connection and replicates cable‑style channel lineups and PVR.[8][11]

A lot of local advice says: buy the cheapest solid internet you can, then handle TV through streaming/IPTV rather than paying for traditional cable.[12][11][7][8]

## How to check your specific building

Because availability is hyper‑local, the main practical step is to plug your exact address into a few providers and an aggregator:

1. Check a comparison site that covers Calgary (e.g., PlanHub or NetSpeedCanada) and enter your address to see which independents show up and on which network (cable vs fibre vs wireless).[2][3]
2. For each of Oxio, Lightspeed, TekSavvy, NetJOI, CarryTel, and Moby, use their “check availability” tool with your apartment’s full postal code + unit to confirm service.[4][1][2][3]
3. If the building manager or condo board is reachable, ask directly:
   - “Is the building wired for any non‑Telus/non‑Rogers fibre, like Moby or building‑specific fibre?”  
   - “Do you have any exclusive agreements that block independent ISPs?”  
4. If you truly want to avoid the big two even as underlying infrastructure, check whether your balcony or windows have clear sky for Starlink; then verify Starlink’s address eligibility.[9][1][5][3]

From a systems perspective, you’ll likely end up either:
- Using an independent ISP riding Rogers/Telus (no direct relationship with them, but same physical lines), or  
- Using satellite (Starlink) or a niche fibre provider if your building supports it.

## Human

I don't want to pay for TV, but I don't want to use streaming services. I want all my local channels; I want the fucking old people TV shows—how do I get that with internet only?

## Assistant

You can get almost all the “old people TV” (local broadcast channels, retro sitcoms, news, etc.) with **internet only + a cheap antenna + a few free channel apps**, without paying for a TV subscription or mainstream streaming bundles.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

## Step 1 – Get your local channels free (antenna)

Calgary still has multiple **over‑the‑air (OTA)** broadcast stations that you can receive for free with a digital TV antenna.[8][2][5][1]

- Main local channels you can get with an antenna in Calgary:
  - Global Calgary (CICT‑DT, virtual 2.1).[5][8][1]
  - CTV Calgary (CFCN‑DT, virtual 4.1).[8][1][5]
  - Citytv Calgary (CKAL‑DT, virtual 5.1).[1][5][8]
  - CBC Calgary (CBRT‑DT, virtual 9.1).[5][8][1]
  - Yes TV Calgary (CKCS‑DT, virtual 32.1).[8][1][5]
  - Omni Calgary (CJCO‑DT, virtual 38.1).[1][5][8]

Local discussions say you typically see **5–7 free channels** in Calgary with a decent indoor antenna, covering CBC, CTV, Global, Citytv and Yes TV.[2]

Concrete steps:
- Buy a **flat indoor HDTV antenna** (Best Buy, Walmart, London Drugs, Amazon).[2]
- Plug it into your TV’s antenna input and run a channel scan.  
- If reception is weak, move it near a window or higher up, or consider a small amplified antenna.  

Result: you now have live local news, daytime shows, and a lot of those old‑school syndicated series **without any monthly TV bill**.

## Step 2 – Use free “network apps” instead of streaming subscriptions

Without paying for TV, you can still use the **free apps and websites from the Canadian networks** to get current shows, reruns, and lots of “old people TV” content over your internet.[3][4][6][7]

Key pieces:
- **CBC Gem** (free account tier):
  - Has 14 free CBC News streaming channels, including **CBC News Calgary**, live 24/7.[4][3]
  - Carries CBC network shows and classic content, accessible via app or website.[3][4]

- **CTV app / CTV.ca**:
  - Free access to the **last 7 days of episodes** for most shows on the CTV over‑the‑air network (no subscription needed).[6][4]
  - Good for catching up on network dramas, news, and older episode reruns.[4][6]

- **Global TV app**:
  - Free local news streams and recent episodes from Global’s broadcast network.[4]

- **Citytv app**:
  - Free live channel streams and catch‑up content for Citytv in Canada on supported devices.[4]

- **Crave’s “CTV Throwback / CTV Movies” free section**:
  - Includes a large library of **free classic TV shows and movies**, labeled as fan favourites and timeless TV classics.[7]

These are technically “streaming,” but they are **direct network apps** with free tiers, not subscription VOD platforms like Netflix/Prime, and they focus heavily on the kind of broadcast and older show catalogue you’re asking for.[7][4]

## Step 3 – Build an “internet‑only old‑school TV” setup

You can combine antenna + free apps into something that behaves like classic TV:

- Hardware:
  - A basic **smart TV**, or  
  - A dumb TV plus a **cheap streaming stick** (Roku, Fire TV, or similar) just to run the free network apps.[3][4]
  - An indoor antenna connected to the TV tuner.[2]

- What you actually watch:
  - Live OTA channels via antenna: CBC, CTV, Global, Citytv, Yes TV, Omni.[5][8][1][2]
  - Live and on‑demand news + shows via free apps: CBC Gem, CTV, Global, Citytv.[6][3][4]
  - Older classic shows and movies via Crave’s free CTV Throwback/Movies section.[7]

This gives you:
- Local news and current network schedules like traditional cable.[8][1][5]
- A ton of **older syndicated shows and “comfort TV”** via network catch‑up and throwback catalogues.[7][4]
- No monthly TV bill; you only pay for your **internet** and the one‑time cost of an antenna and possibly a streaming stick.[2][3][4]

## Step 4 – Optional: grey‑market IPTV (with caveats)

Some people who want “every channel + old reruns” without a conventional TV plan use **unofficial IPTV** services that mimic a cable lineup and run over regular internet.[9][4]

- FACT: These services exist and are widely used, but legality and reliability vary and they are not endorsed by broadcasters.[9][4]
- CONSENSUS: If you go this route, you should treat it as **high‑risk**: streams may disappear, EPGs can be flaky, and providers can vanish.[9][4]

Given your preference and desire for local channels, I’d treat that as a last resort and start with **OTA + official free apps**, which are far more stable and legal.
