I'm on an Android tablet without a cell plan, only Wi-Fi.  I'd like to text my wife who's using her cell phone

## Assistant

You have two main options on a Wi‑Fi–only Android tablet: use an internet messaging app you both share, or get yourself a phone number that works over Wi‑Fi (e.g., Google Voice).[1][2][3]

### 1. Easiest: Use a shared messaging app

If your wife can install an app, this is simplest:

- WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Facebook Messenger, etc. all work over Wi‑Fi and don’t need a cell plan on your tablet.[2][1]
- You both install the same app, create accounts, and then you can message her phone from your tablet as long as both devices have internet.[1][2]

Example:  
Install WhatsApp from Play Store on your tablet, register with your Google account or a number you control, then add your wife’s mobile number as a contact in WhatsApp and chat there.[2][1]

### 2. Get your own Wi‑Fi phone number (pseudo‑SMS)

If you specifically want to send messages *to her normal phone number* and don’t care if they are technically SMS or app messages:

- Use a service like Google Voice, Hushed, or similar “Wi‑Fi texting” apps that give you a phone number and let you send/receive texts over Wi‑Fi.[4][2]
- After setup, you open that app on your tablet and text her cell number directly from there; she sees normal texts from your new number.[4][2]

Note: classic carrier SMS from *your* existing cell number is not possible directly from a Wi‑Fi‑only tablet unless it’s paired/synced with a phone that has cell service. SMS normally requires a cellular connection on at least one end of the sender’s setup.[5][3]

WhatsApp always needs *some* phone number to register, and the normal tablet “companion” setup requires access to your existing phone at least once.[1][2]

Since you don’t have your phone with you right now, here are practical alternatives that work *only* on the tablet over Wi‑Fi:

Pick something that doesn’t require your own mobile number:

- Facebook Messenger: Log in with your Facebook account, then search your wife by name or phone/email and chat.[3]
- Telegram: You can sign up with a temporary number once, then use username‑to‑username messaging; if she already has Telegram, she can find you by username.[4][5]
- Signal still needs a number, so it’s not ideal without access to a phone.

If your wife can install one of these quickly, this is the least painful route.

### 2. Get a temporary/virtual number, then use WhatsApp

If you really want WhatsApp *now* without your physical phone:

- Install a virtual‑number app like TextNow or similar on the tablet; it will give you a free number that can receive SMS or calls.[6][7]
- Install WhatsApp on the tablet, enter that virtual number, and choose SMS or “Call me” to get the verification code inside the virtual‑number app.[7][8][6]
- After verification, you have a WhatsApp account that lives only on the tablet and can message your wife’s normal cell number.[6]

Caveats:  
- Some virtual numbers are blocked by WhatsApp, so you may need to try one or two providers.[9][7]
- Using virtual numbers may not be fully aligned with WhatsApp’s terms and could get the number banned if abused.[7]

### 3. If you can access *any* phone line (landline, VoIP)

If you have access to a landline or VoIP number where you are:

- Install WhatsApp on the tablet, enter that landline/VoIP number, let SMS fail, then choose “Call me” to hear the code via an automated voice call on that line.[10][7]
- Enter the code on the tablet and you’re in.

### How you’d use it on your Android tablet

1. Install the Google Voice app from the Play Store on the tablet.[6][3][7]
2. Sign in with your Google account and choose a free Google Voice number (US‑only service).[5][4]
3. In the app’s Settings, set calls to “Prefer Wi‑Fi and mobile data” so everything works over Wi‑Fi.[9][10][8]
4. In Google Voice, start a new message, type your wife’s existing cell number, and text her from the tablet.[7][1][2]

### Important limitations

- Google Voice is officially for U.S. residents and issues U.S. numbers only.[5]
- It cannot be used to call emergency services; you’d still need a regular phone for that.[12][5]
- Texts and calls go through the Google Voice app (or website), not through your tablet’s default Messages app.[1][6][7]
