## Human

Which beta 3 blood pressure meds are typically used with adhd meds

## Assistant

Nebivolol (Bystolic), a third-generation beta blocker with beta-3 receptor activity via nitric oxide-mediated vasodilation, is a blood pressure medication sometimes used alongside ADHD stimulants like Adderall. No true beta-3 selective blockers exist for routine hypertension treatment, but cardioselective beta-1 blockers like nebivolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol, or atenolol are generally compatible with ADHD meds under monitoring, as stimulants can raise heart rate and blood pressure while beta blockers counteract this.[1][2][3][4][5][6]

## Common Combinations
Beta blockers are not first-line for uncomplicated hypertension but become relevant when ADHD stimulants cause cardiovascular side effects.[7][5]

- **Nebivolol**: Cardioselective (beta-1), promotes vasodilation; low-dose (e.g., 2.5-5 mg) praised in user reports for pairing with stimulants without major interference.[3][6]
- **Propranolol**: Non-selective; used for stimulant-induced anxiety/hypertension but may blunt ADHD med efficacy slightly.[1][7]
- **Metoprolol**: Cardioselective; reported to aid calmness with ADHD meds in some cases.[8]

Evidence shows no high risk of harm from beta blockers + stimulants, though regular blood pressure and heart rate checks are advised.[2][1]

## Key Considerations
Stimulants like Adderall or Vyvanse increase systolic BP by ~1-6 mmHg and heart rate by ~4-6 bpm on average; beta blockers mitigate this but require dose adjustments. Always consult a doctor—interactions vary by patient, and abrupt beta blocker stops can cause rebound effects. Non-beta options like alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine, clonidine) are also used for ADHD with BP benefits.[9][10][11][12]
