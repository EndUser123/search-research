## Human

How does a dehumidifier work

## Assistant

A dehumidifier removes excess moisture from the air to improve comfort and prevent issues like mold. Most common models operate like refrigerators or air conditioners by cooling air below its dew point, causing water vapor to condense into liquid.[1][2]

## Compressor Dehumidifiers
A fan draws humid air over cold evaporator coils filled with refrigerant, cooling the air and condensing moisture that drips into a collection tank or drain.  The now-dry air passes over warm condenser coils, reheating it slightly before releasing it back into the room, which enhances efficiency by drawing in more moist air.  These work best above 20°C (68°F) and 45% relative humidity.[2][3][1]

## Desiccant Dehumidifiers
Moist air passes through a rotating wheel coated with silica gel or similar absorbent material that captures water molecules.  Hot reactivation air (90-140°C) then evaporates the moisture from the wheel, expelling it outside while dry air is supplied to the space.  These perform well in colder temperatures unlike compressor types.[4][5][6]

## Other Types
Whole-house or ventilating dehumidifiers integrate with HVAC systems, exchanging moist indoor air with drier outdoor air or using heat exchangers.  Hybrid models combine compressor and desiccant methods for versatile performance across conditions.  Chemical absorbent units use single-use materials like silica gel packs for small-scale applications.[5][7][2]
