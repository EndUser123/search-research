## Human

I’m going to the US tomorrow from Canada.  Does the US really want
To see my email or social media?

## Assistant

No, US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) does not routinely require Canadian citizens to provide email addresses or social media details upfront for land or air entry, as Canadians are generally exempt from ESTA and visa processes. However, CBP officers have broad authority at the border to request access to your electronic devices, which could include viewing emails, social media apps, photos, or messages without a warrant.[1][2][3][4][5]

## Device Search Authority
CBP can perform "basic" searches (manual review of device contents) on any traveler's phone, laptop, or similar without suspicion, and "advanced" searches (using external tools) with supervisory approval if national security or law violations are suspected. This applies to Canadians crossing from Calgary-area borders or flying to the US, and refusal may lead to device seizure, entry denial, or detention. Searches focus on verifying your travel purpose, detecting fraud, or security threats, not mass email/social media collection.[6][7][2][8][9][4]

## Proposed Social Media Rules
A December 2025 CBP proposal seeks mandatory 5-year social media history (plus emails/phone numbers) for ESTA users from 42 visa-waiver countries, but this does not apply to Canadian citizens and remains in public comment as of February 2026. Even if implemented, land border Canadians typically just need proof of ties to Canada (e.g., job letter, return ticket).[10][11][12][1]

## Traveler Tips
- Minimize data on devices: Use cloud storage, traveler profiles, or leave sensitive devices behind.[2][13]
- You can refuse access verbally, but expect consequences like denial of entry; consult a lawyer post-incident.[6]
- Carry ties to Canada (employment proof, property docs) to affirm quick return.[1]

## Human

What should Canadians do if CBP asks to see their phone

## Assistant

Canadians should comply politely if US CBP asks to see their phone, as officers have legal authority to conduct searches without a warrant or suspicion. Refusal risks entry denial, device seizure (potentially for weeks), or detention, though you have the right to verbally decline and request supervisory review.[1][2][3]

## Preparation Steps
- Back up data to the cloud and use a "clean" traveler phone/laptop with minimal personal info (e.g., delete apps, messages, calendars).[4][1]
- Ensure your story matches device content: Clear travel purpose, proof of return (job letter, ties to Canada), no conflicting plans.[2][1]
- Turn on airplane mode or disconnect Wi-Fi/Bluetooth before the border to limit remote access.[1]

## During the Search
- Provide your device and password if asked; CBP can demand access to emails/social media but not cloud data beyond what's on-device.[3][1]
- Stay calm, cooperative, and brief—don't volunteer extra info. Ask for a receipt if seized, noting how to retrieve it.[2][1]
- Request a supervisor if uncomfortable, but expect the same outcome.[5]

## Aftermath Options
- If denied entry or device seized, contact the Canadian consulate from Canada; no appeal for entry decisions.[6][2]
- Document everything for potential privacy complaints to CBP or legal review later.[1]
