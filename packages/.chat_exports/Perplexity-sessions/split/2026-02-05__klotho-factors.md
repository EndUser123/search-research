## Human

Klotho Factors

## Assistant

Klotho refers to a family of proteins, primarily α-Klotho, recognized as an anti-aging factor in biological research. It plays key roles in regulating phosphate and vitamin D metabolism, while mutations lead to accelerated aging phenotypes like shortened lifespan and organ dysfunction in mouse models.[1][2][6]

## Key Functions
Klotho acts as a co-receptor for fibroblast growth factors (FGFs), such as FGF23 (via α-Klotho), influencing mineral homeostasis, insulin/IGF-1 signaling, and Wnt pathways. It also suppresses inflammation via NF-κB inhibition, protects against oxidative stress, and supports cognitive function by enhancing synaptic plasticity and platelet-derived factors like PF4.[2][3][6][8]

## Health Impacts
Higher Klotho levels correlate with longevity, better muscle regeneration, cardiovascular health, and neuroprotection; deficiencies link to chronic kidney disease, sarcopenia, osteoporosis, and cognitive decline. In humans, it's expressed mainly in kidneys, brain, and parathyroid, with circulating forms exerting hormone-like effects.[4][5][7]

## Modulating Factors
Exercise, vitamin D (calcitriol), and PPARγ agonists can boost Klotho, while stress, inflammation, angiotensin II, and aging reduce it. Platelet factors induced by Klotho may mediate rapid cognitive benefits in young and aged brains.[3][6][4]
