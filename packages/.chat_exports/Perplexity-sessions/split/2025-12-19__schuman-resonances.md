## Human

Schuman resonances

## Assistant

Schumann resonances are very low-frequency electromagnetic standing waves that form in the cavity between Earth’s surface and the ionosphere, primarily excited by global lightning activity. They produce a set of resonance peaks, with the strongest “fundamental” mode near 7.8 Hz and additional harmonics at higher frequencies.[1][2][3][4]

## What they are

- The space between Earth’s conducting surface and the electrically conductive ionosphere acts as a spherical **waveguide**, trapping extremely low-frequency (ELF) electromagnetic waves.[5][1]
- Lightning strokes around the globe continuously launch ELF waves that circle Earth and interfere constructively at specific frequencies, creating standing waves called Schumann resonances.[3][6][1]

## Main frequencies

- The fundamental resonance is around 7.8 Hz, with higher modes commonly observed near 14.3, 20.8, 27.3, and 33.8 Hz, though exact values vary with ionospheric conditions.[2][7][4][1]
- These fields are extremely weak: typical magnetic amplitudes are on the order of 1 picotesla and electric fields a few microvolts per meter, far smaller than Earth’s static magnetic field or common man‑made EM fields.[7][8][1]

## How they are measured

- Detection requires specialized ELF receivers: magnetic induction coils with many turns around high‑permeability cores, and high‑impedance electric field antennas (e.g., ball antennas) coupled to sensitive amplifiers.[9][6][1]
- Networks of monitoring stations produce spectrograms that show the resonance peaks and their diurnal and seasonal variations, which correlate with global lightning and day–night ionosphere changes.[10][1][9]

## Links to natural phenomena and earthquakes

- Variations in Schumann resonance spectra reflect changes in global thunderstorm activity, ionospheric conditions, and in some studies, anthropogenic ELF noise.[6][10]
- Several research groups are investigating whether anomalies in these resonances can serve as precursors or contextual signals for earthquakes or severe weather, but this remains exploratory and not an operationally reliable prediction method.[11][12][10]

## Claims about health and “Earth’s heartbeat”

- Popular sources often call 7.83 Hz “Earth’s **heartbeat**” and attribute broad healing, mood, or consciousness effects to it, inspiring sound therapy products and binaural beat tracks tuned to this frequency.[13][14][15][9]
- Mainstream physics and space science sources emphasize that, while the resonances are real and scientifically important for geophysics and atmospheric studies, there is no robust evidence they directly affect human health or brain function in the sweeping ways often claimed in alternative medicine marketing.[8][4]
