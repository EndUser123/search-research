## Human

What is the difference between Islamic Jesus and Christian Jesus?

## Assistant

The core difference is that **Christianity** identifies Jesus as the divine Son of God, part of the Trinity, whose crucifixion and resurrection are central to salvation, while **Islam** regards ʿĪsā (Jesus) as a highly honored prophet and Messiah, but not divine, not the Son of God, and not crucified in the same way Christians believe.[1][2]

## Main differences

- **Nature of Jesus:** Christians believe Jesus is God incarnate and the Son of God; Muslims believe Jesus is a human prophet and messenger of God.[2][1]
- **Crucifixion:** Christians believe Jesus was crucified and rose from the dead; Islam rejects the Christian crucifixion-resurrection doctrine and teaches that Jesus was not ultimately killed on the cross.[1][2]
- **Salvation:** In Christianity, Jesus’ death and resurrection are the basis of salvation; in Islam, salvation is tied to submitting to God and God’s mercy, not to atonement through Jesus.[2][1]
- **Trinity:** Christianity teaches one God in three persons; Islam rejects the Trinity and insists on the absolute oneness of God.[1][2]

## Shared points

Both faiths give Jesus a special place: he is born of the Virgin Mary, performs miracles by God’s permission or power, and is honored as the Messiah.  They also both expect Jesus to have a future role in the end times, though the details differ.[3][2][1]

## Important nuance

When people say “Islamic Jesus,” they usually mean ʿĪsā in Islamic theology, not a different historical person. The disagreement is mainly about **who Jesus is** and **what his death means**, not whether he mattered.[3][2]
