## Assistant

996 refers to a work schedule of 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., 6 days a week (about 72 hours), originating in parts of China’s tech and startup scene and now discussed more broadly in tech globally.[1][2][3]

### What 996 means

- **Definition**: Work from 09:00 to 21:00, six days per week, totaling roughly 72 hours.[4][3][1]
- **Origins**: Popularized in Chinese internet/tech companies as a “hard‑work” culture and later echoed by some AI/startup firms in the US and elsewhere.[5][3][1]
- **Status in China**: China’s labor law caps normal hours at 8 per day and 44 per week, and the Supreme People’s Court has explicitly stated that enforced 996 schedules violate these limits.[6][7][8][5]

### Legal and health issues

- **Legality**: Chinese authorities and courts have ruled that mandatory or disguised 996 overtime breaches labor law (especially when it is the standard schedule and not occasional overtime).[7][8][9]
- **Enforcement**: Despite this, weak enforcement means many firms still expect de facto 996, often without proper overtime pay or with subtle coercion.[10][5]
- **Health impacts**: Extended 70+ hour weeks correlate with higher risks of burnout, sleep disorders, cardiovascular problems, and stress‑related illness; several high‑profile overwork deaths have intensified criticism of 996 as “modern slavery.”[11][12][1][4]

### Cultural debate and trends

- **Pro‑996 arguments**: Some founders portray 996 as a temporary sacrifice to “build something great,” framing it as voluntary and tied to high upside (equity, rapid career growth).[13][3][12]
- **Criticism**: Worker advocates, media, and many professionals argue that 996 is exploitative, unsustainable, and often imposed under unequal bargaining power rather than true choice.[12][4][5][11]
- **Current trend**: In China, regulators are increasingly signaling that 996 is unacceptable, but long hours persist in parts of tech, manufacturing, and sales; in the US/EU, a small subset of AI and early‑stage startups are experimenting with 996‑like expectations.[3][6][5][12]
