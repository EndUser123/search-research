### The Fundamental Problem

USB-C to USB-C extension cables are **explicitly forbidden by the USB standard** because:
1. They can't include an e-marker (electronic identifier) that tells devices the cable's capabilities
2. This creates fire hazards with high-wattage chargers (240W PD in your cable's case)[1]
3. Signal integrity degrades unpredictably with dual connectors (insertion loss of ~2-3dB per interface)[1]

### Real Performance Impact

Even if it "works," you may get:
- **Intermittent disconnects** with the enclosure
- **Throttled speeds** (dropping from 10 Gbps to 5 Gbps or worse)[3][5]

### Why 6 Feet Is Marginal

1. **USB 3.2 Gen 2 operates at high frequency (10 GHz)** — high frequencies are susceptible to attenuation over distance[2][1]

3. **Real-world losses accumulate:**
   - Connector insertion loss (~0.5 dB per end)
   - Cable trace loss (~0.3-0.5 dB per foot)
   - Impedance discontinuities at joints
   - Total at 6 feet: ~1.5-2 dB, which is near the error-correction threshold[4]
