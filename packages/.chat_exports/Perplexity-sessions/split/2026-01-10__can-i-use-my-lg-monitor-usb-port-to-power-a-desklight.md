## Human

can i use my lg monitor usb port to power a desklight?

## Assistant

Yes, you can often power a small USB-powered desk light from an LG monitor’s USB port, as long as the light’s power draw is within the port’s limits and the monitor is on.[1][2][3]

## What monitor USB ports can supply

- Most USB-A ports on monitors supply 5 V at about 0.5–0.9 A (2.5–4.5 W), similar to standard USB 2.0/3.0 ports.[4][3]
- If your LG has a USB-C port with USB PD, it may supply significantly more (15–90 W), mainly for charging laptops or tablets.[5][3]
- LG’s own docs state their monitor USB ports are intended for charging phones and powering peripherals like keyboard/mouse, so light low-power accessories are expected use.[2]

## When it’s safe to power a desk light

- Check the lamp label or listing for input like “5 V 0.5 A” or “5 V 1 A”; aim to stay at or under about 0.5–0.9 A on a USB-A port.[3][6][4]
- Many USB-powered monitor light bars are explicitly designed to run from a monitor USB port at 5 V, and work fine in that configuration.[7][1]
- If the lamp tries to draw more current than the port can provide, the port should typically shut down rather than be damaged, but the light may flicker or turn off.[8][9]

## Practical checks for your setup

- Confirm your exact LG model’s specs (manual or LG support page) for USB output limits and whether it has USB PD on USB-C.[10][2][5]
- Make sure the monitor’s USB upstream cable to the PC (if required for its hub) is connected; some monitors only fully power downstream ports when this is plugged in.[11][2]
- If you notice instability (light flicker, monitor USB errors), switch to a dedicated USB power adapter rather than the monitor port.[4][8]

If you share your specific LG model and the lamp’s rated input (e.g., 5 V / 0.5 A), a more precise yes/no is possible.

## Assistant

**Quntis LI-HY-0212-BK Monitor Light Bar** powers via USB at 5V and draws up to 9W maximum (about 1.8A at full brightness), with 88 front LEDs (0.2W each) for desk lighting and 32 rear RGB LEDs. It includes dimming, color temperature adjustment (3000K–6500K), and wireless remote control, designed for monitor mounting without glare.[1][2]

## Power Compatibility
Standard LG monitor USB-A ports typically output 5V at 0.5–0.9A (2.5–4.5W), which falls short of the light's 9W max draw. Reviews note it can run from a desktop PC USB port (often 0.9A+), but may flicker or dim at high brightness on lower-output ports like most monitors; LG recommends 10W+ supplies for stability.[2][4][5][6]

## Usage Recommendations
- Test at low brightness first—front light may work partially, but full use (especially RGB rear) likely needs a dedicated 5V/2A+ adapter or PC USB hub.[2]
- If your LG has USB-C PD (15W+), that could handle it fully after confirming model specs.[5][7]
- No risk of damage to the monitor port, as USB limits current automatically.[8][9]
