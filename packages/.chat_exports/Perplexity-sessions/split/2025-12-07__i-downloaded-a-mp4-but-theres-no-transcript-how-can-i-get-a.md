## Human

I downloaded a mp4, but there's no transcript.  How can I get a transcript from free tools or apps?

## Assistant

Use free web-based AI tools like SoundWise or Clipchamp to transcribe your local MP4 file by uploading it directly—no registration or limits required for basic use.[1][2]

## Web Tools
- **SoundWise.ai**: Drag and drop your MP4 for unlimited free transcription in 90+ languages; processes locally for privacy and exports as TXT.[2]
- **Clipchamp (Microsoft)**: Free online editor with built-in AI transcription; select your language, upload MP4, and copy the synced text.[1]
- **Otter.ai or Notta.ai**: Upload MP4s for quick AI transcripts with speaker ID; free tiers allow several minutes monthly.[3][4]

## Offline/Local Options
For privacy-focused transcription without uploads, use open-source apps like Whisper Desktop (via tools like Vibe or Buzz) that run AI models locally on your machine.  These support MP4 input, multiple languages, and subtitle export, aligning with your video analysis workflows.[5]

## Quick Hacks
Upload the MP4 privately to YouTube, wait for auto-generated captions, then download the transcript.  This leverages Google's speech-to-text for free accuracy on longer files.[6][7]

## Human

Ok, I added my video to clipchamp.  What do I do next?

## Assistant

Click your video on the timeline, then use Clipchamp’s captions/transcript feature to generate and download the text.[1][2]

## Generate the transcript

1. Make sure your MP4 is in the timeline (drag it there if it’s only in the media bin).[3]
2. Click the video clip in the timeline so it’s highlighted.[3]
3. On the right side, open the **Captions** or **Transcript** tab in the property panel, or click the **Captions** icon above the video preview.[2][4]
4. Click **Generate transcript** or **Transcribe media**.  
5. Choose the spoken **language**, optionally enable profanity filtering, then confirm to start transcription.[5][2]

## Get the text out

1. Once the transcript appears, skim and fix any typos directly in the transcript panel.[4][2]
2. Use the menu in the transcript/captions area to **download** or **export** an **SRT** (subtitle) file.[1][3]
3. Open the SRT in a text editor if you just want raw text, or keep it as-is if you’ll use it as subtitles.[1]
