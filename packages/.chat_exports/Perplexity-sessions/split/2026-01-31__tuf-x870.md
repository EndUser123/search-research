## Assistant

ASUS TUF Gaming X870 is an AM5 ATX motherboard series built around AMD’s X870 chipset, aimed at Ryzen 7000/8000/9000 CPUs with modern I/O (PCIe 5.0, DDR5, USB4, Wi‑Fi 7 on some models).[1][4][6]

### Core specs (X870‑PLUS WIFI)

- Socket/chipset: AMD AM5, X870; supports Ryzen 7000, 8000, 9000 series desktop CPUs.[4][6]
- Power delivery: 16+2+1 80A power stages, 8‑layer PCB, designed for high‑core‑count Ryzen and sustained loads.[1][4]
- Memory: 4× DDR5 DIMMs, up to 256 GB, up to DDR5‑8000+ OC depending on kit/IMC.[6][4]
- Expansion:  
  - 1× PCIe 5.0 x16 slot for GPU.[4][6]
  - 4× M.2 (2× PCIe 5.0, 2× PCIe 4.0) with large heatsinks.[4]
- Connectivity:  
  - Wi‑Fi 7 (802.11be) and 2.5 GbE LAN.[1][4]
  - Dual USB4 40 Gbps Type‑C on rear, plus 20 Gbps front‑panel Type‑C with PD fast charge.[4]
  - HDMI for iGPU output.[6][4]
- Target use: “Advanced AI PC” marketing, gaming, and general high‑end desktop with long‑term AM5 support.[6][1][4]

### X870 vs X870E (context)

- X870E boards guarantee PCIe 5.0 for both GPU and at least one NVMe slot and use a dual‑chipset setup, but lane counts are similar (44 total) and X870 also brings native USB4.[6]
- TUF X870‑PLUS aims at value: strong VRM, 4 M.2, latest I/O, without the more extreme feature set/price of premium X870E boards.[4][6]

### If you’re considering it for a build

For your likely use (heavy dev workflow, AI work, lots of storage):

- Pros: plenty of M.2, good VRM for sustained loads, DDR5 headroom, USB4/Type‑C for fast external drives, Wi‑Fi 7 + 2.5 GbE, AM5 longevity.[1][6][4]
- Potential cons: only one PCIe 5.0 x16 slot (fine unless you need exotic add‑in cards), “TUF” line is more value‑oriented than ROG in terms of extras.[6][4]

## Human

"avoid M.2_2 if using a PCIe GPU to prevent x16→x8 bifurcation", how do I know?

## Assistant

**Yes, you can easily confirm this on the ASUS TUF Gaming X870-PLUS WIFI.** The warning about M.2_2 sharing lanes with the primary PCIe x16 (G5) GPU slot is explicitly documented.[3][6]

### How to Verify
- **Motherboard Manual (PDF)**: Download from ASUS support page (search "TUF GAMING X870-PLUS WIFI manual"). Page ~9 and PCIe section state: "PCIEX16(G5) shares bandwidth with M.2_2. PCIEX16(G5) will run to x8 mode when M.2_2 runs."[6]
- **ASUS Tech Specs Page**: Under "PCIe Slots" and "Storage": PCIe 5.0 x16 slot note: "**The PCIEX16(G5) shares bandwidth with M.2_2**." Direct link in prior results.[3]
- **BIOS Visual**: Boot to BIOS → Advanced → PCIe/Storage submenus show lane allocation tables (e.g., GPU x16 → x8 if M.2_2 populated). Real-time indicator under boot.[2]

### Quick Test (Post-Build)
1. Install GPU in PCIEX16(G5), populate only M.2_1 or M.2_3/4 first.  
2. GPU-Z or HWInfo → PCIe tab: Confirms x16 @ PCIe 5.0.  
3. Add SSD to M.2_2 → Drops to x8 (still fine for 99% GPUs; ~1-5% perf hit max).[7][2]

**For your workflow**: Use M.2_1 (CPU PCIe 5.0, boot/OS), M.2_3/4 (chipset PCIe 4.0, storage/transcripts). Keeps GPU full x16 unconditionally.[2][3]

## Human

wait, M.2_2 is a slot not a version?

## Assistant

Yes, M.2_2 is a specific slot label on the ASUS TUF Gaming X870-PLUS WIFI motherboard, not an M.2 "version" like PCIe gen or form factor.

### Slot Breakdown
Motherboards label M.2 slots as M.2_1, M.2_2, etc., to show their position and lane source (from CPU or chipset).[1][2]
- **M.2_1**: PCIe 5.0 x4 (CPU direct, no sharing).  
- **M.2_2**: PCIe 5.0 x4 (shares GPU lanes → x16→x8 split).  
- **M.2_3/4**: PCIe 4.0 x4 (chipset, independent).  

Your Kingston NV3 fits all as standard M.2 2280 PCIe NVMe—slot choice only affects GPU bandwidth if using M.2_2.[3]
