## Human

Calgary how much for the second main water line

## Assistant

Costs for installing a second main water line in Calgary vary widely depending on whether it involves private property work or extending under public property, but typical replacement or new installation quotes range from $10,000 to $18,000. For backyard suites or secondary structures requiring a new water service connection under public land, costs can reach $20,000 to $30,000 or more due to specialized contractors and City requirements. Permit fees for related plumbing or development site servicing start low, around $112 to $1,533, but do not cover full installation labor and materials.[1][2][3][4][5]

## Cost Factors
Pipe material, excavation method (trenchless vs. open), line length, and depth influence pricing. City approval through plumbing permits or Development Site Servicing Plans (DSSP) adds administrative steps, with re-inspection fees up to twice the base if issues arise. Alberta-wide residential waterline installs average $3,000-$6,000, but Calgary-specific quotes trend higher due to urban regulations.[6][4][1]

## Permit Requirements
A plumbing permit ($112 + 4% fee = $116.50) is needed for new or altered water lines. For secondary suites, additional building permits ($388+) and potential development permits apply. Contact Calgary's Water Utility for service connection specifics via WaterUtilityInspections@calgary.ca.[7][2][4]

## Recommendations
Obtain quotes from licensed plumbers like Mr. Rooter or Mr. Trenchless, and check for service line insurance covering up to $25,000. Verify if your project qualifies under Water Utility Bylaw 40M2006 for fees like reconnections. For precise costs, submit a DSSP application ($1,533 initial fee).[8][9][4][1]

## Assistant

Installing a second main water service line from the City of Calgary's reservoir (public water main) involves utility connection fees set by the Water Utility Bylaw 40M2006, but specific rates for additional services are not explicitly listed in current public schedules and require a Development Site Servicing Plan (DSSP) application. Plumbing trade permits for such work start at a $112 processing fee plus $9.79 per $1,000 of construction value (minimum total $116.50), while a full DSSP submission costs $1,533 initially. Total project costs, including contractor labor for trenching to the reservoir connection, typically range $10,000-$30,000 based on local quotes for similar extensions.[1][2][3][4]

## Human

For the city to replace the line from the reservoir

## Assistant

The City of Calgary owns and replaces the public portion of the water service line from the reservoir (water main) to the property line at no direct cost to homeowners, covering breaks, leaks, or proactive upgrades like lead pipe removals. Homeowners handle the private portion from the service valve to the house, with replacement costs typically $8,000-$18,000 via contractors; City turn-off/on fees apply during repairs. For lead service lines, the City coordinates full public-private replacements (up to the foundation), subsidizing the private side to cap homeowner costs at around $3,500 (payable upfront or via taxes).[1][2][3][4][5]

## Responsibilities
- **City-owned (free replacement)**: From reservoir main to service valve near property line, including valves.[1]
- **Homeowner-owned**: From service valve to meter/foundation; requires plumbing permit ($116.50 min).[1]
- **Shared lead programs**: City replaces public side only after private replacement or bundles both to minimize disruption.[3]

## Replacement Process
Report issues via 311; City confirms leak location, turns off service (fees ~$200-$400), and repairs public side promptly. For non-emergency public upgrades (e.g., corrosion), included in Water Main Replacement Program funded by utility rates. Homeowners need contractor clearance from utilities before private work.[6][5]

Contact 311 or Water Utility for inspection; verify insurance for private coverage up to $25k.[2]

Calgary has a broken reservoir main line

## Assistant

Calgary experienced a catastrophic break in the Bearspaw South feeder main—a critical line from the Bearspaw Water Treatment Plant to city reservoirs—on December 30, 2025, near 16th Avenue N.W. and Sarcee Trail, causing flooding, road closures, and vehicle rescues. This marks the second failure of the same pipe since June 2024, prompting Stage 4 outdoor water restrictions citywide, indoor conservation pleas, and a boil water advisory for areas like Bowness, Parkdale, Montgomery, Point McKay, and West Hillhurst. City crews isolated the break, opened the Emergency Operations Centre, and shifted supply from Glenmore Plant; repairs are expected in 7-10 days with flushing/testing.[1][2][3][4][5]

## Incident Details
The rupture flooded under the CPKC rail bridge and Trans-Canada Highway, buckling asphalt over 200m and trapping 13 people in vehicles overnight. The 60% capacity feeder main (concrete pipe) burst due to ongoing issues like soil chlorides and wire corrosion, despite 2024 emergency fixes. No injuries reported, but officials urge minimizing water use to avoid shortages.[2][6][7][1]

## Response Measures
- **Immediate**: Flow rerouted; boil advisory (boil 1 min for drinking/cooking).[8]
- **Ongoing**: Excavation underway; parts ready from prior learnings.[5]
- **Long-term**: Twinning the feeder, new treatment plant, redundancy mains planned.[1]

## Impacts and Advice
Citywide outdoor bans (no rinks/snowmaking); monitor updates via calgary.ca/emergencies or 311. Stock bottled water; Mayor vows accountability and full replacement. Repair costs echo 2024's multimillion-dollar scale, funded via utilities.[4][9][2][5]
