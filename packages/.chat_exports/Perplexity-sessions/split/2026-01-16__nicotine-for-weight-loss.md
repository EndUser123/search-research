## Human

Nicotine for weight loss

## Assistant

Nicotine can suppress weight gain primarily by increasing fat metabolism and utilization, as shown in animal studies, though human evidence is more mixed and often tied to smoking. However, its use for weight loss carries significant health risks and is not recommended due to high addiction potential and cardiovascular dangers. Safer alternatives like calorie control and exercise outperform it for sustainable results.

## Mechanisms
Nicotine shifts the body's respiratory exchange ratio toward fat burning, reducing body fat percentage without major changes in food intake, activity, or total energy expenditure in rat models. It boosts lipolysis and thermogenesis, potentially increasing daily energy expenditure by about 10% or 200 kcal, which could lead to 10 kg loss over a year if calories stay constant. Some effects appear independent of appetite suppression, with subtle shifts like smaller meals or more frequent eating.[1][2]

## Evidence from Studies
Animal research consistently shows self-administered nicotine suppresses weight gain via fat metabolism, with effects starting at low doses. Human data links smoking to lower weight, but quitting often causes gain, attributing effects to nicotine. Recent trends promote pouches like Zyn, but clinical proof for non-smokers remains limited.[3][4][1]

## Health Risks
Nicotine is highly addictive, altering brain receptors and dopamine release, leading to dependence harder to quit than many drugs. It raises blood pressure, risks heart attack, stroke, artery hardening, anxiety, insomnia, and may worsen cancers or insulin resistance. Unregulated pouches deliver unknown doses, unlike FDA-approved cessation aids.[5][6][1]

## Recommendations
Focus on proven methods: calculate maintenance calories, create a 500-calorie deficit daily via diet and exercise for 1-3 pounds weekly loss. Include protein per meal, cut sugar, prioritize veggies and grains; pair with activity for dopamine boosts without addiction. Consult a doctor before any nicotine use, especially with heart issues.[1]
