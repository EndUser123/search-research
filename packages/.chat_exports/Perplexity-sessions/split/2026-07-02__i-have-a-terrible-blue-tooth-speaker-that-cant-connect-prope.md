## Assistant

You can’t directly mount the Edifier R1280T to a monitor out of the box, but you *can* rig monitor‑adjacent mounting using VESA‑based brackets, clamps, or DIY hardware if you’re comfortable with some tinkering. The R1280T is a mid‑size bookshelf speaker, not a lightweight “clip‑on” monitor bar, so you need to treat it like a small shelf load.[1][2][3]
## What the R1280T is physically
- Dimensions: roughly \(146 \times 234 \times 196\) mm (W × H × D), about 5.75" wide × 9.5" tall × 7" deep per speaker.[1][3]
- Weight: about 4.9 kg total pair, so each speaker is roughly 2.4–2.5 kg.[4][1]
- Design: MDF bookshelf cabinet with **no built‑in mounting points** (no threaded inserts, no keyholes), intended for desk or stand use.[3][1]
Because of that, **direct “clip to monitor bezel” mounting is not safe**; the weight and leverage can damage your monitor or cause the speakers to fall.[5][6]
## Practical monitor‑adjacent mounting options
### 1. VESA‑based bracket behind the monitor
If your monitor has VESA holes (e.g., 75×75 or 100×100):

- Idea: Use a **VESA adapter plate or custom bracket** bolted into the VESA mount, then attach small shelves or arms that hold the speakers at the sides.  
- There are DIY examples where people:
  - Bolt a flat bar or board to VESA holes.  
  - Attach small speaker shelves, clamps, or articulating “magic arms” to that board.[5][7][8]

Pros:
- Load goes into the monitor mount/arm, not the plastic bezel.  
- Speakers can sit just off‑monitor but visually “mounted.”  

Cons:
- Requires DIY or careful shopping for compatible arms.  
- You must respect weight limits of your monitor arm/stand and the bracket.
### 2. Using desk‑clamp arms (mic arms or “magic arms”)
Another common hack:

- Clamp a **microphone boom arm** or **articulated “magic arm”** to the desk behind the monitor, and attach the speaker to the arm (with a small platform or strap).[5]
- Position the speaker so it appears to float beside or above the monitor, without touching the monitor itself.[8][5]

Pros:
- No stress on the monitor or VESA mount.  
- Very flexible positioning for listening height and angle.  

Cons:
- Visual complexity; requires making a stable attachment to the speaker (e.g., small platform screwed or strapped to the speaker bottom).  
- Arms must be rated to hold ~2.5 kg per speaker.
### 3. Small wall shelves behind/above the monitor
If the wall behind your desk is usable:

- Mount compact **wall shelves** or speaker brackets at ear height behind the monitor.  
- Place the R1280T on those shelves; run speaker wire and RCA/3.5 mm cables down behind the monitor.  

Pros:
- Excellent stability if shelves are properly anchored.  
- Clean desk space and good stereo imaging.  

Cons:
- Requires drilling into walls.  
- Less “monitor‑mounted” feel, more “wall‑mounted” system.
This kind of dimensional drawing shows why you want something more like a shelf or arm than a bezel clamp; the cabinet depth and weight are significant.[1][2]
## Approaches to avoid
- **Double‑sided tape / VHB directly on monitor sides** for R1280T:  
  - For tiny speakers it can work, but this cabinet is too heavy; community suggestions using tape and hinges are for much lighter speakers.[5][9]
  - High risk of dropping the speaker or stressing the monitor frame.  

- Clamping the speaker directly to the **monitor’s thin plastic bezel**:  
  - Most bezels are not designed to carry several kilograms; you could warp or crack the casing.
## Recommended path given your constraints
Given you want a reliable, wired setup and clean ergonomics:

- If you already use a **monitor arm** with VESA:  
  - Look into a **VESA accessory bracket** or DIY flat bar that bolts into the same VESA pattern and extends to the sides, making mini shelves for the speakers.[5][7][8]
- If you don’t want to DIY metalwork:  
  - Use **desk‑clamp mic arms or articulated arms** behind the monitor and mount the speakers on small platforms on those arms — effectively “floating” them next to the screen.[5]

In both cases, the safest mental model is: “I’m mounting *shelves* or *arms* near the monitor,” not the speakers directly on the monitor.
