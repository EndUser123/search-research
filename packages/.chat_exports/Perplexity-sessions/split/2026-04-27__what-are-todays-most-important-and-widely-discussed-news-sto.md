## White House Correspondents' Dinner Shooting

The most significant breaking story involves a shooting incident at the White House Correspondents' Dinner on Saturday evening, April 25, 2026, at the Washington Hilton. Cole Thomas Allen, a 31-year-old California educator and engineer from Torrance, was arrested after opening fire at a security checkpoint while attempting to breach the event where President Trump and his Cabinet were present. Allen was armed with a shotgun, handgun, and multiple knives when he charged the Secret Service barrier at approximately 8:36 p.m., firing at least one shot before being subdued.[2][4][5][6]

Authorities report that Allen sent a "manifesto" to family members approximately ten minutes before the attack, expressing his intent to target Trump administration officials. The suspect traveled by train from Los Angeles through Chicago to Washington, D.C., and checked into the Washington Hilton hotel within days of the attack. President Trump and other officials were safely evacuated by Secret Service during the incident.[4][7][6]

## Deadly Tornado Outbreak

The most destructive was a violent EF-4 tornado that struck Enid, Oklahoma, on Thursday evening, causing complete destruction in some areas and triggering a rare tornado emergency. In North Texas, deadly tornadoes killed at least two people near Gateway Bay, approximately 80 miles northwest of Dallas, damaging homes and displacing numerous residents.[3][1]

Nearly 50 million people remain under alert for damaging winds, large hail, and tornadoes across an extensive area from the Mississippi Valley to the lower Ohio Valley, with meteorologists warning that Monday's supercells could generate violent EF-3 or higher tornadoes and baseball-sized hail.[11][1][3]

## Colombia Bus Bombing

A devastating bomb attack on a bus traveling along the Pan-American Highway in Colombia's Cauca department killed at least 19 people and injured 38 others on Saturday, April 25. The death toll initially reported as 13-14 rose to 19 by Sunday morning according to Colombia's National Institute of Legal Medicine and Forensic Sciences. Among the victims were five children.[12][13][14]

Military officials stated that assailants blocked traffic using vehicles before detonating the explosive device, creating a significant crater in the road and overturning multiple buses and vans. President Gustavo Petro linked the attack to drug trafficking networks controlled by FARC dissident leader "Iván Mordisco" and offered a million-dollar reward for the capture of group leaders. The violence comes ahead of Colombia's presidential election next month.[13][14][12]
