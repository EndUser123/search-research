## The Sweet Spot for HDD Storage in Canada

Based on current Canadian pricing data, **the optimal value lies in the 8–12 TB range for internal drives and 20–28 TB for external drives**, depending on your use case and whether you need immediate access.

### Price-Per-TB Analysis[1]

The **best overall price per TB** in Canada is approximately **CA$14.54–CA$15.96/TB**, found in **24–28 TB external Seagate Expansion drives**. These deliver the lowest cost per terabyte available in the market.[1]

**Top value options by capacity:**

- **8 TB internal**: CA$23.75–26.87/TB (Seagate BarraCuda or WD Elements)[1]
- **12 TB internal**: CA$24.58–34.12/TB (WD Blue or WD Red Plus/Pro for NAS)[1]
- **24 TB external**: CA$14.54/TB (Seagate Expansion)[1]

### The Practical Sweet Spot

For most users seeking balance between **affordability, accessibility, and capacity**, the **8–12 TB internal drives** represent the practical inflection point. Here's why:

**8 TB sweet spot** — These drives hit approximately **CA$23–27/TB** and offer strong value without the enterprise premium. They're ideal if you need genuine desktop performance, gaming, or working storage.

**12 TB sweet spot** — At **CA$25–34/TB**, 12 TB drives are where NAS and archival storage becomes economically sensible. The jump from 8 TB to 12 TB adds 50% more capacity for roughly 30–40% more cost—a favorable ratio.

### When to Consider Larger Capacities

If you're building a **data hoarding setup or bulk archival**, the **20–28 TB external Seagate Expansion units are unbeatable at CA$14.54–16.25/TB**. These use enterprise-class helium-filled mechanisms and cost roughly **40% less per TB** than consumer 8–12 TB drives. However, they're external drives (USB 3.0) and less suitable for direct system integration.[1]

### Key Takeaway

**For typical users**: Aim for **8 TB or 12 TB internal drives** at **CA$23–27/TB**. **For bulk/backup storage**: Grab **24 TB Seagate Expansion externals** at **CA$14.54/TB**. Anything larger than 12 TB (internal) or smaller than 8 TB typically offers worse value per terabyte in today's Canadian market.
