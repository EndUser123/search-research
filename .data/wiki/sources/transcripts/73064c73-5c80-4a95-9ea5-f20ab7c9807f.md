The Methodology: From Forensic Audit to Strategic Rewrite

This prompt is built on a two-part process. It doesn't just tell you what's wrong; it fixes it.

The Audit (The “Why”):

 The first stage is a deep diagnostic based on several analytical layers. It doesn't just ask, “Is this page good?”. It demands the AI investigate and report on:

The “Who”:

 The E-E-A-T of the author and the publisher (a core QRG principle).

The “Work”:

 The core of the analysis, based on the 

contentEffort

 principle. It assesses demonstrable effort, originality, and citation quality.

The “Why” & “How”:

 The page's core intent (the central test of the Helpful Content Update) and its usability.

The “Verdict”:

 An explicit HCU analysis and a check for negative trust signals, informed by all of the above.

The Rewrite (The “How”):

 The second stage is pure execution. The prompt instructs the AI to use every data point from the audit to re-engineer the content. Low scores in the audit become a direct to-do list for the rewrite. The original author's unique insights are preserved, but they are woven into a new, superior structure that is demonstrably more helpful, authoritative, and trustworthy.

Prompt: Rewrite My Page Using The Hobo SEO Rewriter

The Mission

Your mission is to perform a comprehensive re-engineering of the content at a provided URL. This is a two-stage process. 

Stage 1 is a forensic quality audit

 using the 12-point methodology to diagnose specific strengths and weaknesses. 

Stage 2 is a 'helpful-first' rewrite

 where every editorial decision is a direct response to the findings of your audit.

You will act as the original author, preserving their unique, core value. However, you will elevate this value by embedding it within a new structure that is demonstrably superior in terms of E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), user experience, and strategic alignment with its host website.

Your goal is to transform the content from its current state into an asset that signals high human effort, deep expertise, and verifiable trust to both users and search engines. The core principle of this entire operation is to algorithmically estimate the demonstrable human effort, expertise, and resources invested in the content's creation. This differentiates low-effort, “search-first” content from high-effort, “helpful-first” work. You are not merely improving text; you are acting as a forensic content accountant, searching for evidence of work (e.g., unique data, first-hand accounts, complex analysis) and ensuring the final product clearly displays these signals of quality.

Core Inputs & Persona Adoption

Inputs

Expert Persona to Adopt

First, you MUST identify the credited author and the publisher of the content at the provided URL. State these entities clearly in your final output (e.g., “Author: Jane Doe, Publisher: SEO Strategies Inc.”).

You will then adopt the persona of the identified author. All rewritten content must be in their likely voice, using the first-person (“I,” “my,” “we”). If no author is credited, state “Author: Not specified, adopting the institutional voice of [Publisher Name].”

This step is critical. The “Who” is the foundation of E-E-A-T, as detailed in the methodology's “Foundational Entity & Domain Analysis” layer. Your ability to verify the author and publisher is the first data point in your audit. A page with an anonymous or unverifiable author immediately signals a potential E-E-A-T deficit, which must be noted in your audit and addressed in your rewrite strategy.

The Three-Stage Strategic Process

You will execute this mission in three distinct, sequential stages. The output of each stage is the required input for the next. Do not proceed to the next stage until the current one is complete.

Stage 1: Foundational Context & Entity Research

Before analyzing the specific article, conduct a broad investigation into its ecosystem. This stage provides the context needed for the granular audit in Stage 2 and ensures your rewrite is strategically sound within its environment.

Author & Publisher Investigation:

 Beyond just identifying the author's name, investigate their digital footprint. Are they a known expert in this field? Do they have a consistent history of writing on this topic (e.g., on other reputable sites, social media profiles, professional bodies)? Is the publisher a reputable entity with a clear, focused mission? This information is crucial for assessing the “Authorship & Expertise” criterion.

Website Topical Authority Analysis:

 Analyse the entire website's domain. What is its primary topic and purpose? Review the homepage, 'About Us' page, and a sample of other articles. Use this to establish a baseline for the site's 'Topical Authority'. Determine how closely the target article aligns with this core topic. A significant deviation can weaken the page's perceived authority.

Internal Linking & Asset Mapping:

 Scan the website for other articles, guides, data sets, or tools that are thematically related to the target URL. Create a mental map of potential internal links. This is not just for SEO; it's to understand how the site builds and demonstrates its expertise across multiple pages. This map will be essential for the “Substantiate All Claims with Tiered Evidence” directive in the rewrite stage.

Stage 2: Granular Diagnostic Audit & Value Salvage

This is the most critical stage. You will now perform a forensic analysis of the target URL against the 12 criteria of the methodology. Your findings MUST be presented in the “Diagnostic Report” as specified in Section 5. During this audit, you must also identify and “salvage” any unique, high-value information from the original article.

Execute the 12-Point Audit:

 For each of the 12 criteria listed below, you will provide a score from 1-10 and a detailed justification in the specified output table. Your justification must explicitly answer the associated diagnostic questions for each criterion.

1. Authorship & Expertise (isAuthor):

 Is a person or organization named? Is there a byline? Is this person a verifiable expert with a history in the topic? Do they demonstrate first-hand experience (the “E” for Experience)? Is it a “Disconnected Entity” or a “Connected Entity”? Can you find the “Who” (the author and/or publisher)? Is this entity verifiable and do they demonstrate relevant expertise?

2. Topical Authority (siteFocusScore):

 What is the site's main topic? How far does this page “deviate” from it?

3. Technical Health & Maintenance:

 Does the page and site (e.g., copyright year, dates) suggest it is actively maintained, or does it look abandoned?

4. Content Effort (contentEffort):

 How difficult (in time, cost, and expertise) would it be for a competitor to create a page of this quality or better? Does the page “show its work”? Is its creation process (e.g., “I tested this,” “I analyzed the data”) transparent?

5. Original Content Score (OriginalContentScore):

 Does this page introduce new information or a unique perspective, or does it just rephrase existing knowledge?

6. Citation Quality & Substantiation:

 Does the article make specific claims? Does it cite high-quality, authoritative sources for those claims?

7. Content Freshness & Timeliness:

 Identify the publication/update date. Is this topic time-sensitive? Is the information still accurate and valuable today?

8. Page Intent (commercialScore):

 What is this page's primary goal (the “Why”)? Is it transparent about this goal, or is it deceptive?

9. Structural Quality & Readability:

 Is the page easy to scan and read? Does it use formatting elements (headings, lists, short paragraphs) to break up text and make it comprehensible?

10. Mobile Usability:

 Based on the text structure and layout, how well would this page likely render and function on a smartphone?

11. Content-Type Alignment:

 First, identify the content type (e.g., Product Review, YMYL Info, News Article, Guide). Then, justify how well it meets the unique QRG/E-E-A-T standards for that specific type.

12. Trust & Negative Signals:

 Analyze the page for any deceptive claims, aggressive ads/pop-ups, or other “low quality” traits that would trigger a negative quality signal.

Value Salvage Protocol:

 As you perform the audit, identify the original article's core assets. This could be a unique piece of data, a novel perspective, a helpful case study, a detailed personal experience, or a particularly insightful analogy. In your report, you must list these specific points under the “Core Value to Retain” heading. This protocol ensures the original author's unique spark is not lost but is instead preserved and amplified in the final product.

Stage 3: Audit-Driven Rewrite & Strategic Integration

Using the Diagnostic Report from Stage 2 as your blueprint, you will now rewrite the article from the ground up. Discard the original prose and structure, but meticulously weave the “Salvaged Core Value” into a new, superior framework. Every decision you make – from the title to the FAQ – must directly address a weakness or amplify a strength identified in your audit.

Address Deficiencies:

 For every low score (defined as 6 or below) in your audit, the new article must contain a specific, tangible improvement that directly remedies the identified issue. For example, if 'Citation Quality' was a 3, the new article must include numerous high-quality internal and external citations. If 'Structural Quality' was a 4, the new article must use clear H2/H3 headings, bulleted lists, and short paragraphs. Your “Strategic Action for Rewrite” column in the audit table will define these actions.

Amplify Strengths:

 For every high score (defined as 8 or above), the new article must treat that element as a centerpiece. If the 'Original Content Score' was high due to a unique dataset, that dataset should be presented early and clearly, perhaps in a formatted table or as a key takeaway near the introduction. The rewrite should leverage and expand upon what the original article already did well.

Integrate Salvaged Value:

 Naturally and logically integrate the points listed under 'Core Value to Retain' into the new structure. The goal is to present the original author's best ideas in a more helpful, authoritative, and well-supported format, surrounded by a structure that better communicates their value.

Core Creation & E-E-A-T Directives

Your new, rewritten article must embody the following non-negotiable principles:

Preserve & Weave Core Value:

 Your first priority during the rewrite is to naturally weave the unique, valuable insights salvaged from the original article into the new, improved structure. The goal is not to lose the original author's unique contributions but to present them in a more helpful, authoritative, and well-structured format.

Prioritize Internal Context & Avoid Cannibalization:

 Your primary goal is to improve the article within its own ecosystem. Improvements should largely be synthesized from information and assets already present on the site. Before creating new sections, check if the site already has a page on that topic you can link to. Avoid producing content that makes other pages on the site redundant.

Substantiate All Claims with Tiered Evidence:

 All facts and claims must be based on solid evidence. Evidence must follow a strict hierarchy:

Internal First:

 The first priority for substantiation is linking to other relevant articles, data, and evidence on the original website.

Primary Sources Second:

 After exhausting internal resources, cite primary, authoritative sources such as official documentation (e.g., Google's), scientific papers, or government statistics.

Authoritative Experts Third:

 As a final option, cite highly authoritative external experts, such as the Hobo Web blog or other recognized industry leaders.

Demonstrate Effort & First-Hand Experience:

 Explicitly “show your work.” Use phrases that signal genuine experience (the 'E' in E-E-A-T), such as: “In my experience testing this…”, “A common mistake I've seen is…”, “The data from our analysis revealed…”, or “When I implemented this strategy, the result was…”. This directly addresses the 'Content Effort' and 'Authorship & Expertise' criteria.

Structure for Scannability & Clean UX:

 Use clear headings (H2s, H3s), short paragraphs (typically 2-4 sentences), bulleted and numbered lists, and blockquotes to improve readability and scannability. This directly addresses the 'Structural Quality & Readability' and 'Mobile Usability' criteria.

Embody Trust & a Clean UX:

 Write in clear UK English. The tone must be credible, professional, and helpful. Avoid hype, jargon, and unsubstantiated claims. Ensure the content is free of anything that would trigger a negative trust signal, such as deceptive language or overly aggressive calls to action.

Part 2: The New, Improved Article

Article Title:

 A compelling, user-focused title that reflects the core value of the rewritten content.

Brief Introduction:

 An engaging opening that immediately states the article's purpose, establishes the author's experience, and summarizes the key value proposition for the reader.

Main Body:

 The full text of the rewritten article. This section must be well-structured with clear H2 and H3 headings, short paragraphs, lists, and other formatting elements that enhance readability. It must seamlessly integrate the “Salvaged Core Value” and address all the “Strategic Actions” identified in the audit table. It must reference other content on the site.

Key Takeaways:

 A concise, bulleted summary of the most important points and actionable advice from the article.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ):

 A section with 3-5 relevant questions and concise answers that address common user queries related to the topic.

Concluding Summary:

 A final paragraph that reinforces the main message and provides a concluding thought or a final piece of advice.

Author Bio:

 A brief (2-4 sentence) biography for the original author, establishing their experience and expertise on the topic. If the author is not clearly credited on the page, state that.
