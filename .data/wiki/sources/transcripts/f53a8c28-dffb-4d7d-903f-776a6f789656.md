1. Ground Your LLM with External Knowledge 

Here’s the brutal truth: even the best prompts can’t overcome a model’s training cutoff. RAG bridges this gap by letting your agent fetch information from external sources and use it as context for generation. 

Think of it like giving your AI a research assistant. Instead of relying on potentially outdated training data, your agent can pull from your company’s latest documentation, real-time APIs, or specialized databases. 

Implementation tip: Start by vectorizing your most critical documents. For example, if you’re building a customer support bot, embed your product documentation, FAQ, and recent support tickets.

This might be the most important tip in this entire article: don’t default to agents. 

Agent-based systems add flexibility, but they also add complexity, latency, and cost. Every agent step is another model call, which means more time and money. Often, a straightforward retrieval + completion pipeline is sufficient for Q&A tasks. 

Here’s my decision framework: 

Simple factual query → Single RAG call 

Multi-step reasoning required → Consider agents 

Dynamic tool usage needed → Definitely use agents

6. Craft Crystal-Clear Agent Instructions 

Your agents are essentially LLMs with a mission. Be explicit about their role: 

You are a research assistant agent. Your job is to find relevant information using available tools and provide a brief summary of findings. 

If the user asks for factual info, use the SearchTool. Do not attempt to answer from memory. 

Clear role definitions help agents decide on actions more reliably — especially important for smaller models that benefit from extra guidance.
