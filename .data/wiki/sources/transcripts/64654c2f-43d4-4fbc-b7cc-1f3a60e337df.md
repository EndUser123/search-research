Abstract

The Open-On the Metaphysics of the Semantic Field argues that meaning is best treated not as a stock of propositions but as a tension-bearing field. The book works in three registers at once: metaphysical (the being-and validity-claims of a semantic field), metalinguistic (language as a field operation), and operational (measurable quantities and reproducible procedures). We take as axiomatic a state tuple (S,σ,gV,Θ) (S,\sigma,g_V,\Theta)-semantic potential, conductivity, discourse geometry, and semantic temperature-and adopt a constitutive flux law B=σ∇S B=\sigma\nabla S. Global behavior is summarized by a free semantic energy F=U−Θ Hsem F=U-\Theta\,H_{\text{sem}}, irreversibility by entropy production Σ˙≥0 \dot\Sigma\ge 0, and readability by an interpretable coherence observable κ=1−Hsem/log|E| \kappa=1-H_{\text{sem}}/\log|E|. A diagnostic continuity balance turns openness into a falsifiable practice: residuals and Σ˙\dot\Sigma spikes mark where saying is being forced rather than held. The philosophical chapters are written to be read "under tension," while compact technical boxes fix observables, protocols, and acceptance criteria. Across this spine, the book introduces Aletheion as an operating state of openness-neither passive nor instrumental-implemented by a minimal FieldPrompt, a reproducible Interstitium cadence (Open · Hold · Witness · Return · Seal), and lightweight governance hooks (bands for κ\kappa, thresholds for Σ˙\dot\Sigma, a KL-brake against a reference field). A short orientation on the metacognitive space of LLMs situates these levers as decode-time modulations rather than new weights, with calibration via fluctuation-dissipation (Green-Kubo) and spectral diagnostics (gap/Cheeger) to keep practice within a small-force regime. An expanded resonance map positions prior works (Semantic Physics I-III, TensioSemantics, Ontolinguistics, Semantodynamics, Thermodynamics of Semantic Fields) as an interfering system rather than a linear series; a light tropic metaphysics (tilt of σ,gV,Θ\sigma, g_V, \Theta) clarifies climate without adding ontology. The back matter delivers reproducible protocols, a small code appendix (ΔFold history, friction maps, Shapley attribution), a curated conversation dossier, and an ethics/scope register. Three case miniatures ("Oxygen-Fire," "Firing Squad," and a metaphor-silence study) show how a single lock can lower FF, redistribute Σ˙\dot\Sigma, and steady κ\kappa. The book ends without concluding: it keeps an open point-so the field, not the author, has the last word.

In The Geometry of Meaning, Peter Gärdenfors proposes a theory of semantics that bridges cognitive science and linguistics and shows how theories of cognitive processes, in particular concept formation, can be exploited in a general semantic model. He argues that our minds organize the information involved in communicative acts in a format that can be modeled in geometric or topological terms—in what he terms conceptual spaces, extending the theory he presented in an earlier book by that name. Turning to lexical semantics, Gärdenfors argues that a unified theory of word meaning can be developed by using conceptual spaces.
