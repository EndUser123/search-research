# Optimising Agentic Velocity through Engineering Grade Deep Research
To get better, actionable information for agentic tools, your deep research prompt must move past surface-level definitions of risk and generic prompt engineering


. Instead, it needs to force the research to analyze the specific friction points between "forced thoroughness" (uncompromising context) and "execution velocity" (the sub-2-minute loop) within an autonomous agent's ecosystem


.


Here is an engineering-grade deep research prompt you can use to yield actionable configurations, rule updates, and skill refinements


:


"Analyze the friction points between forced thoroughness and execution velocity within autonomous agent ecosystems like Claude Code. Do not provide high-level, generic advice. Instead, deliver engineering-grade insights by doing the following: 1. Expose Anti-Patterns: Analyze specific prompt anti-patterns, such as why 'fancy looking' code-fence layouts often act as execution traps for the agent.** 2. Focus on Codebase State: Output concrete, actionable configuration lines that can be dropped directly into project files (like CLAUDE.md) to control the agent's default behavior.** 3. Isolate the 'Falsification' Concept: Detail methodologies for how the agent can systematically prove a mitigation wrong before shipping it, allowing this rigorous logic to be scaled across its other skills."**


Why this prompt is effective:


It requires concrete codebase state changes (like CLAUDE.md edits) rather than useless high-level advice


.


It specifically targets anti-patterns, forcing the research to explain why certain formatting choices fail


.


It isolates the novel falsification concept, giving you a scalable way to improve the agent's logic


.
