# The Great AI Reality Check Has Begun
in 2023 AI was supposed to replace programmers in 2024 AI was supposed to replace customer support in 2025 executives were talking about replacing entire departments and now many of those same companies are quietly rehiring people they let go not because AI is useless but because they made a mistake so common so predictable and so expensive that it has a name the doorman fallacy and if you're a developer who's been skeptical of the AI hype from day one this might explain why so many AI replacement projects are starting to hit a wall for the last few years we've been told that large language models are becoming smarter cheaper and more capable every benchmark looked better than the last higher scores more tokens more code generated more tasks completed the assumption was simple if AI can do the visible part of your job then eventually it can do the whole job sounds reasonable until you look at what actually happened when companies tried it because that's where the story starts falling apart there's a concept from Rory Southerntherland called the doorman fallacy imagine a luxury hotel the manager notices they're paying someone $30,000 a year to stand at the entrance and open doors from a spreadsheet perspective that's ridiculous why not install automatic doors and save the money so they do problem solved except 6 months later customer satisfaction drops guests stop coming back revenue starts declining what happened the doorman wasn't just opening doors he welcomed guests he recognized regular customers he provided security he helped with luggage he made the hotel feel premium the visible task was opening doors the actual value was everything else and that's exactly what many companies are doing with AI they're looking at jobs and reducing them to their most obvious output customer service becomes answering questions programming becomes writing code management becomes sending emails design becomes creating graphics then they ask if AI can do that part why do we still need people that's the fallacy because the visible output is often the least valuable part of the job let's talk about software development most non-technical executives think programmers get paid to write code but every experienced developer knows that's not true writing code is often the easiest part the hard part is understanding requirements making trade-offs designing architecture debugging weird edge cases predicting future problems maintaining systems for years making decisions under uncertainty in other words the hard part is thinking and that's where the current generation of LLM starts showing its limitations ai can generate code incredibly fast but generating code isn't the same thing as understanding a system it isn't the same thing as owning the consequences of decisions it isn't the same thing as knowing why one architecture choice will become a disaster 2 years from now this is why we're seeing an interesting pattern the more experienced the developer the less impressed they tend to be by raw code generation not because the technology is bad but because they know how much of the job exists outside the editor the code is just the final artifact the real work happened before the first line was ever written and we're seeing similar problems outside software companies that rushed to replace customer service teams with AI discovered customers hated the experience fast food chains rolled out AI ordering systems only to run into public failures and embarrassing mistakes some organizations that aggressively cut staff are now reversing course and bringing people back not because AI failed completely but because they optimized for the visible task and ignored everything surrounding it they removed the doorman then discovered the doorman was doing far more than opening doors so when people say AI writes code now or AI answers customer questions now they're often measuring the wrong thing the question isn't whether AI can perform a task the question is whether it can replace all the invisible value surrounding that task
