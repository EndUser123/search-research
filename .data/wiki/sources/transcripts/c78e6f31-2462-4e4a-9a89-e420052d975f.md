Post-training alignment methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) often lead to a reduction in output diversity, a phenomenon termed mode collapse. Unlike prior work that primarily attributes this to algorithmic limitations, this paper identifies a fundamental, pervasive data-level driver: 

typicality bias

 in human preference data. Annotators systematically favor familiar, fluent, and predictable text, a tendency rooted in cognitive psychology principles such as the mere-exposure effect, availability heuristic, processing fluency, and schema congruity theory.

In scenarios where true task utility is approximately flat for a set of responses, typicality bias acts as a tie-breaker, causing the aligned model's output to collapse to the mode of the base model.

Motivated by this analysis, the paper introduces 

Verbalized Sampling (VS)

, a simple, training-free prompting strategy to circumvent mode collapse and recover the inherent diversity of the pre-trained base model. The core insight is that different prompt types collapse to different modes:

Instance-level prompt

 (e.g., "Tell me a joke about coffee"): Collapses to the single most likely instance (mode joke) of the base model.

List-level prompt

 (e.g., "Tell me 5 jokes about coffee"): Collapses to a uniform distribution over a list of related items.

Distribution-level prompt (VS)

 (e.g., "Generate 5 jokes about coffee and their corresponding probabilities"): The model is explicitly prompted to verbalize a probability distribution over multiple candidate responses. This enables the model to approximate the underlying distribution of related items learned during pretraining, thus recovering diversity.

The paper categorizes VS into three variants: VS-Standard, VS-CoT (incorporating Chain-of-Thought), and VS-Multi (multi-turn generation).

Comprehensive experiments were conducted across various LLMs (GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4-Sonnet, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, etc.) and tasks:

Creative Writing (poems, stories, jokes):

 VS significantly improved semantic diversity by over direct prompting, while maintaining or improving output quality. For instance, in creative writing, VS recovered 66.8% of the base model's diversity after DPO alignment, compared to 23.8% for direct prompting. A human study confirmed higher diversity scores for VS. Larger, more capable models exhibited greater diversity and quality gains with VS. VS also allowed tunable diversity by adjusting a verbalized probability threshold in the prompt.

Dialogue Simulation (donation persuasion):

 VS generated donation amount distributions that were more aligned with human behavior (lower Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistics and L1 distance) and produced more diverse linguistic styles (higher Distinct-N and semantic diversity) compared to direct prompting, with some models performing on par with a dedicated fine-tuned model.

Open-Ended QA (e.g., "Name a US state"):

 VS achieved lower KL divergence between the generated and pretraining distributions, and higher Coverage-N (broader range of correct answers) without sacrificing precision (factual accuracy).

Synthetic Data Generation (competition math questions):

 Synthetic data generated using VS methods improved downstream math task performance across multiple fine-tuned models and benchmarks (MATH500, OlympiadBench, Minerva Math). For example, VS-Multi achieved an average accuracy of 37.5%, compared to 30.6% for direct prompting and a 32.8% baseline.

Ablation Studies:

 Showed that VS effectively mitigates mode collapse across post-training stages (SFT, DPO, RLVR) and is orthogonal to temperature scaling and other decoding strategies, allowing for combined benefits.

In summary, this work provides a novel data-centric perspective on mode collapse, identifying typicality bias in human preference data as its root cause. Verbalized Sampling offers a practical, training-free inference-time remedy that leverages the model's capacity to verbalize probability distributions, unlocking the inherent generative diversity of pre-trained LLMs without compromising factual accuracy or safety.
