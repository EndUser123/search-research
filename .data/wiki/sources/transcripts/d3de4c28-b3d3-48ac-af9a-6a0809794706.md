Abstract

Semantic video scene-understanding applications rely on object-camera motion recognition techniques for scene contextual movement representation. While existing machine learning-based methods perform efficiently, their primary limitation is to analyze motion patterns from normal frames only, neglecting the scene transition frames. This causes significant false alarms due to the undetected object-camera motion patterns during scene transitions. In this paper, we propose a novel method for object and camera motion recognition of two consecutive scenes from their transition frames. First, our method detects cut transitions using principal component analysis (PCA) to segment the video into shots. Additionally, it eliminates large text transitions that are often falsely detected as cut transitions using structural similarity index measurement (SSIM) properties. Second, it selects candidate segments to localize normal and wipe transition frames using slope angle characteristics obtained from linear regression. Third, it extracts dense semantic spatial features at multi-scale using the modified DeepLabv3+ network to segment selected candidate frames into foreground, background, and wipe pixels. Finally, an optical flow algorithm-based temporal trajectory tracking model is applied on each segmented pixel to recognize the object, camera pan, zoom-in, and zoom-out motion patterns. We further remove falsely detected non-transition motion frames to improve wipe transition detection. The experimental results are obtained using the benchmark TRECVID and the multimedia datasets. The proposed method using pixel-level classification and temporal trajectory analysis achieved an average accuracy improvement of 9.28% for object-camera motion recognition, 3.75% for cut transition detection, and 3.01% for wipe transition detection.

ABSTRACT Semantic video scene-understanding applications rely on object-camera motion recognition

techniques for scene contextual movement representation. While existing machine learning-based methods

perform efﬁciently, their primary limitation is to analyze motion patterns from normal frames only,

neglecting the scene transition frames. This causes signiﬁcant false alarms due to the undetected object-

camera motion patterns during scene transitions. In this paper, we propose a novel method for object and

camera motion recognition of two consecutive scenes from their transition frames. First, our method detects

cut transitions using principal component analysis (PCA) to segment the video into shots. Additionally, it

eliminates large text transitions that are often falsely detected as cut transitions using structural similarity

index measurement (SSIM) properties. Second, it selects candidate segments to localize normal and wipe

transition frames using slope angle characteristics obtained from linear regression. Third, it extracts dense

semantic spatial features at multi-scale using the modiﬁed DeepLabv3+ network to segment selected

candidate frames into foreground, background, and wipe pixels. Finally, an optical ﬂow algorithm-based

temporal trajectory tracking model is applied on each segmented pixel to recognize the object, camera pan,

zoom-in, and zoom-out motion patterns. W e further remove falsely detected non-transition motion frames

to improve wipe transition detection. The experimental results are obtained using the benchmark TRECVID

and the multimedia datasets. The proposed method using pixel-level classiﬁcation and temporal trajectory

analysis achieved an average accurac y improvement of 9.28% for object-camera motion recognition, 3.75%

for cut transition detection, and 3.01% for wipe transition detection.

The most common gradual transition patterns investigated

in existing research include the dissolve transition, where

one scene gradually dissolves into the next scene, and the

fade-in or fade-out transition, where one scene fades in or

fades out onto the next scene. In the wipe transition, one

transition detection tasks has been a focus of research for

decades, aiming to enhance the efﬁciency of semantic video

analysis, video indexing, and video retrieval applications.

based model that combined a high-level fuzzy petri net

(HLFPN) with histogram feature difference model to pre-

shots and gradual transitions. This method returned 86.5%

precision and 72.39% recall rates, however, gradual transition

patterns were not classiﬁed by this method. Later, the TSSBD

method was proposed in [9] that used spatial and tempo-

ral features extracted by 3D conv olutional neural networks

(C3D) to classify abrupt and gradual transition patterns. This

method achieved 93.2% precision and 93.8% recall rates.

T o reduce the computation time, Idan et al. [10] proposed

separable moments and support vector machine (SVM) based

method that applied candidate key frame selection strategies

to achieve a fast shot classiﬁcation approach. This method

We improve the cut transition detection method for

facilitating the segmentation of wipe pattern pixels, as

well as the foreground and background pixels of both

"Scene 1" and "Scene 2".

We dev elop an optical ﬂow algorithm-based motion

trajectory analysis model at the temporal dimension

to track motion vector ﬂow directions to recognize 14

types of movement patterns, including wipe transition

and object-camera motions.

We ev aluate the proposed SOCMR method on the

TRECVID benchmark and the multimedia dataset that

contains videos from recent years with unique cam-

era movements and effects. W e achieve state-of-the-

art performance for object-camera motion recognition

during scene transitions and wipe transition detection

in complex motion.

II. RELATED WORKS

W e introduce pixel-level spatial feature extraction and tem-

poral trajectory analysis for recognizing object and camera

movements from wipe transition frames. This novel approach

captures the motion semantics of two consecutive scenes

while classifying the wipe transition patterns. The exist-

ing state-of-the-art performance is either speciﬁc to object

and camera motion recognition methods from non-transition

frames, or only scene transition (cut, gradual: wipe, dissolve,

fade-in/out, etc.) detection of a video. Therefore, we provide

a brief overview of existing methodologies and their high-

lights in two subsections: Subsection II-A presents the state-

of-the-art methods for object and camera motion recognition,

while Subsection II-B explains the state-of-the-art methods

for scene transition detection.

A. OBJECT AND CAMERA MOTION FEATURE-B ASED

APPROA CH

Recognition of camera motion patterns along with the object

movements is a challenging task due to its diverse motion

vector ﬂow directions [14]. While camera motion patterns

carry the temporal global features of a scene's content, object

movements conv ey the local features.

CAMERA MOVEMENT DETECTION MODEL

T o improve shot boundary detection, Lan et al. proposed an

integral template matching strategy based on quantitative and

qualitative analysis using pixel luminance features to detect

camera movements on low-quality videos [15]. Optical ﬂow-

based algorithms for generating motion semantic descriptors

drew the attention of many researchers over the years due

to their computational efﬁciency [16],[17],[18]. Apart

from optical ﬂow-based methods, several machine learning-

based approaches were proposed to classify camera motions

using their semantic features. In [19], a 3D ResNet CNN

was proposed to extract spatial and temporal features and

use them to ﬁnd foreground and background interest points.

A nonparametric technique of motion vector ﬁeld (MVF)

estimation was proposed in [20]. In this method, a mean

shift algorithm using histogram features was utilized for

motion feature extraction, and then a support vector machine

(SVM) was used for camera motion classiﬁcation. Block

motion vector formation (BMV) using Local T etra Pattern

(L TrP) based method was proposed in [21]. They further

used a dimensionality reduction approach using histogram

orientation to extract motion features, which were then fed

to the C-SVM classiﬁer. Ho wever, this method is limited to

camera zoom-in and zoom-out motion pattern classiﬁcation.

OBJECT MOVEMENT DETECTION MODEL

Several background-modeling approaches were proposed

to track foreground objects from non-stationary videos

or in camera movements. A spatio-temporal feature-based

coherency clues and robust principal component analysis

(RPCA) based low-rank features were applied in [22]. While

this method provided good performance for robust motion

tracking, it was also computationally time-consuming. The

low-rank feature analysis strategy led to miss detections of

certain parts of temporally static moving foreground objects.

T o overcome this limitation, Chen et al. [23] proposed an

improved object tracking method based on long-term spatio-

temporal features from non-stationary videos by adding an

optical ﬂow motion vector computation strategy. This method

effectively captured the characteristics of the motion ﬂo w

patterns. Transformer-based visual object tracking facilitates

in identifying object's shape and position in a frame [75],

[76]. In [24], foreground-background motion and intensity

difference features were introduced to track moving fore-

ground objects in a moving camera. Howev er, this method

exhibited low accuracy in cases where the background was

considerably smaller than the foreground. Sajid and Che-

ung [25] proposed another background modeling approach

based on RGB and YCbCr color spaces for capturing pixel

illumination changes. Although this method returned good

accuracy and robustness in sudden illumination-changing

scenes, the dependence on color features also caused some

miss detections due to poor color discrimination in Cb and

Cr channels. This method further employed binary mask-

ing to segment the foreground object pixels. A fuzzy logic

with Gaussian Mixing Modeling (GMM) based background

subtracting method was proposed in [26]. This method

utilized an optical ﬂow-based motion vector ﬁeld to esti-

mate stronger temporal feature descriptors to detect moving

objects. Optical ﬂow motion vector features-based meth-

ods were considered highly efﬁcient for object tracking in

moving backgrounds by many researchers [27],[28],

[29]. A modiﬁed encoder-decoder-based network model was

proposed for video salient object detection (VSOD) in

[30]. The network was built upon the U-Net architecture but

with a modiﬁed decoder network using 3D CNN layers for

stronger temporal feature extraction. The main limitation of

this method was insufﬁcient temporal information which led

to miss detections of some parts of target objects during long

static periods.

B. SCENE TRANSITION PA TTERN FEATURE-B ASED

APPROA CH

Existing scene transition detection methods primarily focus

on capturing transition pattern features in low-motion activ-

ity scenes, where the transitions are not much affected by

the motion patterns. Some of the popular low-level visual

feature extraction methods are pixel-wise statistical property

comparisons such as mean and standard deviation [31],

[32],[33], edge-based feature structure difference estimation

[7],[35], and histogram-based features for threshold value

computation [36],[37]. A pixel sub-sampling algorithm

was proposed in [38] to construct the visual content of a

scene, also known as visual rhythm. Edge-based methods

were introduced to address the issue of local and global

motion sensitivity in pixel-based algorithms (PB As). His-

togram feature-based methods such as HSV, YCrCb, and

RGB are some of the widely used cost-effective methods that

are also less sensitive to local-global motion activity [6],

[39],[41]. However, histogram-based methods are limited to

high-level motion activity due to their fast color information-

changing nature. The visual feature extraction-based methods

performed well in detecting cut [42] and gradual transitions

[43].

However, this approach caused the problem of miss-

detection in high-motion activity frames such as camera

pan, zoom-in/out, and large object movements. Therefore,

motion activity-based methods were proposed to detect scene

transition patterns in several existing methods. Macroblock

(MB) type temporal information was used speciﬁcally to

detect wipe transition patterns in [44]. The primary limi-

tation of this method was sensitivity to the brightness and

illumination changes. Dominant color feature descriptor-

based motion activity analysis method was proposed in

due to their similar motion ﬂow directions.

After extracting features in the scene transition detection

hierarchy, the ne xt step is to generate the feature discontinuity

or continuity signals to localize the transition frames in

the video. Some of the popular discontinuity signal con-

struction approaches are inter-frame pixel-wise dissimilar-

ity approach [54], temporal dissimilarity approach using

background color-edge feature [55], and multiple feature-

based block dissimilarity approach [49]. Inter-frame con-

tinuity signals are constructed using hue histogram feature

difference (HHD) [56], and cosine similarity using SURF

and histogram features [57]. Euclidean distance calculation

method was proposed in [9] and [58].

The ﬁnal step in scene transition detection involv es clas-

sifying scene transition patterns. W ell-trained machine learn-

ing classiﬁers achieve high accuracy due to their ability to

automatically extract relevant features from a wide range

of transition pattern data. Many research studies leverage

machine learning approaches to enhance scene transition

detection [59],[60]. K-mean clustering-based classiﬁcation

technique was proposed in [61]. K-mean Clustering and

linear discriminant analysis (LDA) using SURF and DCT

ﬁngerprint features were proposed in [40]. Some of the

well-trained CNNs were utilized for low-level and high-le vel

feature extraction in [62] and [63]. However, these proposed

methods investigated a fe w transition patterns only. A self-

supervised CNN-based approach was proposed that treated

audio and visual features for detecting cut transitions [64].

Spatio-temporal features were extracted using deep CNN

in [65]. Usually, deep learning netw orks that are trained

with video data require a large amount of processing time.

Addressing this problem, a candidate frame selection method

was proposed in [66] which processed only fewer frames

into the SVM classiﬁer. Another candidate selection method

was proposed in [67]. While the aforementioned machine

learning-based approaches are excellent for detecting the

semantics of transition patterns, they are insufﬁcient for

capturing the semantics of the transition patterns in presence

of the high-motion activity such as object and camera move-

ments. T o reduce the temporal redundancy in shot, event, and

scene-level boundary detection, T an et al. [34] proposed a

visual transformer encoder-decoder network that applied pre-

trained ResNet-50 as the backbone network. T o further im-

prove the ev aluation accuracy, two-stream inputs using opti-

cal ﬂow and RGB were inﬂated in the proposed model (T em-

poral Receiver) which signiﬁcantly improved the cut tran-

sition detection accuracy, however, this approach achie ved

low accuracy in gradual (fading, logo) transition detection.

In addition, the method did not investigate wipe transition

patterns in object and camera movements. Addressing this

issue, recently, Chakraborty et al. [2] proposed spatial and

optical ﬂow motion vector features-based inﬂated 3D CNN

network that classiﬁed 12 types of wipe transition patterns in

high-motion activity frames. Howe ver, this method suffered a

few misclassiﬁcations in the presence of multiple objects and

camera motion ﬂow in a single transition frame. The error

occurred because of the semantic gap between motion pattern

information and transition pattern information.

In this article, we aim to eliminate the semantic gap

between motion patterns and transition patterns by apply-

ing pixel-level segmentation of fore ground, background, and

wipe ﬁrst. Then, we employ an optical ﬂow-based temporal

trajectory analysis algorithm on each segmented class to

recognize object-camera motions and wipe patterns. There-

fore, some of the best state-of-the-art methods for object-

camera motion recognition and wipe transition detection are

provided in T able 1.

III. PROPOSED SEMANTIC OBJECT-CAMERA MO TION

RECOGNITION FROM VIDEO SCENE TRANSITIONS

This section presents the proposed semantic object-camera

motion recognition (SOCMR) method to identify contextual

motion information during scene transitions, as depicted in

Fig. 2. W e describe the method in four steps. First, we

segment the input video using cut shot boundary detection

with the text elimination approach. This technique is based

on a cost-effective hybrid model that uses Principal Com-

the false detection problem of cut transitions caused by the

method for selecting candidate segments. This approach se-

sliding sequence as the ﬁnal candidates. This step localizes

potential wipe transitions, non-transition frames with object-

camera motion activity, and non-transition frames with low

motion activity. The third step is to perform a semantic

contextual spatial feature-based frame segmentation of the

selected candidates using the DeepLabv3+ classiﬁer. This

step classiﬁes the wipe boundary pixels from the foreground

and background pixels of previous and post scenes. Finally,

A. MODIFIED CUT TRANSITION DETECTION FOR

VIDEO SEGMENT ATION

The main objective of cut transition detection is to segment

a large input video into individual shots. This technique

facilitates the analysis of continuity signals between scene

transitions and object-camera motion transitions within each

shot. As a result, it reduces the chances of misclassiﬁcations

of scene transitions in the presence of large object motion ac-

tivity. The distinct feature that separates cut transitions from

wipe transitions is the frame count in the transition process.

However, because both the cut shot boundary and large text

transitions exhibit a similar abrupt nature, false detections

are often encountered. Considering this, we improve the

conventional cut transition detection method by proposing an

eigenvector feature-based frame continuity signal construc-

tion and SSIM-based frame structure similarity measurement

comparison model to detect and classify cut transitions from

text transitions. Finally, we segment the video on the detected

cut transition points to get shots, accordingly. The implemen-

tation of the proposed method for video segmentation with

cut transition detection and text classiﬁcation is described in

Algorithm 1.

The proposed temporal trajectory analysis algorithm based

on the semantic motion ﬂow patterns of individual objects

and camera movement is limited to pixel intensity sensitivity.

This issue leads to motion vectors ﬂowing in multiple direc-

tions. This often increases the magnitude range ( ϕ) of motion

vector ﬂows in the wrong directions. Also, the proposed

method does not classify titling object motion vector ﬂows

from horizontal and vertical ﬂow directions. Additionally,

the presence of camera pan motion, zoom-in/out motion,

and multiple object movements with different directions in

the same frame generate misclassiﬁcation of directions. This

complexity results in false detections during the foreground

and background temporal trajectory analysis. T o overcome

this issue, we propose to include other spatial features that

complement the motion feature semantic in the future. It

is observed from the comparison tables that the proposed

method returned recall rates for "Zoom-in" and "Zoom-out"

motion patterns, it achieved recall rates for "Camera Pan"

movement in both the horizontal and vertical directions.

The precision rates improved in both the wipe transi-

tion with motion and without motion detection. This shows

the efﬁciency of the proposed foreground-background-wipe

pixel segmentation and their motion semantics comparison

technique. However, the F-1 score of 96.10% achieved by

the state-of-the-art STKP+SVM method is nearly similar to

the score of 96.93% achieved by the proposed method. This

happened because the STKP+SVM method returned a higher

precision but lower recall; whereas our proposed method

returned comparatively lower precision b ut higher recall.

Hence, we suggest to analyse multi-modal 3D temporal and

spatial features to improve the gradual transition detection.

VI. CONCLUSION

The proposed SOCMR method introduces pixel-level se-

mantic feature extraction at spatial dimension to recognize

various object-camera motions and improve the detection

of wipe scene transition in the presence of motions. The

proposed motion trajectory analysis algorithm at the tem-

poral dimension creates a good balance between segmented

spatial features and motion features. Scene transition de-

tection is an important step in the semantic video content

analysis approach, and cut is one of the most commonly

used transitions. Therefore our proposed method also de-

tects cut transitions with low processing time. The improved

maximum prominence value and SSIM features comparison

approach works best to increase the TPF rate in cut transition

detection. Simultaneously, it ef fectively reduces the FPF rate

by eliminating falsely detected text transitions. The proposed

candidate key frame selection method makes the system cost-

effective because it only processes a fe w frames to recognize

the motion of the corresponding scenes and transition pattern.

The simple yet effectively modiﬁed ASPP module in the

existing classiﬁer captures semantic spatial features at a

multi-scale wipe transition data in the encoder, thereby the

sharp object and wipe pattern pixel boundary gets reﬁned

in the decoder model. This enhances the multi-class classiﬁ-

cation task for frame segmentation. The proposed temporal

trajectory analysis approach extracts the motion semantic

patterns of the individual camera and object directions. It

detects four distinct wipe transition patterns and various

object-camera motion patterns, each with its corresponding

direction, resulting in 14 distinct movement types. Evaluation

of object-camera motion recognition and wipe transition pat-

tern detection on the benchmark TRECVID and Multimedia

datasets demonstrate signiﬁcant improvement in accuracy

compared to existing methods. The proposed method can be

extended to recognize the interaction between objects and

their surroundings using pixel-level spatial-motion features.

In the future, we will explore motion feature enhancement

techniques to identify other unique scene transition patterns

in the presence of multiple camera movements.

In the past few years, visual tracking methods with convolution neural networks (CNNs) have gained great popularity and success. However, the convolution operation of CNNs struggles to relate spatially distant information, which limits the discriminative power of trackers. Very recently, several Transformer-assisted tracking approaches have emerged to alleviate the above issue by combining CNNs with Transformers to enhance the feature representation. In contrast to the methods mentioned above, this article explores a pure Transformer-based model with a novel semi-Siamese architecture. Experimental results show that our ATST-based model attains favorable results against state-of-the-art convolutional trackers. Moreover, it shows comparable performance with recent “CNN + Transformer” trackers on various benchmarks while our ATST requires significantly less training data.

Generic Boundary Detection (GBD) aims at locating the general boundaries that divide videos into semantically coherent and taxonomy-free units, and could serve as an important pre-processing step for long-form video understanding. Previous works often separately handle these different types of generic boundaries with specific designs of deep networks from simple CNN to LSTM. Instead, in this paper, we present Temporal Perceiver , a general architecture with Transformer, offering a unified solution to the detection of arbitrary generic boundaries, ranging from shot-level, event-level, to scene-level GBDs. The core design is to introduce a small set of latent feature queries as anchors to compress the redundant video input into a fixed dimension via cross-attention blocks. Thanks to this fixed number of latent units, it greatly reduces the quadratic complexity of attention operation to a linear form of input frames. Specifically, to explicitly leverage the temporal structure of videos, we construct two types of latent feature queries: boundary queries and context queries, which handle the semantic incoherence and coherence accordingly. Moreover, to guide the learning of latent feature queries, we propose an alignment loss on the cross-attention maps to explicitly encourage the boundary queries to attend on the top boundary candidates. Finally, we present a sparse detection head on the compressed representation, and directly output the final boundary detection results without any post-processing module. We test our Temporal Perceiver on a variety of GBD benchmarks. Our method obtains the state-of-the-art results on all benchmarks with RGB single-stream features: SoccerNet-v2 (81.9 percent average-mAP), Kinetics-GEBD (86.0 percent average-f1), TAPOS (73.2 percent average-f1), MovieScenes (51.9 percent AP and 53.1 percent M i o u M_{iou} ) and MovieNet (53.3 percent AP and 53.2 percent M i o u M_{iou} ), demonstrating the generalization ability of our Temporal Perceiver. To further pursue a general GBD model, we combined various tasks to train a class-agnostic Temporal perceiver and evaluate its performance across all benchmarks. Results show that the class-agnostic Perceiver achieves comparable detection accuracy and even better generalization ability compared to dataset-specific Temporal Perceiver.
