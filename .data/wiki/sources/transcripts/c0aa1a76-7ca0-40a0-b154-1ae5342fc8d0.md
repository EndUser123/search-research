Confused About Agents? Start Here.

📍 You're not alone. This is the #1 thing I hear. Question from the community: "I'm still a little confused about agents and how to deploy them. Where do I start?" Here's the simplest path forward. Step 1: Pick ONE task you hate doing. (Answering the same email over and over? Scheduling? Sorting files?) Step 2: Pick ONE tool. Start with Claude CoWork, Perplexity Computer, or Manus Agent ... all are beginner-friendly. Step 3: Give it that one task. Write clear instructions. Run it. See what happens. Step 4: Tweak it. It won't be perfect the first time. That's okay. Fix one thing. Run it again. Step 5: Repeat with the next task. That's it. You don't need to understand everything. You just need to start somewhere. Clarity comes from doing, not studying.

What Can Agents Do That Custom GPTs Can't?

This one trips people up. Let me clear it up fast. Question from the community: "What are agents doing that custom GPTs can't?" Here's the key difference. - A custom GPT is a trained chatbot. - It talks. It answers. It explains. But it doesn't DO anything outside the chat window. An agent acts in the real world. It can: 🔹 Open files on your computer 🔹 Send emails 🔹 Update your CRM 🔹 Search the web 🔹 Run code 🔹 Book appointments 🔹 Connect to other apps Manus Agent is a great example. You give it a task ... like "research my top 5 competitors, perform a SWOT analysis on each, identify one key question that our audience is asking that each competitor is not answering in their online presence, and write a summary." It goes out, searches, reads, writes, and delivers. A CustomGPT can help you think. An agent helps you execute. Both are useful. But they're not the same thing. Type ACTION below if you want a side-by-side comparison!

AI agents are NOT replacing CustomGPTs.

They're actually going to send more people running toward them. Here's why I'm more bullish on CustomGPTs than ever - even as agents get all the attention. The noise around AI agents is real. They're powerful. They can take multi-step actions, work autonomously, and handle complex workflows that were impossible two years ago. But here's what the hype crowd isn't telling you: Most people don't want complex. They want simple. Your clients, your team, your community - the majority of them need a tool that does ONE thing really well, without a learning curve. That's a CustomGPT. It's purpose-built, easy to use, and doesn't require a PhD to hand off to someone on your team. AI agents are creating massive awareness that people need AI specialists. That's the wave. But when those people come looking for help? Most of them are going to want simple, reliable tools - not a complex autonomous system they don't understand and can't control. CustomGPTs ARE those tools. Agents and CustomGPTs aren't competing. They're serving different audiences at different levels of sophistication. And right now, the vast majority of the market lives at the "give me something simple that works" level. So no - I'm not abandoning CustomGPTs. I'm doubling down. The rising tide of agent hype isn't a threat to your CustomGPT business. It's a recruiting tool. What questions do you have about AI agents vs CustomGPTs? Drop them below - I'll try to answer every one.

The most expensive thing you can do with AI is spend six months waiting for a winner👇

GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude 4.6 are now statistically tied at the top of every major AI benchmark. The performance gap between top models on practical business tasks is marginal, not meaningful. The decision you've been postponing has already been made by the technology: there is no clear winner, and there won't be one. A Perfect Prompt: Act as an AI strategy consultant who specializes in helping [type of business, e.g., coaches, consultants, service providers] get real ROI from AI tools. My current situation is [describe your AI usage: which tools you use, how often, for what tasks]. I want to audit my AI stack and identify the one tool I should go deepest on for the next 90 days. Based on what I've described, which tool should I commit to and why? What criteria should drive that decision? Ask me any questions you have. The entrepreneurs getting the most from AI right now are not the ones using the most tools. They are the ones who know one tool deeply: the quirks, the best prompt patterns, the workflow integrations, the shortcuts. That depth of knowledge does not transfer between platforms. It accumulates. Time spent testing tool 4 is time not spent mastering tool 1. A Perfect Prompt: Act as an expert in AI-assisted [your profession, e.g., coaching, copywriting, consulting]. I currently use [AI tool name] for [list your primary use cases]. I want to identify the five most powerful ways to use this specific tool that most people in my field are not using yet. Give me five specific, underused capabilities of [tool name] with a practical example of how I could apply each one in my business. Ask me any questions you have. The instinct to "wait for the best tool" is the same instinct that kept people off social media until 2018, off podcasting until 2021, and off video until it was already crowded. The entrepreneurs who committed early and went deep had a compounding advantage. The same dynamic is playing out in AI adoption right now, today. A Perfect Prompt: Act as a business strategist who works with [type of entrepreneur] on competitive positioning. I run [brief description of your business]. I want to understand where I stand in my market in terms of AI adoption compared to my competitors. Help me assess: What are the five AI capabilities my competitors are most likely already using that I might not be? For each one, what is the competitive risk if I remain behind on this? Ask me any questions you have. When you commit to one AI tool, something changes. You stop asking "which tool should I use for this" and start asking "how do I do this better." That shift from tool selection to skill development is where the real productivity gains live. Pick your tool. The benchmark debate is not your job. A Perfect Prompt: Act as a productivity coach who specializes in AI-assisted work for [your profession]. I have committed to using [AI tool name] as my primary AI platform. I want to build a 30-day skill development plan that takes me from [current level: beginner/intermediate/advanced] to genuinely advanced proficiency. Create a week-by-week plan with specific skills to develop, practice exercises, and a way to measure improvement. Ask me any questions you have. The one action that separates entrepreneurs who talk about AI from entrepreneurs who profit from it is simple: they pick a tool and get good at it. Not perfect. Good. Competence compounds. Pick your tool this week, not next quarter. A Perfect Prompt: Act as an implementation coach for entrepreneurs who want to integrate AI into their business operations. I have chosen [AI tool name] as my primary tool. My business is [brief description]. My main goals for using AI are [list 2-3 goals]. Create a one-week onboarding plan that starts this Monday and ends with me having completed at least one real business task more efficiently using this tool. Include specific daily tasks, what to practice, and what "success" looks like at the end of the week. Ask me any questions you have.

Generalists are common, specialists are wealthy. Here is how to pick a niche and dominate before your competition even wakes up👇

1 - Identify a High-Pain Problem: Focus on a specific problem that costs people time or money. I am looking to transition from a generalist service provider to a specialist. I need to identify high-pain problems in the [INDUSTRY] sector. Provide a list of the top 5 most expensive or time-consuming problems currently facing businesses in this niche. Ask me any questions you have. 2 - Define Your "Anti-Persona": Determine exactly who you do NOT want to work with. Aggressive niching is as much about saying no to the wrong clients as it is about saying yes to the right ones. A Perfect Prompt: Act as a strategic business consultant. I am refining my target market and need to define my anti-persona to ensure I am not wasting resources on low-value leads. Help me create a profile of a "bad fit" client for a specialist in [SPECIFIC SERVICE]. Provide 5 red flags I should look for during a discovery call. Ask me any questions you have. 3 - Create a Unique Mechanism: Do not just offer a service; offer a proprietary process. Giving your method a name makes it a unique asset that clients can only get from you, increasing your perceived value. A Perfect Prompt: Act as a branding expert. Ask me any questions you have. 4 - Build a Case Study Factory: Generalists show portfolios; specialists show results. I need to turn my recent client success into a high-converting case study. The client was in the [NICHE] industry and we achieved [SPECIFIC RESULT]. Draft a structured case study outline that highlights the "Before," the "Process," and the "Measurable After." Ask me any questions you have. 5 - Dominate One Traffic Source: Do not try to be everywhere. Pick the one platform where your niche hangs out and become the most recognizable authority there through consistent, specialized content. A Perfect Prompt: Act as a social media strategist. My target audience is [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE] and I want to dominate [PLATFORM]. Create a 30-day content calendar focused exclusively on establishing me as the go-to expert for [SPECIFIC PROBLEM].
