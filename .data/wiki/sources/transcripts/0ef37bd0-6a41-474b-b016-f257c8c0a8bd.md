Re: Jobs, Attrition & Layoffs in IT companies

I work in tech and also work closely with newest buzz words like AI/Data etc. and regarding layoffs in IT and AIs impact on it there are two sides to it.

On one side, many companies accumulated a lot of bloat during the covid boom and AI is a simply a super convenient bogey man to hide behind. Pre AI, news of mass layoffs were usually associated with negative business sentiments like lower profitability. This meant a mass layoff was an event that companies wanted to avoid at-least publicly.

But today many companies are actually able to present mass layoffs as a sign of their increasing abilities in AI. Contrast the messaging between two statements “XYZ company fires XX% of staff due to lower profit outlook” versus “XYZ companies fires XX% of staff due to increasing efficiencies from AI”.

It's quite obvious that the AI washed layoff message sounds a lot more digestible for the wider market and why companies do it.

On another side, there is definite structural shift in technology where coding tasks are being accelerated thanks to AI tool. Me/my team use AI based coding tools (Claude/Cursor etc) extensively and find it quite a revolution. Fact is a large proportion of any codebase is boilerplate codes which can readily be done by these tools. Areas which need real “intelligence” is quantitatively smaller and devs can focus there. A senior developer with good architectural/integration knowledge can really turbo charge himself with an agentic coding tool. As a result we are hiring less junior developers who otherwise would have been billed from our Indian consultants.

This is going to hugely affect all traditional service based companies in India (the WITCH). These companies built their business model around providing a steady supply of human developers. Going was soo good that they got comfortable being the body shops. They never built any serious capabilities. Huge amounts were spent in stock buybacks boosting the stock price instead of investing in R&D. This represents a huge missed opportunity for Indian IT. Had they put some serious efforts and resources, maybe the WITCH would not have got stuck in the lower to mid end of the value chain and we might have had few tier 1 tech companies from India.

I do this quite a lot for the personal projects I do. Main reason is because I don't have another human to look at the code and also because I don't trust any model completely based on experience. So for any AI generated code(I normally use Claude Sonnet 4.5 with Windsurf as the Agentic AI editor), I look through it to see if it broadly looks ok, then I take the code and needed references and given it to other models with the context and ask if this approach is good enough. I usually try it with the same Claude 4.5 from the Claude UI (it does behave differently), then with Gemini and sometimes with xAI(Grok). Then I look at what all the models are suggesting and if something makes sense I go back to my took and ask to refine based on these inputs (or I do it myself).

One recent example, I was building something to extract data from certain PDF reports. I wanted it as an app and all local processing. Claude went with a pure raw text extraction and then parsing. The challenge with this was that the PDF reports could come in different formats(although the content would be fairly standard) and I had no control over the formats. Claude's idea was to create a structure for parsers, so if we encountered a new format then we create a new parser. Was not feasible.

Went to google Gemini 3 and it's approach was ' Why are you doing this-Google now ships the ML Kit with Android 15 and above and you can also run Google Gemini Nano locally' . So I gave all the links on this to Claude and said come up with a plan based on this which it did.

Still ran into issues due to various other reasons, but the summary is that using models against each other is sometimes good to get more clarity on the way forward.

I have always been a bit sceptical about it because of all the hype about vibe coding and AI replacing software engineers. All the tools and models before claude Opus 4.5 and claude code were mere hypes.

I have been using Opus 4.5 model in the internal AI tool of my company and it performs decently well for a small to medium level difficultly level task. Yes it does require a lot of prompting and one has to have a solid plan in mind before allowing it to make any changes. I have made sure to review the code extensively once AI completes the task, track down all the changes and make sure that I fully understand what it does. This has really shot up my productivity level and I am able to close a lot of JIRA tickets quickly.

It doesn't work well for a complex performance bug in a very large codebase but I am sure the models will become more and more advance in the future.

I believe the current foundation models like the Opus 4.5 have not been trained on very large scale enterprise codebases as it is obviously out of scope for it's training data.

I feel programming has changed for good. It feels more fun to just convert ideas into production code without much effort. For business POV, it makes a lot of sense for companies to push AI assisted coding.

I believe learning to code and understanding computer science fundamentals is more important than ever. One has to just guide the LLMs in a direction to achieve things faster.
