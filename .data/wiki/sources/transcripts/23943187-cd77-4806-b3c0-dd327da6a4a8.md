# Self-reflecting Large Language Models: A Hegelian Dialectical Approach - arXiv
Self-reflecting Large Language Models: A Hegelian Dialectical Approach

Sara Abdali  Can Goksen  Saeed Amizadeh  Kazuhito Koishida 

Abstract

Investigating NLP through a philosophical lens has recently caught researcher’s eyes as it connects computational methods with classical schools of philosophy. This paper introduces a philosophical approach inspired by the  Hegelian Dialectic  for LLMs’  self-reflection , utilizing a self-dialectical approach to emulate internal critiques and then synthesize new ideas by resolving the contradicting points. Moreover, this paper investigates the effect of LLMs’ temperature for generation by establishing a dynamic annealing approach, which promotes the creativity in the early stages and gradually refines it by focusing on the nuances, as well as a fixed temperature strategy for generation. Our proposed approach is examined to determine its ability to generate novel ideas from an initial proposition. Additionally, a Multi Agent Majority Voting (MAMV) strategy is leveraged to assess the validity and novelty of the generated ideas, which proves beneficial in the absence of domain experts. Our experiments show promise in generating new ideas and provide a stepping stone for future research.

2  Related Work

2.1  Self-Reflection in LLMs

In the context of LLMs, self-reflection involves evaluating and refining model outputs through iterative cycles of feedback and adjustment  (Chen et al., 

2024

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib10

; Li et al., 

2024

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib29

) . Inspired by human cognitive processes, this approach aims to enhance the model’s ability to synthesize complex and sometimes conflicting information, leading to more coherent outputs. For instance, Li et al.  (Li et al., 

2024

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib29

)  explore diverse perspectives, summarize the discrepancies, and organize them into a checklist for refinement.Another work  (Madaan et al., 

2023

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib31

)  presents a method to improve the outputs of LLMs through iterative feedback and refinement. Self-reflection allows agents to rapidly learn from trial-and-error, showing significant performance improvements across various tasks  (Shinn et al., 

2023

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib42

) . Additionally, self-reflection enables LLMs to generate, critique, and refine Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)  (Zhang et al., 

2024a

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib53

) . This approach has shown promise in improving the model’s ability to handle nuanced tasks such as translation  (Wang et al., 

2024a

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib47

)  and scientific research  (Smith & Doe, 

2022

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib44

) , promoting a deeper and more nuanced understanding of complex topics.

Self-reflection in its naive form, where the model is simply instructed to reflect on its own answers, can suffer from degeneracy-of-thought, where the agent, after finding a high confidence answer to the user’s query fails to diverge from its answer in the following steps  (Liang et al., 

2024

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib30

; Wang et al., 

2024b

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib48

) . In this study, we aim to tackle this issue by utilizing self-antithesis generation as a means of self-critique and integrating it with the initial thought process in a Hegelian dialectical fashion, thereby promoting the progression of ideas.

2.2  Philosophy Inspired NLP

Researchers have recently examined NLP through a philosophical lens, bridging the gap between computational techniques and classical philosophical debates. For example, recent work by Milliere et al.  (Millière & Buckner, 

2023

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib34

; Milliere & Buckner,

By revisiting these classic debates, researchers seek to deepen their understanding of both artificial and human intelligence, fostering a more holistic approach to NLP.

In a similar vein, philosophical prompting techniques for LLMs take inspiration from classical philosophical methods to enhance their reasoning and cognitive abilities. A prominent example is the Socratic method  (Chang, 

2023

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib9

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib19

) , which structures the prompting process to emulate human cognitive processes. This method involves understanding the context, planning a response, executing the plan, and then reflecting on the outcome to refine future responses.

3.1  Hegelian Dialectical Method

The term 

Dialectic

 denotes a logical debate that involves periodic consideration and resolution of opposing views. In “Hegel’s dialectical method”, proposed by German philosopher George Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel in 1807  (Hegel, 

1807

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib22

, 

2010

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib24

) . According to Plato’s dialectics, if the premises of an argument lead to a contradiction, they must be deemed false, resulting in a rejection without further advancement. In contrast, Hegel’s dialectics views contradictions as essential stages toward achieving higher unity and synthesis, facilitating the ongoing development of ideas  (Hegel, 

1807

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib22

; Popper, 

1940

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib38

; Maybee, 

2020

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib32

)

Note that, Hegel’s dialectic focuses on the evolution of ideas and their contradictions through  self-sublation , which involves the processes of self-negation, self-preservation, and self-elevation, through which an idea or entity resolves its internal contradictions and transforms into a higher state  (Maybee, 

2020

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib32

) . Although Hegel was influenced by Fichte’s work  (Fichte, 

1982

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib17

) , he did not use Fichte’s triadic “Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis (TAS)” terminology in his own philosophical writings  (Maybee, 

2020

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib32

) . However, we adhere to the tradition of Hegel’s followers by employing TAS as a simplified model.

3.2  Proposed Hegelian Self-reflection

Hegelian Dialectic employs an iterative process, allowing the LLM to evaluate an initial idea a.k.a. “ Thesis ” through a generated critique a.k.a. “ Antithesis ” and subsequently generate a new ideas a.k.a. “ Synthesis ”, by incorporating the best elements of the two. This method aims to improve the model’s ability to integrate complex and contradictory information, leading to more coherent and novel ideas.

Additionally, we explore two temperature configurations and their effect on the generated ideas: 1) a dynamic approach by introducing an annealing scheduler for the LLM’s temperature. This ensures high creativity in the early stages of synthesis, gradually reducing it and emphasizing the addition of subtle nuances to the synthesized idea and, 2) a static approach that maintains a constant temperature throughout all iterations.

Broadly speaking, we aim to generate new ideas that meet two key metrics of the generated synthesis: 1)  validity , which confirms that the generated response follows all instructions of synthesis process, and 2)  novelty , which evaluates whether the synthesis is more novel than the thesis and adds new information to it.

To accomplish this, we draw inspiration from Marvin Minsky’s concept known as the “Society of Mind” (SoM)  (Minsky, 

1988

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib35

) , where intelligence is attained through computational modules (e.g., an LLM/VLM) that interact and collaborate with one another to achieve objectives that cannot be accomplished by a single module alone  (Zhuge et al., 

2023

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib57

) .

Measuring validity and Novelty of Synthesis

Drawing inspiration from the “Society of Mind” concept and multi-agent voting, we employ multiple LLMs to assess validity and novelty of the generated synthesis as mentioned earlier.

In particular, we separately prompt three LLMs and ask if the generated response has followed the synthesis generation instructions (validity test), and if the information in the synthesis is more novel than the thesis (novelty test). We then take the majority vote of the LLMs’ opinions. If there is consensus on both validity and novelty, we proceed with the dialectic process; otherwise, we stop at that point. For instance, if a step does not meet our novelty criteria, we do not proceed. In reality, some debates may go back and forth without producing significant novelty until later stages. 4  Prompt Engineering

In this section, we present the prompts used for generating antithesis and synthesis in the proposed self-reflection process, as well as the prompts employed for evaluating validity and novelty in the MAMV process.

4.1  Self-reflection Prompting

Antithesis:

Engaging in self-debating is one way to generate diverse viewpoints. To create an antithesis, we employ an iterative self-debating, Solo Performance Prompting (SPP) strategy. SPP harnesses the model’s theory of mind reasoning abilities by instructing it to “split” into various personas and collaborate on a given prompt through a brainstorming session among these personas  (Wang et al., 

2024b

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib48

) . Our framework requests the model to generate an arbitrary antithesis persona for each iteration of the self-dialectic. The following instructs model to practice SPP:  {mdframed}  [linecolor=red!40, linewidth=1pt, backgroundcolor=yellow!20]  Imagine you are someone X, who has an  orthogonal  OR  diametrically opposed perspective  on the same topic. 

The goal of the antithesis step is to create a counter-argument that contradicts the thesis, and to express it in reference to the thesis’ weaknesses and limitations. Since the thesis is self-consistent, the antithesis then has to generate new points that have not been addressed in the thesis to introduce a contradiction. Meanwhile, it is important to ensure that the antithesis is “self-consistent”, namely all parts of the counter-argument logically follow from its premises and the premises don’t involve any contradictions. We encode these points in the following prompt instructions:

{mdframed}  

[linecolor=red!40, linewidth=1pt, backgroundcolor=yellow!20]  Produce a competing thesis that is  self-consistent , follows an argumentative structure, and contradicts the Thesis.  Include  novel, opinionated perspectives or angles  beyond what has been stated.

This approach ensures we avoid iterating over existing knowledge and establishes a stopping point; other than that, the MAMV does not interfere with the model’s self-reflection and is not part of the synthesis process.

6  Limitations

Deliberate adaptation and interpretation choices in Hegelian dialectic

We formulate Hegel’s dialectic as a 2-step progress, where the flow from thesis to antithesis and then synthesis does not follow any sort of logical necessity as proposed by  (Maybee, 

2020

; Kaufmann, 

1965

; Inoue, 

2014

) . This also means that the perspective from which the antithesis introduces a contradiction is arbitrary, i.e. delegated to the LLM agent. (2) The synthesis always maintains a self-consistent position. In Hegel’s dialectic, statements can remain ambiguous, where they are true from one perspective and false from the other, without requiring a resolution  (Hegel, 

1807

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib22

; Maybee, 

2020

; Inoue, 

2014

https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14917v3#bib.bib25

)  for more details.

Measuring novelty of a text is challenging

as it involves subjective assessment and context-awareness. Novelty is not just about adding new information, it also requires understanding existing literature to ensure that the idea has not been previously explored. Comparing an idea solely based on an initial proposition does not account for depth of existing research. Our novelty prompt has inherent limitations. It does not explicitly define whether altering the strength of an argument is considered novel, even if all points have been previously mentioned. Additionally, it remains unclear whether the model should regard the negation of existing premises as novel, as it involves manipulating existing information rather than introducing new information. In the Hegel’s dialectic, however, we view such negation as novel.

Difficulties in distinguishing creativity from LLM remembering in baseline evaluation

Evaluating new ideas against pre-proposed synthesis is extremely challenging as distinguishing whether a model is merely recalling training information or generating creative ideas is challenging.

Measuring the effect of annealing due to randomness of generation:

We often observed that, a constant  τ 𝜏 \tau italic_τ  either fails to produce novel content when set too low or leads to irrelevant information when set too high. However, due to inherent randomness in the generation, it is not straightforward to generalize this observation. Conducting statistical significance tests over multiple rounds of prompting can help to evaluate this observation more effectively.

Reproducibility of results:

The inherent randomness in the generation process, coupled with constantly evolving nature of LLMs and the lack of control, especially when using black-box models (e.g. GPT family), makes it difficult to reproduce the results which is essential in scientific settings. While white-box models offer better control over outputs, they may not be as powerful as black-box competitors.

Repetition and randomness in API calls

We have occasionally observed identical antithesis and synthesis paragraphs, as well as main points, raising concerns about the extent of randomness and the possible presence of hidden caching mechanisms during these experiments.

Need for domain expert LLMs

Utilizing the extensive knowledge of domain-specific LLM experts can help identify unique contributions and ensure comprehensive coverage of the field, making it more feasible to measure novelty.

8  Concluding Remarks

This paper presents a novel technique for LLMs’ self-reflection, by framing it as a self-dialectical approach using the principles of Hegel’s dialectic. our approach employs an iterative process in which an initial idea (thesis) is evaluated through generated critiques (antithesis), and subsequently refined into a new idea (synthesis) which incorporates the best elements of both. Additionally, we explore the effect of LLMs temperature on the novelty of generated ideas by establishing two configurations: 1) a constant temperature and, 2) a dynamic annealing temperature settings. Our goal is to generate new ideas that satisfy two metrics: validity which measures whether the synthesis process is followed correctly and novelty, which assesses if the synthesis adds new information and is more novel than the thesis. In order to do so, we employ MAMV a multi-agent framework i.e., to collaborate and vote for these metrics.

Our experiments demonstrate that responses varied even with the same hyper-parameters and prompts, resulting in different numbers of steps and novelty scores. Lower temperature settings often led to fewer steps and lower novelty scores, confirming reduced creativity, although not in all instances. High constant synthesis temperatures could yield out-of-topic synthesis or novel ideas. Most innovative synthesis typically occurred early on, becoming more nuanced and stable over time.
