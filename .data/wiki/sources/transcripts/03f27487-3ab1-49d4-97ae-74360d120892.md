Verbalized Sampling in AI Design

Introduction: From Prompt Engineering to Prompt Sampling

Prompt engineering keeps evolving — and the latest breakthrough, according to 

Lance Eliot

 in his 

Forbes

 piece, is called 

Verbalized Sampling (VS)

. The article is titled, “Prompt Engineering Newest Technique Is Verbalized Sampling That Stirs AI To Be Free-Thinking And Improve Your Responses”. This innovative prompting method encourages 

free-thinking AI

 by asking it to verbalize multiple responses 

along with their probabilities

 — essentially, to share its 

inner thoughts

.

👉 

Read the original article on Forbes

https://www.forbes.com/sites/lanceeliot/2025/11/01/prompt-engineering-newest-technique-is-verbalized-sampling-that-stirs-ai-to-be-free-thinking-and-improve-your-responses/?utm_source=chatgpt.com

What Is Verbalized Sampling?

Verbalized Sampling (VS)

 is a technique where you instruct an LLM to:

“Generate

 n * different responses to the same prompt, each with its probability of being generated.”*

This transforms a standard prompt into a 

meta-prompt

, asking the AI to output not one answer but a 

distribution of possible answers

.

Example:

Prompt: Suggest five creative startup ideas for sustainable energy, 
and include the probability that you'd pick each one.


The result?

A spread of options that reveal how the model “thinks” — and helps users explore diverse, creative, or non-obvious ideas.

Why It Matters

Traditional prompt responses often show 

mode collapse

 — AI defaults to “safe,” predictable answers.

That's due to 

typicality bias

 introduced during alignment training, where human evaluators reward familiar, acceptable answers.

VS helps reverse this by:

Increasing output diversity

Encouraging alternative reasoning paths

Revealing creative or less typical options

“You're not retraining the model — you're just

 changing how you ask the question 

.”

Empirical Findings

According to research referenced by Eliot:

VS improved 

output diversity

 by 

1.6× to 2.1×

 compared to normal prompts.

Larger models (like GPT-4-tier LLMs) benefit the most.

The technique enhances creative writing, ideation, and brainstorming tasks.

Despite richer diversity, factual accuracy and safety remain largely intact.

Think of it as turning your AI into a creative collaborator instead of a one-shot answer machine.

Implications for AI Practice

For Prompt Engineers:

VS reframes prompt design — it's not about 

finding one perfect prompt

, but 

designing prompts that reveal multiple possible truths.

For Researchers:

VS provides a 

training-free

 tool to test internal model diversity and understand hidden probability distributions.

For Professionals:

Writers, designers, and data scientists can leverage VS for:

Creative ideation

Dialogue simulations

Synthetic data generation

Exploratory reasoning tasks

Limitations & Caveats

Like all tools, VS isn't magic. Be aware that:

The 

probabilities

 verbalized by the model may not reflect its true internal likelihoods.

Generating multiple outputs increases 

response time and cognitive load

.

It won't fix hallucination or factual drift — it enhances 

diversity

, not 

truthfulness

.

So while VS expands creativity, 

critical evaluation

 remains key.

How to Try Verbalized Sampling Yourself

Here's a template to experiment with:

Prompt: 
Please generate [N] possible answers to the question below. 
For each, include your estimated probability of selecting it. 
Question: [insert your question here]


Try it on ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude.

Then, compare the creativity and range of answers you get — you'll see VS in action.

Conclusion

Verbalized Sampling represents a 

philosophical and practical shift

 in how we interact with large language models.

It reminds us that intelligence isn't just about precision, it's about 

possibility

.

By asking AI to show its range of thoughts, we become better collaborators, not just consumers of its output. As AI tools become increasingly central to creativity and problem-solving, 

Verbalised Sampling

 may well define the next chapter in prompt engineering.
