🧠 PART 1 — FOUNDATIONS OF PROMPTING

What is Prompting (in simple terms)

Prompting is the art of talking to an AI system in a way it truly understands.

Think of it as giving clear instructions to a hyper-intelligent assistant that always does exactly

what you say — not what you meant.

If you ask vaguely, you get vague results.

If you ask clearly, you get targeted, high-quality output.

💬 “Prompting is not about telling the AI what to do. It's about teaching it how to think

for your context.” – Jayesh Ranjan

2. How AI Actually Interprets a Prompt

When you type a prompt into ChatGPT or Claude, the model breaks it down into tokens

(small pieces of text).

It then predicts the next most probable token based on patterns learned from billions of

sentences.

➡ That means:

●

It does not think like a human; it mimics thinking through statistical prediction.

●

The AI pays more attention to the start and end of your prompt (primacy + recency

bias).

●

The AI remembers information only within its context window (e.g., 128k tokens in

Claude 3 Opus, 32k in GPT-4-Turbo).

Therefore → your prompt must be structured like a good brief:

Goal → Context → Instruction → Output Format.

3. Why Prompting Matters More Than Ever

AI is now the interface to all creation — writing, design, video, music.

Prompting is the new language of creation.

If you can prompt well, you can build anything.

If you can't, you depend on others who can.

Master prompting → you master AI itself.

4. The 4 Golden C's of Prompting

C Meaning Why it Matters

Clarity Be precise about what you want Avoids AI guesswork

Context Give background and purpose Improves relevance

Constraints Add rules – length, tone, format Gives structure

Creativity Encourage exploration Unlocks unique outputs

Example :

❌ “Write about marketing.”

✅ “You are a marketing consultant helping Indian start-ups in 2025. Write a LinkedIn post

(120 words) about how storytelling increases customer trust. Tone: conversational, include

one emoji and a CTA to comment.”

5. The R-C-A-D Formula (Universal Prompt Framework)

R – Role → Who should the AI be? (Expert, teacher, analyst)

C – Context → What's the situation, audience, and goal?

A – Action → What do you want done? (Write, analyze, design, compose)

D – Desired Format → How should it look or sound?

Example Prompt:

“You are a growth strategist (R). For a D2C fashion brand targeting Gen Z (C), write an

Instagram caption (A) under 100 characters in a witty, luxury tone (D).”

7. Prompting as Human Design Thinking

Great prompting = great briefing.

You are not “using ChatGPT”; you're designing a conversation.

Each prompt has 3 layers:

1. Intent – your goal (what you need done)

2. Instruction – the steps to achieve it

3. Interaction – how you refine and improve together

Prompting is not static; it's a dialogue.

Start from the goal, then add context, then constraints and style, then decide the format.

If any layer is missing, the output loses quality.

9. Prompt Refinement Cycle

1⃣ Write the initial prompt.

2⃣ Run it → review output.

3⃣ Identify what's off (clarity, tone, facts).

4⃣ Add constraints or clarity.

5⃣ Run again.

6⃣ Save the version that worked best.

Each iteration is training the AI for you.

PART 3 — IMAGE PROMPTING MASTERY

(Midjourney • [Link] • NanoBanana • DALL· E 3 • Higgs Field)

1. Introduction — Turning Words into Worlds

Text-to-image models are essentially dream translators.

They take your linguistic description (prompt) and turn it into a coherent visual composition

by learning what billions of words and images look like together.

When you prompt an image generator, you're doing three things:

1. Describing a scene — what exists.

2. Defining a style — how it should look.

3. Declaring intent — what emotion or message it should convey.

💬 “A good image prompt is 50% clarity, 30% imagination, and 20% technical

control.” — Jayesh Ranjan

2. How Image Models Interpret Prompts

Most visual AI models use diffusion or transformer architectures.

They de-noise random pixels until they match your textual description.

They pay special attention to:

●  Keywords at the start (sets subject & context)

●  Modifiers and style words (define artistic direction)

●  Negative prompts (what to exclude)

●  Weights or brackets (emphasis hierarchy)

So, the order and wording of your prompt dramatically change the output.

The SASLN Framework

(Subject – Action – Style – Lighting – Negative)

Element Description Example

S (Subject) Who or what is the “A wise monk”, “A luxury perfume bottle”

focus

A (Action) What the subject is “meditating under rain”, “placed on marble table”

doing

S (Style) Visual medium and “digital painting”, “photorealistic”, “cyberpunk”

tone

L (Lighting) Mood and atmosphere “golden hour”, “neon reflections”, “soft studio

light”

N Remove unwanted “– blurry, – text overlay, – watermark”

(Negative) things

✅ Full Example Prompt:

“A monk meditating under rain on a mountain cliff, cinematic atmosphere,

photorealistic 8K, soft diffused lighting, detailed raindrops, 85 mm lens —no text,

—no logo, —no extra hands.”

6. Prompt Layering Technique

Start broad → narrow down → refine composition.

Stage 1 – Base Concept:

“A portrait of a monk meditating in the forest.”

Stage 2 – Add Style + Mood:

“…digital art, cinematic lighting, fog, bokeh background.”

Stage 3 – Add Technical Control:

“…shot on 85 mm lens, depth-of-field, ultra-realistic, 8K.”

Stage 4 – Add Negatives:

“…– cartoonish, – extra limbs, – watermark.”

Stage 5 – Refine:

“Version 2 with closer framing and darker tone.”

Each layer moves the output closer to your mental image.

8. Negative Prompt Crafting (Your Hidden Weapon)

Negative prompts refine precision.

Without them, you'll often get distorted faces, extra fingers, or text overlays.

🧩 Best-practice negatives:

– lowres, – blurry, – text, – watermark, – deformed, – duplicate, –

grainy, – bad anatomy, – extra limbs, – cropped, – out of frame

✅ Add brand-specific negatives:

For product shots: – people, – clutter, – background objects

For portraits: – harsh shadows, – open mouth, – exaggerated eyes

PART 6 — ADVANCED PROMPTING STRATEGIES

Multi-Step Logic • Meta-Prompting • Prompt Chaining • Ethical Design

1. 🧭 Why Advanced Prompting Matters

Basic prompting gets you answers.

Advanced prompting gets you control.

It's how top creators:

●  Link several models into one pipeline.

●  Force the AI to reason step-by-step.

●  Build reusable templates and assistants.

●  Achieve consistency across outputs and platforms.

💬 “Beginners ask AI for results. Pros teach AI how to think.” — Jayesh Ranjan

2. 🌳 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning

Ask the model to show its reasoning before the final output.

Prompt Pattern:

“Think step-by-step. Explain your reasoning first, then produce the final answer.”

Example:

“List five ad-creative ideas.

Step 1: Brainstorm 10 hooks.

Step 2: Choose the best 3 by engagement potential.

Step 3: Write an ad copy for each.”

✅ Result → clearer logic, fewer hallucinations.

Use CoT when tasks involve analysis, planning, decision trees.

🔗 Prompt Chaining (Multi-Step Workflows)

Break complex goals into mini-prompts that feed each other.

Example Workflow:

1⃣ Prompt 1 – Research: “List top 5 AI video tools in 2025.”

2⃣ Prompt 2 – Summarize: “Summarize the features of each in a table.”

3⃣ Prompt 3 – Create: “Write a LinkedIn post comparing the top two tools.”

This method keeps clarity, prevents overload, and lets you review at each stage.

🔥 Pro Hack – Use different models for each stage:

Gemini for research → Claude for long writing → ChatGPT for tone polish.

4. 🧠 Tree-of-Thought (TOT) Prompting

Ask the model to explore multiple possible paths before choosing one.

Prompt Example:

“List 3 different strategies to launch a course.

Evaluate each for pros and cons, then recommend the best one.”

This simulates brainstorm + decision logic → perfect for strategy, marketing, and planning.

5. 🤖 Meta-Prompting (Prompt About Prompts)

Tell AI to improve its own prompt.

Example:

“Here is my prompt: 'Write an email about AI.' Analyse it for clarity, context, and

constraints.

Rewrite it for better results.”

Then ask: “Now run the improved version.”

Meta-prompting is how you train AI to coach you back.

⚗ Role Stacking & Prompt Personas

Combine multiple expert identities in one prompt.

Example:

“You are a marketing psychologist + copywriter + designer.

Step 1: Brainstorm concept using consumer psychology.

Step 2: Write the headline.

Step 3: Design prompt for Midjourney matching the headline.”

🧠 Each “role” adds domain knowledge → output becomes multi-dimensional.
