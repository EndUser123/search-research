Abstract

Report issue for preceding element

Prompt injection attacks pose a significant challenge to the safe deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) in real-world applications. While prompt-based detection offers a lightweight and interpretable defense strategy, its effectiveness has been hindered by the need for manual prompt engineering. To address this issue, we propose AEGIS , an A utomated co- E volutionary framework for G uarding prompt I njections S chema. Both attack and defense prompts are iteratively optimized against each other using a gradient-like natural language prompt optimization technique. This framework enables both attackers and defenders to autonomously evolve via a Textual Gradient Optimization (TGO) module, leveraging feedback from an LLM-guided evaluation loop. We evaluate our system on a real-world assignment grading dataset of prompt injection attacks and demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing baselines, achieving superior robustness in both attack success and detection. Specifically, the attack success rate (ASR) reaches 1.0, representing an improvement of 0.26 over the baseline. For detection, the true positive rate (TPR) improves by 0.23 compared to the previous best work, reaching 0.84, and the true negative rate (TNR) remains comparable at 0.89. Ablation studies confirm the importance of co-evolution, gradient buffering, and multi-objective optimization. We also confirm that this framework is effective in different LLMs. Our results highlight the promise of adversarial training as a scalable and effective approach for guarding prompt injections.

Introduction

Report issue for preceding element

 

Figure 1: Overview of adversarial co-evolution framework to systematically explore defenses against prompt injection attacks. Report issue for preceding element

Large Language Models (LLMs) have rapidly become integral components of modern AI systems, powering a wide range of downstream applications such as education, healthcare, and software engineering. However, the deployment of LLMs in real-world settings also exposes them to security risks, most notably prompt injection attacks, where maliciously crafted inputs manipulate the model into producing unintended or harmful outputs. Unlike traditional adversarial examples, prompt injections exploit the semantic and contextual flexibility of natural language, making them particularly challenging to detect and defend against.

Report issue for preceding element

Existing defense mechanisms largely fall into two categories: training-based approaches that require additional fine-tuning of LLMs, and training-free approaches that rely on manually designed prompts, templates, or heuristics. While the latter are attractive for their efficiency and compatibility with black-box LLMs, they often suffer from limited robustness and adaptability because of their dependence on fixed, human-engineered designs. Recent work in prompt optimization has demonstrated the potential of systematic search strategies to improve prompts for performance, generalization, or safety. Yet, most optimization frameworks assume static objectives, leaving open the question of how to adapt defenses in adversarial and evolving environments such as prompt injections.

Report issue for preceding element

In this work, we introduce AEGIS , a novel adversarial co-evolution framework for automated discovery of robust defenses against prompt injection attacks. Our framework jointly evolves attack and defense prompts in an iterative, GAN-inspired process, where attackers continuously refine adversarial strategies and defenders adapt in response. At the core of our method is TGO+, an enhanced textual gradient optimization module that simulates gradient-like updates using natural language feedback. By leveraging multi-route optimization signals, a gradient buffer, and adversarial co-training, AEGIS autonomously explores the space of defensive strategies without requiring model fine-tuning or human-crafted rules.

Report issue for preceding element

We evaluate our framework in the context of automated assignment grading, a realistic scenario where malicious prompts can manipulate grading outcomes. Across multiple LLMs, AEGIS consistently improves both attack and defense prompts over successive iterations, achieving state-of-the-art robustness against real-world injection attempts while preserving utility on benign inputs. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the critical role of co-evolution and gradient replay in sustaining long-term robustness.

Related Works

Report issue for preceding element

Under the scenario of applying LLMs as black-box components in downstream applications, our work focuses on developing an automatic framework for discovering reliable defenses against prompt injection attacks—without relying on any additional training resources or model fine-tuning.

Interactive Objective Prompt Optimization.

Report issue for preceding element

This category includes works that adapt to evolving task demands or adversarial settings. It is suited for evolving task environments, where objectives or threats may evolve over time. Despite its practical relevance, this direction has received relatively limited attention, likely due to the complexity of modeling dynamic behaviors and adversaries. A key real-world motivation for this setting is developing prompt-based defenses against malicious user behavior in LLM applications, such as prompt injection or jailbreak attacks. These scenarios demand optimization frameworks capable of anticipating and resisting adaptive adversarial strategies.

Report issue for preceding element

Robust Prompt Optimization (RPO), proposed by (Zhou, Li, and Wang 

2024

https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00088v1#bib.bib12

) , is closely related to our work and shares a similar optimization objective. However, beyond its white-box setting, RPO and our approach also diverge in more fundamental ways—particularly in the underlying threat models dictated by the targeted scenarios. Specifically, RPO is designed to address jail-breaking attacks, whereas our work focuses on prompt injection attacks. This distinction leads to important differences in the problem formulation, as outlined below.

Report issue for preceding element

• Attacker's perspective: Unlike in the jailbreaking scenario, prompt injection attacks typically occur in application settings where the attacker is an end-user and thus subject to strict query limits imposed by the application owner. These constraints make it impractical to use gradient-based optimization or other approaches that require a large number of model queries. As a result, attackers often rely on semantic search strategies, reusing or adapting known effective attack prompts rather than optimizing them through extensive trial and error. Report issue for preceding element

• Defender's perspective: In contrast to jailbreak detection, where the model's misbehavior is usually explicit and easier to flag, prompt injection defenses are more nuanced. Here, the defender often has more control over the prompt construction pipeline, including both the user input and the system instructions, before sending the full prompt to the LLM. However, despite the stronger control, detecting prompt injections can be far more ambiguous. For example, in a grading application where an LLM scores both a high-quality and a low-quality article with 10/10, an attack may only be present in the low-quality article if it includes hidden or indirect instructions influencing the grading. Report issue for preceding element

These differences in attacker capabilities and defense challenges result in fundamentally different threat assumptions and motivate distinct architectural and algorithmic choices in the design of our framework compared to RPO.

Method

Report issue for preceding element

We propose a general adversarial co-evolution framework that enables both attackers and defenders to evolve automatically through iterative optimization. While our method can be applied to various scenarios, we demonstrate its effectiveness in the context of automated assignment grading. In this setting, an attacker attempts to obtain high scores by injecting adversarial prompts, and the defender aims to prevent misgrading. By leveraging LLM-guided feedback and prompt refinement, our system continuously improves both attack and defense strategies without human intervention.

Attacker.

Report issue for preceding element

The attacker module aims to generate adversarial prompts that can mislead the system to give a higher score. Within the co-evolutionary framework, the attacker evolves in alternating turns against a fixed defender, simulating an arms race between offensive and defensive strategies.

Report issue for preceding element

During each attacker iteration, a training phase is initiated to explore new adversarial candidates. Specifically, the attacker maintains a top- k k pool of the strongest attack prompts from previous cycles, as measured by their effectiveness against the current best defense. New attack candidates are generated using the T  G  O + TGO^{+} module, which synthesizes gradient-like signals derived from grading feedback and guides the editing of existing prompts to enhance their adversarial strength.

By leveraging LLM-based editing, gradient-guided refinement, and evaluation signals from the grading system, the attacker adaptively explores the adversarial prompt space, driving the co-evolutionary process forward.

Defender.

Report issue for preceding element

The defender module aims to develop prompts that are robust against adversarial attacks and capable of eliciting accurate system responses. Unlike the attacker, which seeks to exploit model weaknesses, the defender focuses on maintaining reliability under adversarial pressure.

Report issue for preceding element

Following the co-evolutionary setup, the defender evolves in response to a fixed attacker. After each attacker cycle, the best-performing attack prompt is used as the evaluation context against which the defender is trained. The evolution process mirrors that of the attacker: a top- k k pool of defense prompts is maintained and updated via the T  G  O + TGO^{+} module, which refines existing prompts using gradient-like feedback from the grading system.

Report issue for preceding element

While the underlying mechanics—generation, evaluation, and selection—are symmetric to the attacker's process, the defender faces a different optimization goal. Rather than maximizing disruption, the defender is trained to neutralize the attack while preserving the semantic intent and correctness of responses. This often requires precise prompt calibration and semantic grounding, especially in high-stakes settings.

Report issue for preceding element

Ultimately, the defender provides a moving target for the attacker, contributing to the dynamic equilibrium of the co-evolutionary training process.

TGO+ Framework for Prompt Optimization

Report issue for preceding element

[do we mention algorithm and fig at the same time?] Inspired by Microsoft's TGO framework ( tgo2024) , we adopt a modular design to optimize prompts through gradient-like updates in natural language space. Each TGO+ module is dedicated to a specific optimization goal (e.g., attack or defense) and operates in two stages: gradient acquisition and gradient application, as illustrated in Figure 

3

https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00088v1#Sx3.F3

.

Report issue for preceding element

Gradient Acquisition.

Report issue for preceding element

The module first samples candidate prompts and submits them to the grading system to obtain scores, and the grading system calculate score as Algorithm 

1

https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00088v1#alg1

. Based on the results, an LLM is prompted with a task-specific instruction and the observed error. The LLM returns a feedback message that serves as a natural language proxy for a gradient direction—i.e., a suggestion indicating how the prompt could be improved.

Report issue for preceding element

To support iterative learning, recent feedback messages are stored in a gradient buffer. This buffer helps stabilize and diversify the optimization trajectory.

Report issue for preceding element

Gradient Application.

Report issue for preceding element

In the second stage, the buffered feedback is synthesized into a set of guidance messages. These are used to construct an edit prompt that instructs the LLM to revise the original candidate prompt accordingly. The improved prompt is then evaluated and, if effective, retained for further optimization cycles.

Report issue for preceding element

TGO+ enables gradient-like prompt updates without requiring access to model internals, making it compatible with black-box LLMs such as GPT-4o.

Key Innovations.

Report issue for preceding element

Our implementation of TGO+ introduces several key innovations that differentiate it from the original work:

Report issue for preceding element

• Multi-Route Gradient Optimization: To enhance the optimization process, we employ a multi-route gradient strategy. This means that for each prompt, we generate textual gradients based on multiple performance metrics. For instance, the Attackerś prompts are optimized based on both ASR and the induced score change. Similarly, the Defenderś prompts are optimized based on both TPR and TNR. This allows for a more holistic and effective optimization process. Report issue for preceding element

• Gradient Buffer: We introduce a gradient buffer that stores past textual gradients. This prevents the model from repeatedly making the same mistakes and encourages the exploration of novel optimization pathways. Report issue for preceding element

To understand the contribution of each component in our proposed PIA-GAN framework, we conducted several ablation studies. These studies involve systematically removing or altering parts of our system and observing the impact on performance.

Report issue for preceding element

Without Gradient Buffer

Report issue for preceding element

In this study, we removed the gradient buffer, which stores historical textual gradients for the optimization prompt. This change significantly degrades performance. Without access to past gradients, the optimization process becomes less stable and struggles to build upon previous iterations, leading to a slower convergence rate and a final performance that is approximately 15-20% worse than the full model.

Report issue for preceding element

Without Multiple Gradients

Report issue for preceding element

Here, we simplified the gradient generation process to use only a single performance metric. For the defender, we used only the True Positive Rate (TPR), and for the attacker, only the Attack Success Rate (ASR). This led to a skewed optimization. The defender becomes overly aggressive, achieving a high TPR at the cost of a poor TNR (many false positives). The attacker focuses narrowly on a single attack vector, resulting in a lower ASR when faced with a robust defense.

Report issue for preceding element

Single-Sided Training

Report issue for preceding element

We also experimented with training only one side of the GAN framework. The lack of an adaptive adversary meant that the trained model quickly overfit to its static opponent, highlighting the importance of co-evolution.

Report issue for preceding element

• Defender-Only Training: When training the defender against a static set of attacks, it achieves near-perfect performance on those attacks but fails to generalize to new, unseen attacks, resulting in a lower overall TPR. Report issue for preceding element

• Attacker-Only Training: Similarly, when the attacker is trained against a static defender, it achieves a very high success rate against that specific defense but cannot adapt to a more robust, dynamic one. Report issue for preceding element

Conclusion

Report issue for preceding element

We presented AEGIS , a novel automated co-evolutionary framework for guarding against prompt injection attacks in Large Language Models (LLMs). By iteratively optimizing both attack and defense prompts using a gradient-based natural language strategy, AEGIS systematically explores the prompt space without manual engineering. Our approach demonstrates superior performance over baseline and hand-crafted prompts on several LLMs, achieving promising results in both attack strength and defense robustness.

Report issue for preceding element

Through extensive experiments and ablation studies, we confirmed the importance of co-evolution, gradient replay, and multi-objective optimization. These findings suggest that adversarial training, when applied at the prompt level, offers a scalable and effective solution for safeguarding LLMs in real-world deployments.

Report issue for preceding element

In future work, we plan to extend AEGIS to more complex scenarios, improve prompt interpretability, and explore its applicability to other adversarial tasks in NLP and LLM alignment.
