# Why the “Great Feminisation” Thesis Misdiagnoses the Problem
let's delve into one of the spiciest topics in the culture war right now the great feminization idea so just recently Helen Andrews wrote an essay for Compact Magazine outlining the great feminization thesis the essay exploded across conservative circles provoking everything from agreement to of course accusations of misogyny we published a response to her essay Inquillette by Gideon Scopes i'm going to outline a bit of what he says but first what exactly does Helen Andrews argue so Andrews is a post-liberal commentator who's published an essay in Compact Magazine claiming that wokeness cancel culture speech suppression the prioritization of emotional safety over open debate isn't really about progressive ideology she argues it's the inevitable consequence of women gaining more power in public life and Quillette has already published a fascinating essay on this i've made it into a video it's one of our most popular videos it was written by Bo Weard and Corey Clark and it's called Sex in the Academy and if you've watched the video or read the essay you'd know that it's all about how women entering academia and these academic institutions has really impacted the culture of these institutions and as they argue has resulted in as I've just said a lack of free speech more cancel culture and a prioritization of emotional safety over the truth so Helen Andrews makes a similar argument in her essay she argues that as women have become the majority in institutions like universities law schools HR departments and media organizations these institutions have fundamentally changed the traits she associates with men direct conflict risk-taking valuing truth over feelings have been replaced by what she calls feminine norms consensus building empathy emotional safety and conflict avoidance and the data does show something interesting here a 2022 poll of college students found that 85% of women wouldn't allow a speaker who said transgender people have a mental disorder to speak on campus compared to only 58% of men similar gaps showed up for controversial speakers on topics like Black Lives Matter election fraud and abortion andrews isn't just saying women are different she's saying that when women reach a critical mass in an organization the entire culture flips men leave the incentives change and the institution stops functioning the way it was designed to quitlette has published a response to Helen Andrews's essay by Gideon Scopes in which he doesn't dismiss Andrew's observations outright instead he acknowledges that there are real sex differences in how men and women approach certain questions particularly around free speech and controversial topics but and this is crucial he argues that Andrews is wrong to call this shift inevitable just because a trait is more common in one demographic group doesn't mean increasing that group's representation will automatically transform an organization think about it this way for a demographic shift to inevitably change an institution three things need to be true first the new people joining need to be representative of their demographic group but are the women entering elite institutions actually typical of women in general or are they being selected for specific traits that make them more likely to embrace progressive ideology interesting point second the trait needs to be genuinely common in that demographic but what if the supposed feminine traits Andrew has described aren't actually that common among women broadly what if they're concentrated in specific subgroups third and this is the big one the trait can't be amplified or diminished by social pressure but what if the culture itself is creating these differences what if the illiberal attitudes we're seeing aren't intrinsic to being female but are being taught and reinforced by the ideological environment of these institutions scopes points out that the sex gap on these free speech questions isn't universal it's strongest among young college educated liberals among older adults and conservatives the gap largely disappears so maybe this isn't about women at all maybe it's about ideology about what's being taught in universities and about selection effects yes women show higher rates of supporting speech restrictions but young men in these same environments are also becoming more illiberal just at slightly lower rates if the real issue were biological or essential differences between men and women we wouldn't see these dramatic changes across age cohorts and political affiliations scopes argues that what we're really seeing is a failure of liberal values across the board a failure to teach and defend the principles of open debate intellectual diversity and viewpoint difference both young men and young women are being steeped in an ideology that prioritizes emotional safety and group identity over classical liberal values the solution isn't to reduce women's participation in public life that's both morally wrong and practically impossible the solution is to directly address the liberal ideology itself and here's something that Andrea's critics have pointed out repeatedly if we look at history periods of complete male dominance weren't exactly bastions of free speech and open inquiry the Battle of the S wasn't a triumph of masculine rationality the Salem witch trials weren't run by HR departments mccarthyism wasn't feminine cancel culture the idea that men naturally create more open truth-seeking institutions while women naturally create oppressive ones just doesn't hold up to historical scrutiny so if reducing women's powers isn't the answer what is well Scopes argues that we need to act quickly to reverse illiberal trends among both young women and young men and here are some key points first we need to recognize that this is an ideological problem not a demographic one the issue isn't having more women in positions of power it's having people of any gender who don't value free speech open debate and intellectual diversity second we need to reform how universities and other institutions approach controversial topics this means promoting genuine viewpoint diversity protecting disscent and teaching students male and female how to engage with ideas they find offensive or disturbing third we need to be honest about where the real threats to liberal values are coming from they're not coming from women specifically they're coming from a particular ideological framework that's captured many of our institutions the good news if these trends are driven more by ideology and environment than by biology they can be changed we're not doomed to choose between women's participation in public life and a free open society but we do need to be realistic this isn't going to fix itself it requires a deliberate effort to defend and promote liberal values across all institutions so Helen Andrews raised some uncomfortable questions about changing institutional cultures and divergent attitudes towards speech and debate between men and women these observations deserve serious engagement as we've done at Quilllet but Scopes's response makes a compelling case that the great feminization thesis misidentifies both a problem and the solution the threat to liberal institutions isn't women it's illiberalism itself and it's affecting both sexes so what do you
