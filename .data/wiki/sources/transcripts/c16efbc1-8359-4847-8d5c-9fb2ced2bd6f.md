The 

max_concurrent_predictions

 fix 

@mingyi456

https://github.com/mingyi456

 found makes sense — Gemma 4's hybrid attention architecture has a structural memory issue that multiplies with concurrent prediction slots.

Here's why: the sliding-window attention layers (50 of 62 layers in the 31B model) maintain a 

constant

 SWA KV cache per prediction slot — it doesn't grow with sequence length but it also doesn't shrink. With 

max_concurrent_predictions=4

 (default), you're allocating 4 copies of that constant SWA cache upfront. For Gemma 4 31B, the SWA cache per slot at 1024-token window is:

50 layers × 1024 tokens × 2 (K+V) × 16 KV heads × 256 head_dim × 2 bytes ≈ 3.3 GB per slot


At 4 slots: ~13 GB just for the constant SWA caches before any actual context. That's the "unholy" baseline you're seeing.

Setting 

max_concurrent_predictions=1

 drops that to ~3.3 GB, which explains the recovery. For single-user desktop use, 

max_concurrent_predictions=1

 is the right setting anyway — you're not load-balancing across parallel speculative decoding chains.

The 

q4_0

 KV quantization option (if exposed in LM Studio's settings) would also help for the 10 full-attention layers which do scale with context — cutting them from BF16 to 4-bit takes them from ~4.8 GB at 8K context to ~1.2 GB. Combined with 

max_concurrent_predictions=1

 you should be able to hit 16K+ context comfortably on 24 GB.

Fair point, my math was sloppy. Here's the corrected version:

q4_0 is about 4.5 bits per value (not 4.0), because each block of 32 values has a FP16 scale and min on top of the 4-bit nibbles. So the actual savings from FP16 is ~70% not 75%. For the 10 full-attention layers at 8K context that works out to about 1.4 GB instead of the 1.2 GB I said. Still a big drop from 4.8 GB, but I should have been more careful.

On whether KV quantization makes sense compared to just quantizing weights further: they're actually quite different beasts. KV values are activations that change every inference, but adjacent tokens produce highly correlated KV vectors. You can exploit that temporal redundancy with predictive coding, which just doesn't exist in weight matrices. In practice we see +0.4-0.9% PPL at 2-3 bit KV on Mistral-7B. Doing the equivalent on weights (going from Q4 to IQ1_S) would completely destroy the model. It's not really a fair comparison.

For Gemma 4 specifically, the hybrid architecture is actually perfect for selective compression. You only need to compress the 10 full-attention layers that scale with context. The 50 SWA layers have fixed cost regardless. That's a much better strategy than uniformly quantizing everything.

Either way, your 

max_concurrent_predictions=1

 fix is still the single biggest win for Gemma 4 on consumer hardware. The KV quant is icing on top.
