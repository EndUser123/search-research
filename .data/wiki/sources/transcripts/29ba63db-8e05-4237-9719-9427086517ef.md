# the neuroscience trick that makes any home feel 10x better
have you ever looked at a beautifully designed room and thought "Why does this look so good?" Obviously we all have different tastes but there's also plenty of science and psychology behind what makes certain rooms so timeless and aesthetically pleasing so in this video I'm going to show you how to harness that psychology to upgrade your own home without renovating or even buying new furniture so have you ever walked into a room that has all the right furniture the trendy sofa the marble coffee table and the expensive rug but it still feels a little flat like you're looking at a two-dimensional image instead of standing in a three-dimensional space well this is one of the most common mistakes people make with interior design and it usually happens because you've fallen into the single plane trap in most amateur rooms everything lives on one single horizontal band that starts at the floor and ends around 2 and 1/2 ft up so your sofa your coffee table your media console and your bed all occupy that same narrow slice of air so when your eye scans a room like that it has no reason to travel upward or into the corners your brain basically collapses the whole space into a flat static image and this creates what designers call the dollhouse effect which is a space where scale feels distorted and nothing feels substantial or anchored and so to fix this you have to reclaim the vertical plane so think of your space like a 3D puzzle you need to distribute the visual weight so your eyes have a journey to go on you want to lead the eye from the floor all the way to the ceiling because in interior design a room is defined by how the eye moves through it and so in a flat room the eye scans strictly horizontally and hits a dead end as soon as it reaches a wall and this horizontal scanning makes the ceilings feel lower and the walls feel closer which effectively shrinks the perceived volume of the room but adding in some vertical rhythm disrupts this and by placing visual interest at varying altitudes low medium and high you force the viewer's gaze to travel in a W or M pattern zigzagging up and down and this vertical movement engages the peripheral vision and signals to the brain that the space is expansive and dynamic rather than compressed and static but let me give you a few specific ways to do this so you know exactly what I'm talking about one of the easiest cheats for this is the curtain height hack most people mount their curtain rods right on top of the window frame but you don't want to do that instead mount the rod 2 to 4 in below the ceiling line this forces the eye to travel the entire height of the wall and it makes the room feel instantly taller and more expensive and this works by exploiting a visual ambiguity when the curtain rod sits directly on the window frame you're clearly defining the upper limit of the window and you're showing the brain exactly where the architecture stops but when you raise the rod and let the fabric drape over the wall space above the window you obscure that boundary and so the brain which loves to complete patterns assumes that the window opening extends much higher behind the fabric than it actually does and so you're essentially manufacturing a phantom architectural feature and tricking the eye into perceiving a grander floor to ceiling aperture where one doesn't exist now another pro move is using vertical lines like fluted wall panels or tall slim bookcases because these human figure vertical architectural elements counteract the visual gravity in a room when the eye latches on to a vertical line whether it's the floating on a cabinet or the spine of a tall bookshelf horizontal datim lines created by sofas and tables but that's only how you create spaciousness on the vertical plane but you can also create it on the horizontal plane as well by creating depth in your room and one way to do that is to stop bleeding your furniture into your walls because when a sofa's pushed against the wall it loses its mass and starts looking like part of the architecture instead you want to do what pro designers do which is float their furniture because even moving a couch 6 in away from a wall creates a gap that hints at hidden depth and by doing this you're telling
