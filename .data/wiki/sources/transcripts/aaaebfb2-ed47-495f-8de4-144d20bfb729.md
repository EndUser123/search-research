# Why I'm Switching Back to Paper Filters After This Study
my coffee might be impacting my blood cholesterol and in particular my LDLC and it all depends on how it's made Let's have a look at why this is Coffee is the world's most popular stimulant but how you brew it drastically changes what ends up in your cup and your bloodstream Inside coffee beans are natural lipid compounds called durpins specifically caffestol and kawo While both are extracted by hot water caffestol is the primary compound affecting clinical health markers To establish the long-term impact of these compounds researchers analyze data from a massive 20ear study of over 500,000 participants in Norway The cohort baseline data focused on healthy individuals tracked over the two decades to observe cardiovascular outcomes For transparency the study was independently funded by public health grants and the authors declared no competing commercial interests When the scientists analyzed the long-term mortality data the statistics revealed a clear trend regarding brewing methods Drinking unfiltered coffee is associated with higher cardiovascular mortality particularly in men aged 60 and older Conversely drinking filtered coffee is linked to lower mortality than not drinking coffee at all The primary mechanism driving this risk is a predictable rise in total cholesterol specifically lowdensity lipoprotein or LDL cholesterol as well as triglycerides The data from the paper reports that the difference in the total cholesterol levels between those consuming un unfiltered coffee and those drinking coffee is approximately 0.49 mill moles per liter which translates to 18.95 mg per deciliter These compounds also cause a mild rise in liver enzymes specifically alanine amino transferase or ALT indicating that they actively alter the liver cells processing of cholesterol As promising as the long-term data looks for filtered coffee we have to look closely at the limitations of this study design The research relies heavily on observational epidemiological data and self-reported dietary questionnaires which introduce margins of error regarding exact daily consumption over 20 years Additionally while the correlation between unfiltered coffee and cardiovascular mortality is statistically significant individual metabolic responses to cafeestol vary based on genetic baselines To understand how to manage this risk we must look at how different brewing tools interact with these lipids Paper filters function as a near perfect trap for coffee oils Drip coffee or pourovers that use paper filters contain negligible amounts of caffistol making them neutral in their impact on blood lipids If you drink instant soluble coffee the processing methods leave the final product almost entirely devoid of caffistol and cowol meaning it also has no impact on blood lipids Methods that utilize metal meshes or no filters at allow the oils to pass directly into the final brew A French press or a Scandinavian boiled coffee contain up to 30 times more lipid raising durppins than paper filtered coffee Espresso represents a unique technical paradox as it packs very high concentrations of caffistol per ounce However because traditional espresso is served in small 25 to 50 ml shots the absolute dose of durpins per serving remains moderate meaning a few cups per day represent a low relative exposure An Americano carries the exact same moderate chevl dose as the espresso shot used to create it as diluting the beverage with water does not remove the underlying oils Pod and capsule machines generally behave similar to espresso methods and contain these lipid raising substances Removing these oils alters the volatile organic compounds that reach the cup creating a direct tradeoff between health markers and sensory profiles Paper filters extract higher percentages of flowery and caramel related compounds Pourover highlights the acidity of the beans but because the paper traps the oils responsible for roundness the resulting cup exhibits less body With the floral and spicy notes comes sometimes a thinner mouth feel Metal filters and French press methods allow the oils and micrograms to pass through creating a chemical profile rich in multi fruity nutty and roasted chocolate aromomas These methods yield a sweeter cup with significantly more body a smoother fuller acidity The shape of the filter also impacts extraction as conical filters alter the flow rate differently than flat bottomed or cylindrical filters further shifting the aromatic profile The data suggests a clear association between your choice of brewing equipment and your cardiovascular lipid profile For individuals managing high cholesterol or looking to optimize long-term cardiovascular health switching to paper filtered coffee or instant coffee represents a practical evidence-based intervention This adjustment trades some body and sweetness for a highly acidic cup and improved lipid markers I've been using a metal filter for a few years now
