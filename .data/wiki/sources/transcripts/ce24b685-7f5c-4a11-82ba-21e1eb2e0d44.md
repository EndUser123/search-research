The obvious answer was the incorrect one

The usual calculations for VRAM usage don't apply to Qwen 3.6

✕ Remove Ads

https://www.xda-developers.com/premium/?ref=ad_removal

On paper, the RTX 5090 is a dream local inference beast. 32GB of GDDR7 memory, with plenty of bandwidth to supply the CUDA cores with data. LM Studio says Qwen 3.6 27B at Q4 quant is well within the capabilities, and it works well when it's working. But over time, answers drift, token generation slows, and it gets worse even when I'm not actively using chat.

The first gut reaction is to blame the model, but it hasn't changed since it's pretrained, and you're not training it while running it locally. Or that the days of uptime have degraded it, but that's also incorrect. But the 

context length

, which LM Studio cranked to the maximum of 262,144 tokens, did all the damage.

Obvious things aren't actually obvious

Here's the thing: even the napkin math while diagnosing these issues was wrong. The KV cache at 256K context should work out to 64GB, 

twice the VRAM

 of the RTX 5090. Job done, this model is no good. Except Qwen 3.6 27B isn't a standard transformer; it's built on a hybrid architecture where only 16 of the 64 layers use full attention. The other layers don't scale with context, giving you 16GB of total VRAM usage at the full 256K context.

One thing makes your local LLM feel "dumber"

Once your context window fills up, your conversation loses history

It's important to understand what's happening under the hood while you talk to a local LLM. Every turn of a conversation feeds the history back into the model to glean context and surface new insights. That means more tokens per turn, and more instructions for it to read before it can use tokens to reply.

This then hits a quirk in how transformer models work. They are measurably worse at recalling information in the middle of a long context window, preferring to read the start and the end and make do with that. When you talk to your local LLM long enough, it starts dropping the oldest instructions and forgets your initial prompt.

✕ Remove Ads

https://www.xda-developers.com/premium/?ref=ad_removal

The setting you thought was buying you smarts, the 256K context window, starts turning into the Achilles heel of the whole thing. Add in that Qwen 3.6 is a reasoning model, with a hidden think trace, and you burn up that context window quickly and degrade your experience, not the model.

The model isn't changing; the surrounding scaffold is falling apart

We should clear one thing up. The model is not changing one bit. The weights are frozen at inference; it's not learning more, it can't pick up bad habits, and no, you're not making it worse. Every other piece of the puzzle around that model is fair game, though, and context, memory pressure, and thermals are all contributing to making the LLM perform at suboptimal levels.

For the record, frontier cloud LLMs also have the same constraints. Long inputs and attention issues cause the best LLMs to lose the conversational thread over time, and it's a constant battle for companies to find fixes that don't break the tooling.

The slowdowns are due to another setting

KV cache is one to watch out for

The more I learn about LLMs, the more I realize I don't know anything, and the sheer number of settings needed to get them performing well is one thing I didn't know. KV cache is essential, but it's predetermined by the context length you set, regardless of whether you use the entire context window.

That hidden memory cost can be enough to make your VRAM spill over. Interestingly, the fact I'm using an Nvidia GPU on Windows is part of the problem, because the driver doesn't fail when the VRAM gets full. Instead, it silently offloads the extra into system RAM over the PCIe bus, slowing down the entire computer.

✕ Remove Ads

https://www.xda-developers.com/premium/?ref=ad_removal

A fresh chat fixes many things

Whether it's a long single chat or smaller chats over the course of a week, the VRAM headroom starts to disappear. When gone, it spills over into system memory again, and you hit the slowdown quicker than you used to. Opening a new chat, reloading the model, or even restarting LM Studio fixes things again, telling me that the cache was full and not being cleared.

Yes, as with many computing issues, a simple "have you turned it off and on again" is the fix. It's amusing that such physical action helps with AI, but here we are. A new chat resets the dumbness, and reloading the model fixes the slowdowns.

Your local LLM isn't actually dumber, but how you're using it is

The degradation in performance of local LLMs over time is due to multiple factors working together. Context window issues make the results less useful over time, while memory and thermal issues contribute to slowdown. LLMs are tools; they're not that mysterious. Treat them like any other computing tool and reboot them occasionally, and your experience will feel less variable.
