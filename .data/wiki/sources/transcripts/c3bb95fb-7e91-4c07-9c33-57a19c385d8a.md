# This ONE Hold Builds More Core Strength  FASTER Than Planks (No Floor Needed)
One standing hold builds more real core strength 
than most floor routines. No planks, no crunches,  

no getting up and down off the ground. I'm 
going to show you how to do it right now.  

Walk up to a wall and place both hands flat 
against it about shoulder height. You want  

your fingers pointing straight up and your arms 
fully extended. [music] Now step your feet back  

until your body is at a slight angle. The further 
you step back, the harder this is going to get.  

So start with a small step and work from there. 
Here's why this works so well. Your core's primary  

job is not to crunch you forward or twist you 
around. Its primary job is to resist movement.  

When you hold this wall plank, your entire core 
has to fight gravity pulling your body toward the  

wall. This is called anti-extension. [music] And 
it's the exact same demand a floor plank places on  

your muscles without any of the wrist pressure or 
lower back strain that comes with being face down  

on the ground. [music] We're going to hold this 
for about 30 seconds. If that feels comfortable,  

step your feet back another 6 in and 
you'll feel the difference right away.  

Keep breathing the entire time. Now, if you 
notice your lower back starting to dip or  

your hips beginning to sag, just step your feet 
forward a little bit and reset your line. The goal  

is position you can hold with control, not one 
that forces you to fight through the bad form.  

For this next one, step away from the wall. You're 
going to stand tall with your feet about hipwidth  

apart and place your hands lightly behind your 
head. From here, you're going to drive your right  

knee up and across your body toward your left 
elbow. Your upper body is going to rotate slightly  

to meet it. [music] This is working your core 
through rotation and flexation at the same time,  

which is exactly how your core actually functions 
during your everyday. [music] Each time you reach  

across your body for something, turn to look over 
your shoulder, or twist to grab something off a  

shelf, your core is rotating and stabilizing at 
the same time. This movement trains that exact  

pattern. You're going to alternate sides for 30 
seconds. Keep the [music] movement controlled and  

deliberate. You're not rushing through these. 
Each rep should feel like you're purposely  

driving the knee across while your core pulls 
everything together. If balance is a challenge,  

stand next to a wall and place one-handed 
for support. Stay standing for this last one.  

Same starting position with your hands behind 
your head and your elbows out wide. This time,  

instead of going across your body, you're going to 
go to the same side. Lift your right knee straight  

up while you bend your torso to the right. Bring 
your right elbow down toward your right knee.  

Your body is bending sideways, not twisting. 
Think of it like your torso is making the shape  

of a parenthesis. This targets your obliques, the 
muscles running along both sides of your core.  

They're responsible for keeping you stable when 
you carry something heavy on one side, [music] and  

they keep your spine aligned when you step off 
a curb or walk across uneven [music] ground.  

They do a lot important work that most people 
never train directly. And this is one of the  

most simplest ways to do it without getting on the 
floor. Alternate sides for 30 seconds. You don't  

need a huge range of motion. Even a few inches of 
lateral bend with the knee driving up creates real  

work in the obliques. [music] Keep it smooth and 
keep it controlled. If you catch yourself leaning  

forward instead of bending to the side, reset 
your posture and focus on going directly lateral,  

sideways, not forward. Now, run all three 
exercises back to back. That's about 30 seconds of  

the wall plank hold, 30 seconds of cross body knee 
to elbow, and 30 seconds of standing side crunch.  

Rest for about a minute, then repeat for 
two sets. The whole routine should take  

under 10 [music] minutes. Never touch the floor 
and you're training your core the way it actually  

designed to work, resisting movement, rotating, 
and [music] stabilizing from every angle.
