# Tower-Level Local AI From a Mini PC — 32GB VRAM via OCuLink + USB4
Can two external 16GB GPUs with combined 32GB VRAM, different connections, different manufacturers,

run local AI models like Qwen and Gemma at similar speed as in a tower PC,

or will performance drop because of competing memory buses and the lack of real PCIe slots?

I made two videos about a budget mixed GPU setup with combined 32GB of VRAM in my tower PC,

The mini PC has two USB4 ports. Important, make sure these are full-speed USB4 ports running at

40Gbps. Not all USB4 ports are equal, the minimum spec is only 20Gbps.

quiet regardless. Finally, we connect both devices with the USB4 cable. A quick note on the ASM2464

PD controller inside the UT4G and why that matters. This chip runs PCIe 4.0 on the GPU side,

not the older PCIe 3.0 you'll find in cheaper or older docks. It's also fully compatible with

Thunderbolt 3 and 4 and performs particularly well on AMD Ryzen systems with built-in USB4,

which is exactly what we have here. Worth mentioning, USB4 supports hot plug but in practice

I'd recommend using Hibernate before disconnecting. Disconnecting an active USB4 connection can cause

a blue screen. Before the benchmarks, a quick note on actual transfer rates because the two

connections are not equal. Monitors are on the integrated 700 ATM covered in the last video. OBS

is also running through the iGPU so neither eGPU's readings are affected by display or capture load.

Measured with CLpeak on the Vulkan backend. RX 6800 via USB4, host to device 3.86GB/s,

device to host 3.82GB/s. Arc A770 via Oculink, host to device 5.68GB/s, device to host 5.75GB/s.

PCIe 4.0 X4 has a theoretical ceiling of around 8GB/s per direction. After protocol overhead,

real-world measurements typically land between 5.5 and 6.5GB/s. The A770 via Oculink is right in that

range. USB4 tunnels PCIe but shares its bandwidth budget with other protocols, which is why it comes

in lower, roughly 3.8GB/s here, about 60% of what Oculink delivers. For context, even Thunderbolt 5,

the newest and most expensive option, still rare on mini PCs and laptops, achieves around 5.6GB/s

in real-world eGPU measurements. That's faster than USB4 but still slower than Oculink because

the controller overhead on both ends costs performance. Oculink remains the bandwidth

leader but USB4 gets you much closer than you might expect and at a much lower price than

Thunderbolt 5 hardware. The RX 6800 has noticeably less bandwidth to the host than the A770.
