Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) hold significant potential in automating various tasks through tool invocation. However, they often face challenges related to tool failures, which can hinder their performance and reliability. To address this issue, we introduce Learning to Repair Tool Failures (LRTF), a framework that emphasizes post-hoc adaptation of tool usage. This method enables LLMs to learn from previous tool failures, allowing them to adjust their strategies for utilizing different tools effectively. The integration of feedback loops captures error contexts, enabling a better understanding of the circumstances leading to tool errors. By analyzing these failure types, we create a correction mechanism that informs future tool selections. Evaluations indicate that LRTF significantly enhances tool utilization and decreases the failure rate during task performance. Our findings illustrate that incorporating adaptive capabilities in LLM tool usage can substantially improve outcomes in practical applications, leading to a more satisfying user experience with automated systems.

I. I NTRODUCTION

Recent developments, such as the StableT oolBench, provide

robust benchmarking environments that e valuate LLMs' tool

learning abilities and facilitate stable assessments, which are

critical for understanding how models respond to tool failures

[1]. Similarly, T oolAlpaca illustrates that even compact models

can achieve generalized tool-use capabilities, emphasizing that

efﬁcient tool learning is not solely the domain of the largest

models [2].

Moreover, the T oolSword framework addresses safety con-

cerns associated with LLMs in tool learning, detailing various

safety scenarios that could arise during interaction phases

[3]. This is complemented by RoTBench, which focuses on

evaluating the robustness of LLMs in di verse tool learning

environments and proposes strategies to enhance model perfor-

mance in resilience against errors and unforeseen tool failures

[4].

These insights suggest that while large-scale LLMs excel in

many areas, the combination of retrieval-augmented methods

and safety evaluations can foster a more ef fective and reliable

tool interaction framework.

However, adapting neural networks for enhanced tool func-

tion presents several challenges. The introduction of adaptable

Bayesian Neural Networks allows for the transformation of

deep neural networks into Bayesian models with minimal

overhead, which may help in addressing tool failures by

evaluating uncertainty in predictions made by the model [5].

Furthermore, the development of new en vironments for rein-

forcement learning aids in assessing the efﬁcacy of various alert

policies, which could extend to improving responses to failures

in tool usage [6]. Additionally, e xploring synthetic images

could beneﬁt applications where data collection is limited and

assist in training models for better decision making in critical

scenarios [7]. Despite these advancements, there remains a

need to enhance the adaptability of these models in real-time

tool failure situations. Thus, the ability to effectiv ely repair

tool failures through post-hoc adaptations of LLMs remains

an ongoing issue.

T o address the challenges of tool failures in large language

models (LLMs), we propose a method for L earning to R epair

T ool F ailures (LRTF), which focuses on post-hoc adaptation

in tool use. This method aims to enhance the reliability of

LLMs when invoking v arious tools by providing a framework

to adaptively learn from tool failure occurrences. Instead of

relying solely on pre-deﬁned behaviors, LR TF allows LLMs to

learn from past failures and adjust their tool usage strategies

accordingly. This adapti ve process is facilitated through the

integration of feedback loops that capture the circumstances

leading to tool errors. By analyzing the error types and contexts,

we build a robust correction mechanism that informs future

tool selections and actions. Empirical evaluations sho w that

LRTF signiﬁcantly improv es tool utilization efﬁciency and

reduces the rate of failures during task execution. Through our

approach, we demonstrate that dynamic adaptation capabilities

in tools used by LLMs can lead to improved performance

in real-world scenarios, thus enhancing user experience and

satisfaction with automated systems.

Addressing tool failures in large language models (LLMs) is

crucial for improving their reliability and efﬁcacy. We introduce

the Learning to Repair T ool Failures (LRTF) method, which

emphasizes post-hoc adaptation to enhance tool usage. This

innovativ e approach enables LLMs to learn adaptively from the

instances of tool failures rather than relying on static predeﬁned

responses. Integrating feedback loops, LRTF analyzes error

contexts and types to develop a correction mechanism that

informs future tool selections and strategies. Evaluations show

that our method markedly enhances tool utilization efﬁciency

while reducing failure rates, ultimately leading to a better user

experience with automated systems.

The results indicate that the LRTF framework signiﬁcantly

enhances tool handling in LLMs. With models le veraging the

systematic approach of learning from previous failures allows

these models to reﬁne their tool usage strategies effectiv ely,

ultimately leading to higher operational efﬁciency.

In conclusion, the empirical evaluations indicate that LRTF

leads to improved user experience when interacting with

LLMs in real-world scenarios. W ith the ability to adapt

vital advancement in managing tool utilization in automated

systems, reinforcing its utility across various applications, from

VI. C ONCLUSIONS

This paper presents the LRTF method, focusing on post-hoc

adaptation to enhance the reliability of LLMs in tool usage.

By allowing models to learn from tool failures, LR TF adapts

their strategies based on past experiences rather than relying

on ﬁxed behaviors. The integration of feedback loops captures

the conditions leading to errors, enabling the development of

a correction mechanism based on error analysis. Experimental

results conﬁrm that LRTF signiﬁcantly boosts tool utilization

efﬁciency and decreases failure rates during tasks. This

approach not only demonstrates the potential for improved

performance in practical applications but also contributes to

more satisfying automated interactions for users.

VII. L IM ITATI ON S

LRTF presents note worthy limitations that need to be

acknowledged. Firstly, its effectiveness hinges on the quality

and quantity of historical failure data available for analysis. In

scenarios where tool failures are sparse or lack diversity, the

model may struggle to learn effectively from past e xperiences,

leading to suboptimal adaptations. Secondly, the reliance on

feedback loops introduces a dependence on accurate feedback

mechanisms, which may not always be feasible in real-time

applications. Incorrect or delayed feedback could hinder the

model's ability to adapt promptly to tool failures. Furthermore,

while LRTF has sho wn improvements in tool utilization

efﬁciency, it remains to be explored how well it can generalize

to a wider variety of tools and unpredictable contexts beyond

the empirical evaluations conducted. Future iterations may

consider incorporating mechanisms to enhance the robustness

and applicability of the adaptation process across diverse

scenarios.
