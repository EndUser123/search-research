## Operational details

- Operational pattern 1 — UK–France £662m border deal: signed to reduce English Channel small boat crossings via 50 additional riot-trained police, drones, and helicopters on French beaches, with a conditional £100m clawback tied to a one-year performance review.
- Operational pattern 2 — Mandelson ambassadorial appointment: Number 10 politically nominated Peter Mandelson as UK Ambassador to the US, bypassing the standard diplomatic career path; UKSV was 'minded not to approve' clearance but the FCDO ultimately granted it, and the undisclosed non-Epstein factor became the trigger for accountability.
- Crisis-management trade-off: sacking the official responsible for the process (Ollie Robbins, head of the FCDO) freed him from civil-service obligations to testify before the Foreign Affairs Select Committee, costing the PM approximately three hours of Commons airtime on the Mandelson affair.
- Suspension alternative: an unnamed adviser recommended suspending Robbins pending investigation rather than immediate dismissal, in order to preserve narrative control over which voices were heard.
- Disclosure paradox: publishing the UKSV summary would simultaneously justify Starmer's outrage over the appointment and make the original appointment decision look worse — a double-edged disclosure outcome.
- Funding conditionality: the £662m UK–France deal reserves up to £100m that the UK can withdraw if France is judged to lack sufficient progress on reducing crossings, coupling payment to a measurable operational outcome.
- Public-evidence operational fact: in the £662m deal reporting, ~6,000 migrants had crossed so far that year (36% fewer than the same point the previous year), and a single day (April 18) saw 9 boats carrying 602 people.
- Interception workflow: migrants are typically picked up at sea by HM Coastguard/Border Force, fitted with orange life jackets, brought to port (e.g., Dover), disembarked via jetty, processed, and placed in holding areas rather than immediately returned to France.
- Cross-deal canonicalization: the £662m / £660m / 'have-house-deal' / 'the deal with France' / 'the France deal' all refer to the same UK–France border agreement, and 'Falkland Islands' / 'Las Malvinas' are the same territory in related geopolitical leverage discussions.
- House-fire variant (Wolverhampton, Mason Street): illustrates a fatal house-fire 'deal' context where a woman in her 30s was arrested on suspicion of 'causing or allowing the death' of two children (aged one and three); fire crews arrived within 5 minutes, six firefighters entered the building, and street closure was prolonged pending investigation.
