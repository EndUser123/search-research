## Operational details

- The screwworm was eradicated from the US Southwest in 1966 and from Mexico in 1991 using sterile male fly release programs
- A containment barrier maintained at the Darien Gap (border of Panama and Colombia) held the flies at bay until approximately 2022, when the barrier was breached
- The screwworm has now spread beyond Florida and Texas into New Mexico, with cases confirmed in Texas, a dog in New Mexico, and human risk documented
- Human infestations cause rapidly enlarging painful wounds that can progress to deep tissue with risks of secondary infection, sepsis, and mortality
- The average New World screwworm adult fly lives approximately one month
- The USDA has officially admitted they cannot stop the spread of the infestation
- Senator Roger Marshall claimed the screwworm arrived with immigrants from Central America under Biden's administration, though this claim is presented as fact-checking context in the source
- Infestations have cost the Texas economy an estimated $1.8 billion
