# Brain Fog and Neurotransmitter Dysregulation in ADHD

## Decision context

**Definition:** Brain fog in ADHD results from specific neurochemical imbalances and physiological stressors that impair cognitive function, rather than being a vague or imaginary complaint. Multiple interconnected factors including neurotransmitter deficiencies, cellular energy dysfunction, and inflammatory processes contribute to the subjective experience of mental cloudiness and reduced executive function.

## Operational details

- Dopamine deficiency represents one distinct cause of brain fog, characterized by loss of drive, focus, and motivation rather than fatigue
- Mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to brain fog that manifests primarily as exhaustion and low energy reserves
- Neuroinflammation produces a different pattern of brain fog compared to neurotransmitter-related causes, requiring different interventions
- Sleep disruption constitutes a separate category of brain fog cause, distinct from dopamine or mitochondrial-related presentations
- Low acetylcholine levels represent another identifiable subtype of brain fog with specific symptom presentations
- Brain fog is described as occurring when the brain's normal processing capacity is compromised by these underlying physiological states
- Different underlying causes of brain fog require different supplement interventions, making identification of the root cause essential
- Supplements that address one type of brain fog may be ineffective or counterproductive for another type
- The subjective experience of brain fog can include difficulty concentrating, mental fatigue, and reduced cognitive clarity
- These patterns of brain fog dysfunction are particularly relevant for individuals with ADHD, where neurotransmitter regulation is already impaired
