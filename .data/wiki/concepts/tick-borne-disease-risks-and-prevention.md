# Tick-Borne Disease Risks and Prevention

## Decision context

## Operational details

- Tick populations have reached unprecedented levels, with documented cases of individuals encountering over 50 ticks within a single hour of remaining stationary outdoors, and hundreds of tick encounters reported within a single season.
- Tick-borne illness surveillance indicates approximately 500,000 Americans are diagnosed with Lyme disease annually, making it the most commonly reported vector-borne disease in the United States.
- Emergency department visits for tick bites have exceeded normal seasonal levels, approaching figures not seen in approximately a decade according to CDC data.
- Tick habitat preferences include brush, bushes, and tall grass environments, though tick encounters increasingly occur in suburban and urban settings, contradicting the assumption that only wilderness exposure poses risk.
- The Lone star tick has expanded its range from the southeastern United States northward, introducing alpha-gal syndrome as a tick-associated health threat.
- Alpha-gal syndrome produces an allergic reaction to a carbohydrate molecule transmitted through tick bite, resulting in a severe allergy to red meat and dairy products in affected individuals.
- The Lone star tick carries a sugar molecule in its saliva that, when transmitted through bite, can trigger the development of alpha-gal syndrome in humans.
- Tick activity is not merely opportunistic; documented observations indicate ticks actively seek hosts rather than relying solely on passive transfer from vegetation.
- Environmental conditions with milder winters have contributed to expanded tick ranges and extended active seasons into northern regions.
- A thorough tick check involves visual inspection and physical examination of concealed body areas, as ticks pose greatest danger once they begin blood-feeding.
- Recommended prevention measures include wearing long pants, long-sleeved shirts, and long socks to impede tick mobility across clothing surfaces.
- Avoiding areas with brush, bushes, and tall grass reduces tick encounter probability.
- According to a tick expert, ticks occupy no ecological niche and no other species depends on them for food or reproduction, classifying them as true parasites.
