# Latent Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

## Decision context

**Definition:** Latent chain-of-thought reasoning is an approach where large language models perform reasoning steps internally without explicitly verbalizing intermediate thoughts, as opposed to standard chain-of-thought methods that require models to output reasoning traces. This technique enables decoupling of internal reasoning processes from final verbalization.

## Operational details

- Latent CoT models perform multi-step reasoning within hidden model states rather than through explicit text outputs, enabling faster inference by avoiding the overhead of generating verbose reasoning tokens
- The approach models reasoning as a causal system where latent steps influence each other through learned causal mechanisms rather than discrete token sequences
- Multiple paradigms exist for implementing latent-reasoning models, differing in how they encode and propagate intermediate states across reasoning steps
- The technique addresses the planning-verbalization decoupling problem by separating the reasoning computation from the final output generation
- Research identifies both capabilities and fundamental limits of latent reasoning approaches, examining what problems can and cannot be solved with this method
- Empirical studies analyze the causal structure within latent chain-of-thought to understand how reasoning steps interact and depend on each other
