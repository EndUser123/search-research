# Latent Reasoning in Language Models

## Decision context

**Definition:** Latent reasoning refers to the internal computational steps that AI systems perform during problem-solving, which remain unexpressed in their verbal outputs. Research in this area explores how to model, evaluate, and understand reasoning processes that occur between input and output without being explicitly articulated.

## Operational details

- Latent chain-of-thought reasoning involves intermediate computational steps that are not visible in the model's output, as distinguished from explicit chain-of-thought which verbalizes each step
- Research treats latent reasoning as a causal system where causal variables and latent-step interfaces connect reasoning components
- Different paradigms of latent-reasoning models exist, categorized by how they structure and represent internal reasoning steps
- A design approach called metacognitive co-regulation uses agentic AI loops to supervise reasoning processes in engineering contexts
- Structural information from documents (headers, tables, lists) can be preserved through markdown representations to better support AI reasoning over structured content
- ASCII art inputs present a recognition challenge that can be exploited to bypass LLM safety guardrails
- Automated bash script generation frameworks apply reasoning techniques to operational tasks, requiring assessment and refinement of generated outputs
- Benchmarks for deep research agents evaluate reasoning quality using curated prompts and evaluation rubrics
- User studies examine how system prompt architectures shape LLM behavior and user interactions
- Token-wise strategies for latent reasoning may use discrete tokens to represent reasoning steps internally
- Prompt engineering techniques are investigated for their effectiveness in enhancing human productivity when working with LLMs
- Accessibility of research documents through arXiv HTML formats supports broader dissemination and citation of reasoning research
