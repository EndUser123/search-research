# Internal ADHD Experiences and Hidden Manifestations

## Decision context

**Definition:** The internal, subjective experiences of people with ADHD that are often invisible to observers, including emotional overwhelm, social adaptation strategies, and paradoxical capability-stuck patterns that contradict external behavioral presentations.

## Operational details

- ADHD individuals experience heightened porosity, absorbing all external stimuli, emotions, and information without internal boundaries between life domains
- Children with ADHD experience approximately 20,000 additional micro-rejections compared to neurotypical peers, leading to learned self-rejection and masking behaviors
- Social mirroring functions as a Bluetooth pairing approach where ADHD individuals sync with confident people for safety, learned through years of being told they are 'too much'
- Emotional bookmarking represents a common strategy where unread messages are mentally saved without an actual system to track them
- Oversharing in new relationships stems from a learned belief that more context is needed for acceptability, rooted in childhood rejection experiences
- Time blindness manifests as starting tasks only when internal urgency screams, contributing to chronic lateness of 5-10 minutes
- The paradoxical capability-stuck pattern describes being able to accomplish anything yet unable to initiate important tasks, creating internal confusion about one's own personality
- Sleep disruption affects approximately 80% of people with ADHD, creating a bidirectional worsening cycle with ADHD symptoms
- Research suggests current ADHD diagnostic criteria capture only about one-third of actual ADHD presentations, missing two-thirds of internal manifestations
- ADHD burnout follows a trap pattern where increased productivity never creates a sense of being caught up, requiring counterintuitive rest approaches
