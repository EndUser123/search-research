# Systemic Software Fragility

## Decision context

**Definition:** The recognition that modern digital infrastructure contains fundamental architectural vulnerabilities arising from deep interconnection of components, where failures propagate unpredictably across systems despite traditional safety approaches.

## Operational details

- Software systems have become so deeply interdependent that no single human can fully understand the entire technological stack, leading to emergent failure modes that cannot be predicted through conventional testing
- The 2024 CrowdStrike incident demonstrates how a single security tool designed to protect systems instead caused cascading failures across 8.5 million Windows machines simultaneously, grounding 5,000 flights and disrupting hospitals and banking systems
- Traditional approaches like testing and post-incident reviews do not address the structural nature of this fragility, as these failures stem from accumulated interdependencies rather than individual negligence
- The reliance on millions of layered software dependencies creates what has been described as a 'tower of Babel' that amplifies rather than mitigates systemic risk
- Modern digital infrastructure exhibits entropy where accumulated technical debt and interconnection complexity compound over time, making failures more likely rather than less
