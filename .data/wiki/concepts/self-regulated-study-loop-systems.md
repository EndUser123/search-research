# Self-Regulated Study Loop Systems

## Decision context

**Definition:** Self-regulated study loop systems are iterative frameworks for learning that emphasize continuous feedback and reflection rather than linear effort accumulation. These systems draw from self-regulated learning research and incorporate periodic self-assessment to maintain optimal performance and prevent cognitive overload.

## Operational details

- Self-regulated learning operates as a cyclical loop framework rather than a linear flow, where learners continuously monitor, evaluate, and adjust their strategies based on feedback [00dbf78c-172b-4bbf-aca2-f479f193149c]
- A low-intensity high-frequency intervention approach uses repeated brief self-assessment prompts (such as twice-daily text messages asking about assignment progress) to reduce procrastination habits [043bcc51-ccdb-45c3-897a-786a909337f6]
- The Yerkes-Dodson law indicates an inverted-U relationship between effort-induced stress and performance, suggesting that excessive effort worsens learning outcomes rather than improving them [e32f8ad5-7a42-4e3d-b2d4-96d0776b0fdf]
- Hermann Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve research demonstrates that memory retention follows a predictable pattern of 66% loss within one day and 75% loss within one week without reinforcement [9f1dd0b5-9c08-487e-aaaf-41b402783400]
- Mental models and frameworks serve as thinking tools that enable rapid problem-solving by providing simplified representations of how systems work [7fe63d3c-b0ed-4b09-8a2f-6b411345313c]
