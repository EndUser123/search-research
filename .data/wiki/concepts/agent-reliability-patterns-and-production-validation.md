**Definition:** Agent reliability encompasses systematic approaches to ensuring AI agents operate correctly in production environments, including failure pattern identification, root cause analysis techniques, and observability frameworks that address the unique challenges of autonomous, multi-step agent behavior.

## Operational details

- Multi-agent systems exhibit distinct failure patterns that differ from traditional software failures, including silent failures where agents complete tasks successfully but produce incorrect outputs (Source: Galileo, Source: Maxim AI)
- The predominant root causes of agent failures stem from code-related and configuration-related issues in cloud incident scenarios (Source: PRAXIS paper)
- Production validation requires comprehensive observability across the entire agent lifecycle, including reasoning chains, tool selections, and decision paths (Source: Microsoft Azure Blog, Source: Medium article on anti-patterns)
- Architectural patterns such as ReAct, Plan-and-Execute, and multi-agent orchestration introduce specific failure modes related to state management and coordination (Source: ZIVIS, Source: Hossain)
- Debugging autonomous agents requires tracing through non-deterministic decision paths that standard monitoring tools were not designed to handle (Source: Galileo)
- Gartner research indicates over 40% of agentic AI projects face cancellation risks, with inadequate debugging infrastructure cited as a primary contributing factor (Source: Galileo)
- Multi-agent reliability depends on understanding failure patterns specific to parallel execution and inter-agent communication (Source: Maxim AI)
