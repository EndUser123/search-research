## Operational details

- A carnivore diet practitioner reports wearing a continuous ketone monitor for 14 days and observing significant ketone fluctuations they did not anticipate despite long-term carnivore adherence (Source 7)
- A practitioner who consumed 400 grams of sugar daily for 11 years underwent comprehensive metabolic blood testing to evaluate health impacts, challenging mainstream dietary narratives about sugar harm (Source 4)
- LDL cholesterol levels above 130 mg/dL are identified as a decision point for evaluating whether elevation is genetic or diet-related (Source 3)
- Saturated fat is identified as a dietary factor that influences LDL cholesterol levels, which is described as a major risk factor for heart disease and cardiovascular disease including strokes (Source 3)
- The balance of cholesterol-raising and cholesterol-lowering factors in the diet is described as more important than saturated fat alone (Source 3)
- Fructose is described as seven times more glycating than glucose, presenting a molecular challenge particularly to the liver (Source 16)
- Carnivore diet influencers who adopted the diet for years are noted to have started reintroducing carbohydrates, which the source suggests cannot be dismissed as weakness or cravings (Source 11)
- Protein intake at 30% of total nutrition while allowing ad libitum eating has been shown in studies to result in weight and body fat loss (Source 12)
- Common carnivore diet mistakes include sudden dietary transitions without a transition period, which can shock the microbiome and cause problems either immediately or months later (Source 8)
- Dr. Rhonda Patrick's characterization of carnivore as an elimination diet is addressed, with disagreement expressed about this framing (Source 9)
- Some carnivores cite inability to handle social isolation, adaptation symptoms, cravings, and online backlash as reasons for quitting (Source 11)
- Sourdough bread consumption is reported to have facilitated easier weight loss compared to previous dietary approaches in one individual's experience (Source 10)
