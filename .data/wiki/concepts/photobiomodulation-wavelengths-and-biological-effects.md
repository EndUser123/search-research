# Photobiomodulation Wavelengths and Biological Effects

## Decision context

**Definition:** Photobiomodulation refers to the use of specific wavelengths of light—particularly in the red and near-infrared spectrum—to stimulate biological processes in the body. Light in this range penetrates skin effectively and interacts with cellular components such as mitochondria, influencing energy production and signaling pathways.

## Operational details

- The electromagnetic spectrum from red through near-infrared wavelengths absorbs effectively through human skin, whereas wavelengths toward the blue end of the spectrum absorb less deeply.
- Near-infrared light penetrates tissue and interacts with cytochrome C oxidase, a mitochondrial complex involved in ATP (adenosine triphosphate) energy molecule production.
- Near-infrared light exposure can trigger release of nitric oxide, a molecule also associated with vagus nerve activation, suggesting potential synergistic effects between photobiomodulation and neural stimulation.
- A 2025 preprint study from Brazil examined combination approaches using near-infrared photobiomodulation with vagus nerve activation in 120 tinnitus patients across varying severity levels.
- Sunlight contains multiple spectrum components (UVA, UVB, infrared) and serves dual roles as both a metabolic nutrient for mitochondria and a biological messenger that influences circadian rhythms and complex physiological processes.
- Light spectrum composition, intensity, and ratios appear to influence biological effects differently than total light quantity alone.
- The relationship between sunlight and immune function is characterized as oscillating rather than simply immunosuppressive or immunostimulatory, with daytime light exposure promoting locomotor activity rather than immune comfort zone functions.
