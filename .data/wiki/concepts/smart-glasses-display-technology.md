# Smart Glasses Display Technology

## Decision context

**Definition:** Smart glasses are wearable devices that integrate visual display systems into eyeglass frames, providing hands-free access to information through embedded screens, audio, and sensors. The category spans from basic camera-equipped frames to advanced augmented reality systems with dual-display waveguide optics and AI-assisted features.

## Operational details

- Dual monochrome waveguide displays are available in models like the Even Realities G2, providing integrated display capability in both eyes at 640x350 resolution per eye
- Advanced AR glasses incorporate micro OLED display technology, with the XREAL One featuring a 68-inch virtual screen at 50-degree field of view and custom X1 processing chip
- Smart glasses priced under $100 typically offer limited functionality, with reliable 1080p camera recording being a common differentiator at the $50 price point
- AI-integrated smart glasses like MIRA can passively listen, remember conversations, and retrieve stored information on demand using built-in artificial intelligence
- The XREAL Air 4 Pro and XREAL One represent the current generation of standalone AR glasses, with the XREAL One weighing 84 grams and utilizing a custom X1 chip
- Display glasses often require additional accessories for full functionality, with some models supporting peripheral controllers like ring-based input devices
- Smart glasses can serve as extended displays for laptops, smartphones, and gaming devices through USB-C connectivity, effectively replacing traditional monitor setups in certain use cases
