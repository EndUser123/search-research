# Trading Psychology and Market Behavior

## Decision context

## Operational details

- Consistency in trading requires identifying one well-understood setup with a high winning percentage rather than trading numerous different approaches
- Psychological volatility leads traders to lose composure during small market dips of approximately 5%, especially among newer participants
- Markets exhibit a pattern of extended sideways consolidation followed by significant directional moves, observed across gold, silver, and sector ETFs
- Statistically extreme return events in the S&P 500 (such as 28-31% six-week returns) represent rare 'fat tail' occurrences that fall outside normal market behavior observed over 54 years of data
- Market indicators such as the CBOE 1M correlation indicator signal when single stock call buying becomes excessively expensive, serving as a warning for potential corrections
- IPOs have historically marked major stock market peaks, including Goldman Sachs Trading Corporation in 1928, Intel in the 1970s, and AT&T in April 2000 before the dot-com bust
