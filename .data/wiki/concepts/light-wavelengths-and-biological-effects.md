## Operational details

- The spectrum from red into infrared absorbs well through human skin, unlike blue wavelengths which do not penetrate deeply
- Near-infrared light interacts with cytochrome C oxidase, one of the complexes in mitochondria involved in ATP energy production
- Near-infrared light can trigger the release of nitric oxide, a molecule associated with vasodilation and cellular signaling
- Light functions as both a nutrient and a messenger in biological systems, triggering complex biological processes including circadian rhythm regulation
- The combination of light wavelengths, intensity, and timing affects biological outcomes differently than total quantity alone
- Sunlight contains multiple spectrum components including UVA, UVB, and infrared that may produce distinct biological effects depending on ratios and exposure conditions
- The biological effects of light involve oscillation patterns between day and night exposure rather than simple black-and-white categorization
