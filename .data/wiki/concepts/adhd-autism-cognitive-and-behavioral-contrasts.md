# ADHD-Autism Cognitive and Behavioral Contrasts

## Decision context

**Definition:** ADHD and autism represent distinct neurodevelopmental profiles with contrasting cognitive approaches: the ADHD brain tends toward free-floating, creative thought patterns, while the autistic brain prefers routine, structure, and systematic processing. These differences manifest in distinct behavioral patterns, energy management challenges, and coping strategies.

## Operational details

- The ADHD brain operates with a free-floating, creative quality, while the autistic brain exhibits sticky thinking that gravitates toward routine and structure [5]
- These conditions can mask each other, with ADHD traits potentially hiding autistic characteristics and vice versa [5]
- Autistic individuals may engage in conversation replay loops, rehearsing interactions mentally before they occur [2]
- Both autistic and ADHD individuals experience chronic sleep difficulties that compound other cognitive challenges [3][4]
- Executive functioning issues affect both populations, manifesting differently—ADHD individuals may struggle to learn from mistakes while autistic individuals may have difficulty with routine flexibility [1][3]
- Masking—the effort to appear neurotypical—contributes to constant exhaustion in autistic people [3]
- Early childhood criticism (children with ADHD receive approximately 20,000 more critical comments by age 12) damages self-esteem and creates compounding negative narratives [5]
- Some autistic individuals report difficulty understanding certain ADHD traits, such as the inability to learn from repeated mistakes [1]
