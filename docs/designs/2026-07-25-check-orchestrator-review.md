## 1. One-line summary

The architecture is sound and well-motivated, but the design has one **blocking** conflict with the existing packet-validation contract (empty `event_indices=()` is rejected by both the `Signal` dataclass docstring and `output_validator.py:228-230`), a **factual error** in the existing-test-count claim (actual is **121**, design says **101**), and several **revision** items around detector thresholds and orchestrator fast-path guarantees that should be resolved before approval.

## 4. Overall verdict

**NEEDS REVISION.**

The design is well-structured (all 10 mandatory sections present), well-motivated (the receipt-system and AGENTS.md final-verification rule citations are real), and correctly identifies the orchestrator+specialized-sub-agents pattern as the right 2026 architecture. The PR plan is sensibly ordered (PR 1 is genuinely additive — append to `DETECTOR_NAMES` and `run_all_detectors`, no existing test logic changes), and the latency budget is realistic.

Two issues block approval:

- **F-01** is a direct architectural conflict with the existing packet-validation contract. The `event_indices=()` proposal collides with two enforcement layers (dataclass docstring + validator code) and must be resolved either by relaxing the contract (recommended, with whitelist) or by choosing the rejected §6.2 alternative (top-level key). The "fail-open" language in §5.3 mischaracterizes what will actually happen — the packet will be **rejected at write time**, not silently skipped.

- **F-02** is a factual error. The 101-test claim is wrong by 20 tests. Every downstream count in the design is therefore wrong. This is mechanical to fix but must propagate through all four PRs and the phase gate numbers.

The remaining REVISE findings (F-03 through F-10) are clarifications and threshold justifications, not architectural rework. The ADVISORY findings (F-11 through F-14) are improvements that the operator can opt into during implementation.

The design is **not approved as-is**. After F-01 and F-02 are resolved and the REVISE findings are either addressed or explicitly deferred, it should be re-reviewed and likely approved without further architecture changes.
